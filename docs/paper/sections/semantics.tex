\section{Typed History-Admission Semantics}
\label{sec:semantics}

A history transformation is admitted from its typed operation and contracts,
not from a caller-proposed successor or a Boolean claim that the successor is
safe.  The semantics first derives the correlated future promised by the
operation, then checks that future against the immutable receipt prefix.  This
separates three questions that a snapshot-only rule conflates: what the
operation may do, what it must continue to support, and what remains authorized
after earlier commitments have become durable.

\subsection{Typed-operator future derivation}

A future contract $K=(M_K,R_K)$ contains a downward-closed
\emph{may} family and a downward-closed \emph{required} family over normalized
semantic redemption cells, with
$R_K\subseteq M_K$.
The cells are atomic redemption identities, not controller names or copied
workspace handles.  Define exclusive choice and joint durability by
\begin{align*}
  \mathsf{choice}(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)
    &= \mathcal F\cup\mathcal G,\\
  \mathsf{tensor}(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)
    &= \{X\cup Y\mid X\in\mathcal F,\ Y\in\mathcal G\}.
\end{align*}
Choice retains either arm without introducing their union.  Tensor permits one
configuration from each arm to become durable together.  If the same semantic
cell occurs in both operands, set union counts it once; justifying such an
alias, rather than assigning distinct arm-local cells, is an adapter
obligation.

The six typed history operators derive
$\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$ and $\mathsf{Required}_{op}$ as follows.  Here $N$
is the current contract and $K$ a checkpoint contract.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
  \toprule
  Operation $op$ & $\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$ & $\mathsf{Required}_{op}$ \\
  \midrule
  $\mathsf{ForkChoice}(L,R)$
    & $\mathsf{choice}(M_L,M_R)$
    & $\mathsf{choice}(R_L,R_R)$ \\
  $\mathsf{ForkParallel}(L,R)$
    & $\mathsf{tensor}(M_L,M_R)$
    & $\mathsf{tensor}(R_L,R_R)$ \\
  $\mathsf{RestoreReplace}(K)$
    & $M_K$ & $R_K$ \\
  $\mathsf{RestoreLive}(N,K)$
    & $\mathsf{tensor}(M_N,M_K)$
    & $\mathsf{tensor}(R_N,R_K)$ \\
  $\mathsf{MergeSelect}(L,R)$
    & $\mathsf{choice}(M_L,M_R)$
    & $\mathsf{choice}(R_L,R_R)$ \\
  $\mathsf{MergeJoin}(L,R)$
    & $\mathsf{tensor}(M_L,M_R)$
    & $\mathsf{tensor}(R_L,R_R)$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
A pure Checkpoint records reconstructable state and does not itself change a
future contract.  For every valid typed operator, both derived families remain
well formed and
\[
  \mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}.
\]
This inclusion is a fidelity condition, not yet an authorization result: it
prevents a repair from silently discarding behavior that the typed operation
declared mandatory.

\subsection{Fixed-prefix admission boundary}

The model fixes the authority family $\mathcal A$, durable receipt prefix $D$,
and cell lineage $\ell:\mathcal D\to\mathcal U$ for one decision.
It defines per-configuration compatibility in
\cref{eq:prefix-ok} and filters the generated candidate to
$\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$ in \cref{eq:admitted-future}.
The admitted future is prefix-safe and greatest within this fixed
candidate: every prefix-safe
$\mathcal H\subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$ is contained in
$\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  &\mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}\\
  &\quad\Longleftrightarrow
    \exists\mathcal H.\quad
      \mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathcal H
      \subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}\\[-2pt]
  &\hspace{38mm}{}\land
      \forall X\in\mathcal H.\ \mathsf{PrefixOK}(X).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:required-preservable}
\end{equation}
The result is maximal only within this fixed candidate, prefix, lineage, and
pruning-only repair space; it does not authorize reissued atoms or a changed
workload contract.

\subsection{Decision scope over time}

Equation~\eqref{eq:required-preservable} separates two cases.  If
$\mathsf{Required}_{op}\nsubseteq\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$, no pruning-only
implementation can preserve the typed promise.  Otherwise, a controller
restriction may be eligible only by satisfying the deployment sandwich; the
admitted family alone is not an enforcement mechanism.

One decision fixes $D$.  When immutable receipt bindings grow it to $D'$,
admission is rerun: a candidate lineage newly present in $D'$ fails the
disjointness clause of \cref{eq:prefix-ok}, while unrelated cells may survive.
The authenticated ledger and atomic redemption semantics that supply this
monotone prefix are assumptions at the boundary, not physical exactly-once
guarantees.
