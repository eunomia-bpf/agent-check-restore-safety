\section{Typed Edges and Admission Semantics}
\label{sec:semantics}

This section instantiates the model in three steps.  The edge type derives its
candidate and required endpoint families; the durable prefix derives the exact
serialized-monitor language; and the proposed controller epoch is accepted
only when its physical language refines that monitor without hiding a required
endpoint.

\subsection{Six before/after derivations}

For endpoint families $\mathcal F,\mathcal G$, define
\begin{align*}
  \mathsf{choice}(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)
    &=\mathcal F\cup\mathcal G,\\
  \mathsf{tensor}(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)
    &=\{X\cup Y\mid X\in\mathcal F,\ Y\in\mathcal G\}.
\end{align*}
Choice preserves either alternative without introducing their union.  Tensor
allows one outcome from each continuation to become durable together.  If one
semantic cell occurs in both operands, set union counts it once; the adapter
must justify that alias through $q_{\mathrm{cell}}$.

Let $N$ be the current continuation, $K$ a checkpoint continuation, and $L,R$
two branch continuations.  The before/after relation fixes the family
constructor:
\begin{center}
\scriptsize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{2pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.28\columnwidth}p{0.23\columnwidth}
                  p{0.19\columnwidth}p{0.19\columnwidth}@{}}
  \toprule
  Edge $e$ & before $\Rightarrow$ after
    & $\mathsf{Cand}_{e}$ & $\mathsf{Req}_{e}$\\
  \midrule
  $\mathsf{ForkChoice}(L,R)$
    & $N\Rightarrow L\mathbin{\mid}R$
    & $M_L\cup M_R$ & $R_L\cup R_R$\\
  $\mathsf{ForkParallel}(L,R)$
    & $N\Rightarrow L\otimes R$
    & $M_L\otimes M_R$ & $R_L\otimes R_R$\\
  $\mathsf{RestoreReplace}(K)$
    & $N\Rightarrow K$
    & $M_K$ & $R_K$\\
  $\mathsf{RestoreLive}(N,K)$
    & $N\Rightarrow N\otimes K$
    & $M_N\otimes M_K$ & $R_N\otimes R_K$\\
  $\mathsf{MergeSelect}(L,R)$
    & $(L,R)\Rightarrow L\mathbin{\mid}R$
    & $M_L\cup M_R$ & $R_L\cup R_R$\\
  $\mathsf{MergeJoin}(L,R)$
    & $(L,R)\Rightarrow L\otimes R$
    & $M_L\otimes M_R$ & $R_L\otimes R_R$\\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here $\mid$ and $\otimes$ in the middle column describe the post-edge
continuation relation, while the final columns apply the corresponding family
constructor.  The source $N$ does not grant new authority to freshly declared
Fork arms; their lineage and the fixed receipt prefix do.  A pure Checkpoint
records reconstructable state and creates no history edge.

Union and tensor preserve nonemptiness, the empty endpoint, and downward
closure.  Monotonicity of both constructors gives, for every valid typed edge,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:required-candidate}
  \mathsf{Req}_{e}\subseteq\mathsf{Cand}_{e}.
\end{equation}
Thus the operation type, rather than a caller-proposed ``safe successor,''
determines whether outcomes remain alternatives or may coexist.

\subsection{Exact serialized-monitor semantics}

The transition in \cref{eq:global-monitor-step} is atomic: it evaluates against
$D\cup\ell[X]$ and publishes the new spent state before another successful
fresh commit.  Replays resolve an existing binding and stutter.

\Cref{eq:global-monitor-language} is the exact trace language of
\cref{eq:global-monitor-step}.  The forward direction follows because every
transition leaves its endpoint in $\mathsf{Adm}_{e}(D)$.  Conversely, if
$X\in\mathsf{Adm}_{e}(D)$, downward closure makes every prefix of every
enumeration of $X$ pass the transition.  This proves
\cref{eq:global-monitor-endpoints} and the monitor's maximal permissiveness:
no serialized implementation satisfying the same candidate and prefix
invariant can admit an additional fresh-commit trace.

Filtering also gives the exact semantic feasibility boundary
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:required-preservable}
\begin{aligned}
  &\mathsf{Req}_{e}\subseteq\mathsf{Adm}_{e}(D)\\
  &\quad\Longleftrightarrow
    \exists\mathcal K.\quad
      \mathsf{Req}_{e}\subseteq\mathcal K
      \subseteq\mathsf{Cand}_{e}\\[-2pt]
  &\hspace{36mm}{}\land
      \forall X\in\mathcal K.\ \mathsf{PrefixOK}_{D}(X).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
When the left side fails, even the exact atomic monitor must eventually block
some required endpoint.  Installing the history edge anyway would remain safe
one commit at a time but would make the edge's required contract false.  This
is the gap between commit authorization and history admission.

\subsection{Controller compilation as language refinement}

The manifest replaces a global round trip only when its entire physical
language is contained in the monitor language.  Containment alone is
insufficient: the empty language is safe but destroys every nonempty promise.
Required transparency rules out that degenerate compilation.  Expanding
\cref{eq:history-admissible} using
\cref{eq:global-monitor-endpoints,eq:physical-language}, and writing
$\mathcal T=\mathsf{Phys}(E,L,\Gamma)$ and
$\mathcal P=\mathsf{RawPhysical}(E,L,\Gamma)$, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathcal T\subseteq\mathsf{Traces}(\mathsf{GM}_{e}(D))
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\
 \mathcal P\subseteq\mathsf{Adm}_{e}(D),\\
 &\mathsf{Req}_{e}\subseteq\mathsf{End}(\mathcal T)
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\
 \mathsf{Req}_{e}\subseteq\mathcal P.
\end{aligned}
\]
Their conjunction is exactly \cref{eq:deployment-ready}.  It characterizes,
for the supplied finite model, when the edge's serialized monitor can be
compiled into a controller epoch.  A pointwise atomic monitor proves the right
inclusion during execution; history admission additionally proves the left
inclusion before the edge becomes active.

This comparison gives the baseline its strongest reasonable advantage.  It
knows the complete edge candidate and current receipt frontier, serializes all
fresh commits, and checks the full source family.  History admission does not
claim a safer per-commit decision.  It establishes the different fact that a
specific post-edge controller topology refines that decision for every trace
and does not obtain safety by deleting required outcomes.

\subsection{Version-bound edge cuts and seals}

The mathematical predicate is useful only if the checked cut is the one the
runtime installs.  We state the required binding as an abstract transition
contract rather than claiming an implemented protocol.

Let an authoritative admission view at version $\nu$ be
\[
  V_\nu=
  \langle\mathcal A,e,q_{\mathrm{cell}},\ell,
  q_{\mathrm{ctrl}},E,L,\Gamma\rangle.
\]
An \emph{edge-cut seal}
\[
  S=\langle\nu,D,V_\nu,\mathcal K,\pi\rangle
\]
binds the exact receipt frontier and view, sets
$\mathcal K=\mathsf{RawPhysical}(E,L,\Gamma)$, and carries a checkable
derivation
$\pi:\mathsf{HistoryAdmissible}_{D}(e;E,L,\Gamma)$.
Its abstract installation rule is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:seal-install}
\frac{
  \mathsf{CurrentView}=V_\nu\quad
  \mathsf{CurrentFrontier}=D\quad
  \mathsf{Valid}(S)
}{
  \mathsf{ActiveEpoch}\xleftarrow{\;\mathsf{Install}(S)\;}
  \langle\nu,D,e,E,L,\Gamma,\mathcal K\rangle
}.
\end{equation}
The assignment in \cref{eq:seal-install} is one compare-and-install event: it
retires the preceding controller epoch and makes only the sealed epoch
authoritative.  A parallel Fork, live Restore, or join Merge retains old
continuations by placing them inside the new typed epoch, not by leaving an
unversioned predecessor authoritative.  Every protected fresh commitment must
carry the active epoch identity, and retired epochs must fail closed.

If the view version or receipt frontier changes before installation, the
comparison fails and admission is recomputed.  Commits produced after
installation are not stale reuse of the seal: they form one trace in
$\mathsf{Phys}(E,L,\Gamma)$, already covered from base frontier $D$ by the
trace refinement.  The next history edge observes the now-grown authenticated
frontier and obtains a new view and seal.  A receipt created outside the active
epoch, an unversioned controller, or a bypass around the epoch check violates
the stated runtime boundary rather than the finite predicate.

\Cref{eq:seal-install} is an integration obligation.  The current compiler and
Lean development check finite family, prefix, cover, and evidence results; they
do not implement or mechanize this atomic installer, authenticate $V_\nu$, or
prove that a concrete Agent runtime's traces equal the supplied manifest.

\subsection{Commitment substrate}

The fixed frontier relies on a separate atomic redemption substrate.  A fresh
prepare checks an open cell, a held unspent atom, an unbound stable key, and a
fresh receipt; it then consumes the hold and records immutable
cell--epoch--atom and receipt bindings.  Retry and replay resolve the binding
and stutter, settlement changes only the external phase, and crashes do not
erase the ledger.  These assumptions justify the monotone $D$ used above.
They do not by themselves guarantee exactly-once execution at a remote sink,
which requires idempotency, status reconciliation, or a transactional outbox.
