\section{Exact History Admission}
\label{sec:admission}

Let \(\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal U^*\) be a regular,
prefix-closed authority policy.  The authenticated receipt log \(\Delta\)
contains cell identities, phases, and qualified authority labels; its
projection \(\mathsf{lab}(\Delta)\in\mathcal A\) is an authority word.  Write
\(D_\Delta\) for its receipted cells.

\subsection{Fresh and alias resolution}

The key typing rule is that logical occurrences never disappear.  Starting
with \(R_0=D_\Delta\), the deterministic transducer
\(\Resolve_\Delta\) maps a raw word \(w=x_1\cdots x_n\) to a resolved word
\(z_1\cdots z_n\).  For \(d_i=q_{\rm cell}(x_i)\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:resolve}
 z_i=
 \begin{cases}
  \Fresh(x_i,d_i), & d_i\notin R_{i-1},
       \quad R_i=R_{i-1}\cup\{d_i\};\\
  \Alias(x_i,d_i), & d_i\in R_{i-1},
       \quad R_i=R_{i-1}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The authority projection erases aliases but not occurrences:
\[
 \mathsf{auth}(\Fresh(x,d))=\ell(d),\qquad
 \mathsf{auth}(\Alias(x,d))=\epsilon .
\]
A repeated delivery of the \emph{same} invocation identifier is \Retry and
does not occur a second time in \(w\).  In contrast, two restored occurrences
with the same cell both occur in \(w\); one may be fresh and the other alias.

\begin{lemma}[Resolution]
\label{lem:resolution}
\(\Resolve_\Delta\) is deterministic, length preserving, and prefix
compatible.  Its fresh projection contains each cell at most once outside
\(D_\Delta\), while its occurrence projection is exactly the input word.
Moreover, if
\(\Resolve_\Delta(rv)=zv'\), then \(z=\Resolve_\Delta(r)\) and
\(v'=\Resolve_{\Delta_z}(v)\), where \(\Delta_z\) extends \(\Delta\) by the
fresh events of \(z\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the input length and the accumulated set \(R_i\).  Each step emits
one symbol, updates \(R_i\) only in the fresh case, and depends only on the
input prefix.  Set membership gives uniqueness of fresh emission; both cases
retain \(x_i\).  Splitting the same induction after \(r\) gives the streaming
equation.
\end{proof}

This lemma fixes two distinct replay cases.  A network retry is a stutter in
both histories.  A copied occurrence is a stutter only in the authority
projection: it still resolves choice, sequence, or join causality.

\subsection{Safe completions and the admission boundary}

Suppose \(H\vdash u\Downarrow
(H',\mathcal C_{H,u},\Src_{H,u},\mathsf{Tgt}_{H,u})\).
For each structurally derived outcome \(o\in O_{H,u}\), define all resolved
completions and then its safe subset:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:safe-completions}
\begin{aligned}
 R_o(H,u)&=\{\Resolve_\Delta(w)\mid w\in\Lin(p_o)\},\\
 M_o(H,u)&=\{z\in R_o(H,u)\mid
   \mathsf{lab}(\Delta)\mathsf{auth}(z)\in\mathcal A\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The alphabets now agree: \(M_o\) contains resolved occurrence words, and the
policy is consulted only through their authority projection.

\begin{definition}[Agent history admission]
\label{def:admission}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:admission}
 \mathsf{Admit}(H,u)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \forall o\in O_{H,u}.\ M_o(H,u)\ne\varnothing .
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Universal quantification is over indexed outcomes derived by the trusted
operation, not over caller-supplied promises.  Existential quantification
inside \(M_o\) chooses a legal scheduler linearization; it does not promise
fairness.  A true parallel contract therefore requires a safe execution
containing both inputs, while a choice contract requires a safe execution for
each arm.

Let \(B^\star_{H,u}=\bigcup_{o\in O_{H,u}}M_o(H,u)\) be the marked safe
completions.  The ideal generated language is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:history-language}
 W_{H,u}=\Pref(B^\star_{H,u}).
\end{equation}
It contains prefixes of safe \emph{complete} executions, not every prefix
that happens to be policy safe now.

\begin{definition}[Realization]
\label{def:realization}
A generated/marked pair \((L,B)\) realizes \((H,u)\) when:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
 \item \emph{history fidelity:}
\(B\subseteq\bigcup_{o\in O_{H,u}}R_o(H,u)\) and
\(L\subseteq\Pref(\bigcup_{o\in O_{H,u}}R_o(H,u))\);
 \item \emph{policy safety:}
 \(\mathsf{lab}(\Delta)\,\mathsf{auth}(z)\in\mathcal A\)
 for every \(z\in L\);
 \item \emph{completion nonblocking:}
 \(L=\Pref(B)\); and
 \item \emph{outcome coverage:}
 \(B\cap R_o(H,u)\ne\varnothing\) for every \(o\in O_{H,u}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Greatest realization]
\label{lem:greatest-realization}
A realization exists iff \(\mathsf{Admit}(H,u)\).
On admission, \((W_{H,u},B^\star_{H,u})\) is a realization, and every
realization \((L,B)\) satisfies
\(B\subseteq B^\star_{H,u}\) and \(L\subseteq W_{H,u}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every member of \(B^\star\) resolves a derived outcome and is safe;
\(W=\Pref(B^\star)\) gives nonblocking, and admission supplies coverage.
Conversely, fidelity and safety put every \(b\in B\) in some \(M_o\), so
\(B\subseteq B^\star\) and
\(L=\Pref(B)\subseteq\Pref(B^\star)=W\).  Finally, coverage of \(o\) would
witness \(M_o\ne\varnothing\), so a missing \(M_o\) is impossible.
\end{proof}

The nonblocking clause is what makes the boundary stronger than filtering
pointwise-safe prefixes.  For example, if a payment prefix is legal but makes
every registered shipment completion impossible, it is absent from \(W\).

\begin{lemma}[Residual coherence]
\label{lem:residual-coherence}
If \(z\in W(\mathcal C,\Delta)\), then
\(\Resolve_\Delta(\mathsf{raw}(z))=z\),
\(\mathsf{lab}(\Delta_z)=
 \mathsf{lab}(\Delta)\mathsf{auth}(z)\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 W(\mathcal C,\Delta)/z
  &=W(\mathcal C/\mathsf{raw}(z),\Delta_z),\\
 B^\star(\mathcal C,\Delta)/z
  &=B^\star(\mathcal C/\mathsf{raw}(z),\Delta_z).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Because \(z\) prefixes a member of \(B^\star\), prefix compatibility and raw
recoverability in \cref{lem:resolution} give the first equality; its streaming
case gives the ledger equality.  Taking the indexed pomset residual removes
exactly that raw prefix while retaining every consistent outcome identity.
Applying the same policy residual to the remaining suffixes gives the marked
equality, and taking prefixes gives the generated equality.
\end{proof}
After a prefix, coverage ranges over residual outcome indices consistent with
that prefix, not over alternatives already resolved away.

\subsection{The ideal atomic history machine}
\label{sec:ideal}

The ideal LTS retains abstract gate/owner identities so protected calls and
history edges are defined, but has no generation phases, seals, preloading, or
handoff microsteps: a history edge updates all affected identities atomically.
Its durable state contains \((\kappa,H,\Delta,\mathsf{out})\) plus
the current admitted cut
\(c=(H_c,u,W_{H_c,u},B^\star_{H_c,u})\) and resolved prefix
\(r\).  It has the following transitions.

\paragraph{History edge}
\(\mathsf{Edge}(u)\) atomically applies the unique judgment
\(H\vdash u\Downarrow(H',\mathcal C,\Src,\mathsf{Tgt})\) when
\(\mathsf{Admit}(H,u)\), persists the already version-advanced \(H'\), and
sets the active reference to
\((W_{H,u},B^\star_{H,u},\epsilon)\).  There is no semantic
handoff protocol: this is the independent atomic behavior that the
implementation must refine.

\paragraph{Protected occurrence}
Let \(x\) be an enabled occurrence of the current frontier with expected gate
and authenticated cell \(d\).  Resolve it against the current receipts to
\(a\in\{\Fresh(x,d),\Alias(x,d)\}\).  The transition is enabled exactly when
\(ra\in W_{H_c,u}\).  It atomically residualizes the frontier by \(x\),
appends \(a\) to \(\chi\), advances \(\nu\), and sets
\(r:=ra\).  In the fresh case it also appends an immutable receipt
for \(d\), labeled \(\ell(d)\), to \(\Delta\) and enqueues \(d\); in the alias
case those fields do not change.

An invalid occurrence, gate, handle, or disabled branch produces
\(\mathsf{deny}(x)\) without changing state.  An alias binds the restored
occurrence to the same durable result future as its cell; it advances causal
history but does not invent a result that the original invocation has not yet
produced.

\paragraph{Retry, release, and crash}
A same-invocation retry returns the recorded result without changing history.
\Dispatch may release only the oldest prepared outbox item, so the release
trace is a prefix of fresh preparation order.  Settlement changes only a
receipt phase.  Crash discards nondurable computation and leaves the LTS state
unchanged.  We make no claim about completion order inside an external
network after release.

By \cref{lem:greatest-realization}, every reachable reference prefix retains
a policy-safe completion until the next explicit history edge changes the
contract.  The next sections implement this ideal machine without consulting
the full completion set at every call.
