\section{Lifecycle Semantics}
\label{sec:semantics}

The untrusted agent proposes lifecycle and effect operations.
The trusted controller admits a proposal only if its durable target is well formed and authority-continuous.
This section makes the rule-specific targets explicit; \cref{sec:results} proves their common consequences.

\subsection{Fork, selection, and restore}

\paragraph{Fork does not copy claims.}
When a leaf \(b\) becomes children \(b_1,b_2\), its local state may be copied.
For each \(c\in Q_b\), the ledger must choose one durable operation: transfer \(c\) to one child, split a divisible demand into fresh claims whose sum is authorized, retain \(c\) in an explicit escrow, or move it to \(X\).
Placing \(c\) in both children violates \cref{eq:partition}.
Creating two fresh full-demand claims is visible to \(\ac\).

\paragraph{Selection is a security event.}
Selection precedes release, not merely garbage collection.
The controller records which outcomes remain possible, tombstones losing epochs, and moves their tentative claims to \(X\).
A crash between these actions must resume from the durable decision.

For an eligible branch \(b\), define the family conditioned on retaining it:
\begin{equation}
\Phi{\downarrow}b
=
\{C'\mid \exists C\in\Phi.\ b\in C\land C'\subseteq C\}.
\label{eq:condition}
\end{equation}
This keeps branches that may coexist with \(b\) and removes alternatives that cannot.

\paragraph{Two contextual restore modes.}
Let \(E[\cdot]\) be the surrounding structured lifecycle context of leaf \(b\).
\textsc{RestoreReplace} atomically kills \(b\) and installs \(E[b']\) with a fresh epoch.
It transfers a tentative claim only when its issuer permits owner/epoch transfer and the staged object required by its binding remains durably available or is proven binding-equivalent after reconstruction; otherwise the claim enters \(X\).
\textsc{RestoreLive} installs \(E[b\parallel b']\), not \(E[b]\parallel b'\).
The contextual rule preserves conflicts inherited from alternatives outside the restored leaf.
For example, restoring \(b\) live inside \(b\Box x\) yields \((b\parallel b')\Box x\), so the clone does not become spuriously compatible with \(x\).

\subsection{Escape promotion}

Plain escape moves \(c\in Q_b\) to \(D\) without changing \(\Phi\).
This is not a bookkeeping-neutral commit.
Let
\[
M_{\neg b}
=
\bigvee_{\substack{C\in\Phi\\b\notin C}}q(C).
\]
The empty frontier ensures this supremum exists.
For frontiers containing \(b\), moving \(c\) from \(Q_b\) to \(D\) preserves total load.
For frontiers not containing \(b\), it adds \(w(c)\).
Thus, from a safe source, plain escape is safe with added capacity \(A\) exactly when
\begin{equation}
A\ge
\Delta_{\esc}(b,c)
=
[d+w(c)+M_{\neg b}-G]^+.
\label{eq:escape-deficit}
\end{equation}

The gate performs this admission before dispatch.
It first persists the promotion or an uncertain record; otherwise a crash could deliver an external effect while restoring \(c\) as tentative.
If the sink supplies a stable operation token, reconciliation may later refine an uncertain result.
Without such evidence, conservatively charging \(c\) is necessary for safety and may sacrifice liveness.

\subsection{Merge and revocation}

Merge must state what it retains and what lifecycle it creates.
Combining only files or model outputs does not move authority.
There is no unique topology implied by merging two branches.
A join-merge may replace sources already present in one co-durable cone; an alternative-synthesis merge supplies and admits a new \(\Phi'\).
The operation explicitly records source tombstones and transfers each retained claim once, subject to binding preservation.
Combining claims from exclusive alternatives usually expands support and may violate \(\ac\).
Durable claims never move with a branch: an escaped payment or disclosure remains in \(D\) whether the branch is merged, selected, or discarded.

Revocation closes a grant epoch in the durable ledger.
Tentative claims under that epoch can no longer escape and move to \(X\).
Durable uses remain historical consumption.
A checkpoint contains a reference to the closed epoch; restoring it does not recreate a live grant.

\subsection{A deliberately safe non-example}

The semantics accepts delayed escrow.
Two exclusive children with \(Q_{b_1}=Q_{b_2}=\varnothing\) may compute arbitrarily.
After durable selection, a parent transfers one available claim to the winner.
No topology-aware reservation was needed before selection.
Our claim is narrower: lifecycle structure is necessary to account for \emph{advance conditional guarantees} and effects that change support, not to make pure speculative computation safe.
