\section{Typed History-Admission Semantics}
\label{sec:semantics}

A history transformation is admitted from its typed operation and contracts,
not from a caller-proposed successor or a Boolean claim that the successor is
safe.  The semantics first derives the correlated future promised by the
operation, then checks that future against the immutable receipt prefix.  This
separates three questions that a snapshot-only rule conflates: what the
operation may do, what it must continue to support, and what remains authorized
after earlier commitments have become durable.

\subsection{Typed-operator future derivation}

A future contract $K=(M_K,R_K)$ contains a downward-closed
\emph{may} family and a downward-closed \emph{required} family over normalized
semantic redemption cells, with
$R_K\subseteq M_K$.
The cells are atomic redemption identities, not controller names or copied
workspace handles.  Define exclusive choice and joint durability by
\begin{align*}
  \mathsf{choice}(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)
    &= \mathcal F\cup\mathcal G,\\
  \mathsf{tensor}(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)
    &= \{X\cup Y\mid X\in\mathcal F,\ Y\in\mathcal G\}.
\end{align*}
Choice retains either arm without introducing their union.  Tensor permits one
configuration from each arm to become durable together.  If the same semantic
cell occurs in both operands, set union counts it once; justifying such an
alias, rather than assigning distinct arm-local cells, is an adapter
obligation.

The six typed history operators derive
$\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$ and $\mathsf{Required}_{op}$ as follows.  Here $N$
is the current contract and $K$ a checkpoint contract.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
  \toprule
  Operation $op$ & $\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$ & $\mathsf{Required}_{op}$ \\
  \midrule
  $\mathsf{ForkChoice}(L,R)$
    & $\mathsf{choice}(M_L,M_R)$
    & $\mathsf{choice}(R_L,R_R)$ \\
  $\mathsf{ForkParallel}(L,R)$
    & $\mathsf{tensor}(M_L,M_R)$
    & $\mathsf{tensor}(R_L,R_R)$ \\
  $\mathsf{RestoreReplace}(K)$
    & $M_K$ & $R_K$ \\
  $\mathsf{RestoreLive}(N,K)$
    & $\mathsf{tensor}(M_N,M_K)$
    & $\mathsf{tensor}(R_N,R_K)$ \\
  $\mathsf{MergeSelect}(L,R)$
    & $\mathsf{choice}(M_L,M_R)$
    & $\mathsf{choice}(R_L,R_R)$ \\
  $\mathsf{MergeJoin}(L,R)$
    & $\mathsf{tensor}(M_L,M_R)$
    & $\mathsf{tensor}(R_L,R_R)$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
A pure Checkpoint records reconstructable state and does not itself change a
future contract.  For every valid typed operator, both derived families remain
well formed and
\[
  \mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}.
\]
This inclusion is a fidelity condition, not yet an authorization result: it
prevents a repair from silently discarding behavior that the typed operation
declared mandatory.

\subsection{Fixed-prefix admission boundary}

The model fixes the authority family $\mathcal A$, durable receipt prefix $D$,
and cell lineage $\ell:\mathcal D\to\mathcal U$ for one decision.
It defines per-configuration compatibility in
\cref{eq:prefix-ok} and filters the generated candidate to
$\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$ in \cref{eq:admitted-future}.
The admitted future is prefix-safe and greatest within this fixed
candidate: every prefix-safe
$\mathcal H\subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$ is contained in
$\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  &\mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}\\
  &\quad\Longleftrightarrow
    \exists\mathcal H.\quad
      \mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathcal H
      \subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}\\[-2pt]
  &\hspace{38mm}{}\land
      \forall X\in\mathcal H.\ \mathsf{PrefixOK}(X).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:required-preservable}
\end{equation}
The result is maximal only within this fixed candidate, prefix, lineage, and
pruning-only repair space; it does not authorize reissued atoms or a changed
workload contract.

\subsection{Structural inheritance and full readmission}

An admitted envelope stores a version $v$, the fixed prefix $D$, an admitted
old future $\mathcal F$, its lineage $\ell_0$, and a proof that every
$X\in\mathcal F$ is prefix-compatible.
A structural refinement of a typed target at version $v'$ supplies a parent
map $p:\mathcal D_{op}\to\mathcal D_0$ such that $v\leq v'$, every target
configuration maps injectively into a configuration of $\mathcal F$, and the
lineage square commutes:
\[
  \ell(x)=\ell_0(p(x)).
\]
These obligations transport the old prefix certificate to the complete
candidate $\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$ without rechecking the authority family.
They do not assert that a concrete runtime realizes that candidate.

If no such parent map is available, the full-readmission path checks
$\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}=\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$ directly.  Structural
inheritance is therefore a sufficient certificate-reuse path into full
readmission, not a weaker safety class.  Refinements compose: version order is
transitive, parent maps compose, their configuration morphisms compose, and
the two lineage squares yield the commuting square for the endpoint.  A
runtime may consequently certify a sequence of Fork/Restore/Merge edges at the
same durable prefix by composing their edge refinements.

\subsection{Four fixed-prefix outcomes}

The classifier checks structural inheritance first, then complete
readmission, then the admitted future.  For a valid typed operator
it returns one of four outcomes:
\paragraph{$\mathsf{Inherit}$}
A version-monotone structural refinement transports the old certificate to
the complete candidate.

\paragraph{$\mathsf{ReadmitOK}$}
Structural inheritance is unavailable, but the complete candidate passes the
fixed-prefix check directly.

\paragraph{$\mathsf{NeedsMechanism}$}
The complete candidate fails fixed-prefix admission, while every required
configuration survives in $\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$.  This is not an accepted
deployment by itself: the admitted future is not an enforcement mechanism.
Deployment becomes structurally eligible only if the submitted controller
manifest supplies a restriction satisfying
$\mathsf{Required}\subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}$;
installation and complete mediation remain external obligations.

\paragraph{$\mathsf{Reject}$}
No prefix-safe pruning between $\mathsf{Required}_{op}$ and
$\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$ exists for the fixed authority family, durable
prefix, and lineage.  Reauthorization, fresh identities, or a changed contract
constitutes a new admission problem.

The first two outcomes partition complete admission according to whether an
old certificate can be reused.  The latter two are separated exactly by
\cref{eq:required-preservable}; required behavior cannot be relabeled as
optional to obtain acceptance.  All four remain structural results rather
than credentials for executing an external effect.

\subsection{Receipt-frontier growth}

The durable prefix is obtained from immutable receipt bindings.  Ledger
monotonicity therefore gives $D\subseteq D'$ as execution commits additional
atoms.  Structural transport deliberately preserves $D$ definitionally: it
cannot turn a proof about $(\mathcal A,D)$ into one about
$(\mathcal A,D')$.  Once the receipt prefix grows, the runtime must reconstruct
the envelope and rerun prefix admission against $D'$ before applying further
structural inheritance.

This recheck is substantive rather than bookkeeping.  If a candidate cell
maps to an atom newly present in $D'$, the disjointness conjunct in
\cref{eq:prefix-ok} rules out policy-oblivious reuse even when a snapshot-only
configuration morphism would still succeed.  A future using a distinct atom
may remain admissible.  Thus prefix growth forces readmission, not automatic
rejection; the new receipt determines which candidate configurations survive.

\subsection{Operational commitment substrate}

The prefix comes from a separate unit-normalized commitment semantics.  A
fresh \Prepare{} atomically checks an open cell, a held unspent atom, an unbound
stable key, and a fresh receipt; it consumes the hold, records the spent
cell--epoch--atom triple and immutable bindings, and marks the receipt prepared.
Replay and Retry resolve that binding and stutter; settlement advances only the
external phase, and Crash is an atomic stutter.  The preserved invariant and
monotone ledger show that only a fresh step adds a receipt, justifying $D$ as a
monotone prefix.  They do not prove physical exactly-once execution at a sink.
