\section{Problem and Security Boundary}
\label{sec:lifecycle}

\subsection{History-transforming computation}

A checkpointed execution traditionally replaces its current continuation with
one reconstructed from an earlier state.  The workload considered here is
more general: a runtime may retain several descendants, replace one
continuation while another remains live, or combine results from histories
that were previously alternatives.  We call an execution with these operations
a \emph{history-transforming computation}.  Speculative workflows, search and
reinforcement-learning rollouts, deployment planners, and notebook branches
all fit this class.  Tool-using agents make the class especially prominent
because they construct the execution graph online while invoking services
whose state is outside the reconstructed process.

Current agent interfaces already expose fragments of this distinction.
Codex documents thread creation, resume, fork lineage, and event streams, while
Claude documents both resuming and forking saved sessions
~\cite{codexappserver,claudesessions}.  Filesystem-oriented agent substrates
add isolated worktrees or sandbox checkpoints
~\cite{wang2026fork,wu2026crab}.  These are useful reconstruction mechanisms,
but their names do not determine security semantics.  A fork may mean
exclusive alternatives or co-durable parallel work; a restore may replace the
current continuation or leave both versions live; and a merge may select one
arm or retain commitments from both.  We therefore model six typed operators:
choice and parallel Fork, replacing and live Restore, and select and join
Merge.  A pure Checkpoint records reconstructable state and changes no future
contract by itself.

The distinction matters because one execution graph need not be specified in
advance.  A runtime describes the proposed edge and a finite contract for the
protected authority slots its arms may and must retain; it need not predict
the natural-language call graph.  A later call needing a new cell or behavior
outside that contract is not covered by the seal and triggers a new admission
decision.  Every later edge is checked against the then-current receipt
frontier; a newly committed receipt likewise reopens admission.  This is how
the finite model handles a dynamically growing graph without claiming to
classify unmodeled future calls.

Rolling back every local byte is insufficient in three different ways.
First, an external effect may already have escaped the rollback domain.  A
restored workspace can erase evidence of a message or remote write without
undoing it; Claude's documented checkpoint boundary, for example, excludes
Bash, external, and most subagent changes~\cite{claudecheckpoint}.  Second,
rolling back the durable receipt ledger would revive authority already spent.
The ledger must therefore advance independently of reconstructable state.
Third, total rollback is often not the requested operation: live Restore and
parallel Fork deliberately retain both descendants, while join Merge combines
them.  Discarding every descendant would implement different semantics and
could destroy unrelated completed work.

The missing object is consequently not ``more workspace state.''  For a fixed
durable prefix, admission must determine which sets of prospective semantic
commitments may still become durable together.  This is a family of
configurations rather than a vector of per-effect flags: admitting each effect
separately does not imply that their union is authorized.  The controller
layout is yet another object, because the state enforcing such a correlation
may live behind tool gates, reference monitors, or remote services rather than
inside the restored workspace.

\subsection{Two independent identities}

Handle identity asks whether names share one atomic redemption cell; gate-use
identity asks whether executions share the controller state preserving
correlation.  Neither determines the other: aliases may pass through replicas,
while distinct cells may rely on one joint controller.  Atomic redemption
addresses the first case, not arbitrary correlations
(\cref{prop:identity-erasure}).

\paragraph{Worked instance}
Let
\[
 U(2,3)=\{X\subseteq\{a,b,c\}\mid |X|\leq2\}.
\]
Consider a deployment-planning checkpoint $K$ with three one-shot rollout
cells $a,b,c$.  Its complete leaf contract is
$M_K=2^{\{a,b,c\}}$ and $R_K=U(2,3)$: any subset may be requested, while the
edge promises to retain every at-most-two outcome.  Take the exact typed edge
$\mathsf{RestoreReplace}(K)$, an empty receipt prefix, identity lineage, and
source authority family $U(2,3)$.  The operator rule and prefix filter now
derive the first three rows of \cref{tab:worked-instance}.  Each deployment
gate $g_x$ has the locally sound choice
$L_{g_x}=\{\varnothing,\{x\}\}$.  If all three gates may remain live, their
product contains the forbidden triple and admission rejects.  Restricting
co-liveness to at most two gates yields exactly $U(2,3)$ and admission accepts.

Thus the full sandwich is computed, not inferred from individual calls.
Arity two cannot distinguish the layouts, while arity three decides this
contract.  Separately, copied names must first be folded to cells and gate uses
to controllers: otherwise the checker does not know whether apparent
independence is real.  This is why the raw manifest supplies both identity
relations before the co-liveness projection.

\subsection{Runtime integration contract}

Our proposed tool sits at the control-plane boundary before a runtime installs
a Fork, Restore, or Merge edge.  A trusted adapter supplies (1) the typed
operator and complete per-arm may/required contracts; (2) explicit cell alias
and authority-lineage evidence; (3) the authenticated append-only receipt
frontier; and (4) controller access, local-family, and co-liveness evidence.
The compiler and independent verifier implement the finite decision in
\cref{sec:algorithm}.  Their output is a history-admission seal or a replayable
counterexample, not permission to execute a tool.

Agent lifecycle events and pre-tool hooks are prospective adapter surfaces
~\cite{codexappserver,codexhooks,claudesessions,claudehooks}.  Their existence
does not prove that every relevant effect is mediated, that two handles are
true aliases, or that two gate uses share controller state.  A concrete
integration must authenticate those facts and establish the runtime refinement
obligations
\[
  \mathsf{Required}\subseteq\mathsf{Actual}
  \subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}.
\]
The first inclusion prevents the adapter from obtaining safety by deleting
mandatory behavior; the second requires the declared controller product to
overapproximate every configuration the installed runtime can actually make
durable.  The offline compiler checks neither fact by observing a transcript.

Exact projected co-liveness has three distinct operational paths.  A verifier
can receive full $\Gamma$ and compute its projection; a runtime can install
the projected hereditary family itself as the complete epoch policy and
resubmit it; or a trusted adapter can attest that a compressed input is exactly
$\Pi_{\leq r}(\Gamma)$ for a hidden family.  Only the last path leaves
projection completeness as an external fact.  The schema distinguishes these
paths and never upgrades a mere upper bound to a positive decision.

\subsection{Adversary, trusted base, and goal}

The adversary controls model output, copied or restored history, tool
arguments, proposed handles, and stale reconstructable state.  It may request
any documented history operation or retry after an ambiguous external
outcome.  The trusted base consists of the policy and authority issuer, the
lifecycle adapter for the admitted edge, the append-only receipt integrity
root, and the controller instances mediating protected commitments.  The
adapter must report the actual operation semantics and supply complete leaf
contracts and identity evidence; these facts cannot be inferred reliably from
natural-language intent or a filesystem diff.

For one fixed authority family and durable receipt prefix, the semantic goal
is to retain exactly the greatest admitted future inside the typed candidate while
preserving all declared required configurations when possible.  The deployment
goal is to ensure that the manifest's raw controller product covers the
required family and stays inside that admitted subfamily.  Under the additional
runtime refinements above, every actual co-durable configuration inherits the
durable-prefix safety certificate.

The result is conditional on truthful manifests, complete mediation,
controller freshness, and atomic redemption.  It does not establish prompt
integrity, infer policy from model text, validate a lease, guarantee useful
task completion, prevent issue-time information leakage, or provide physical
exactly-once execution at an external sink.  Ambiguous external outcomes still
require authenticated status, sink idempotency, or explicitly authorized
compensation.
