\section{One Agent, Two Histories}
\label{sec:lifecycle}

Consider a procurement Agent authorized to place one order.  It durably
reserves the budget, so the receipt ledger \(\Delta\) satisfies
\(\mathsf{lab}(\Delta)=r\), and saves checkpoint \(k\) before approval.
Approval occurrence \(x_a\) then commits through cell \(d_a\), whose authority
label is \(a\), so
\(\mathsf{lab}(\Delta)=ra\).  The Agent requests a choice Fork between two
suppliers, \(L\) and \(R\).  Its registered causal-completion contract has exactly two indexed
outcomes.
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:running-outcomes}
\begin{split}
 o_L&:\ x_L^p\prec x_L^s,\\
 o_R&:\ x_R^p\prec x_R^s ,
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where \(x_i^p\) and \(x_i^s\) are payment and shipment occurrences.  The
alternatives are exclusive, but both must have a safe completion at the cut.

A logical occurrence is distinct from the semantic cell whose authority it
redeems.  The protected-call registry authenticates
\[
 q_{\rm cell}(x_L^s)=q_{\rm cell}(x_R^s)=d_s.
\]
The two branch occurrences refer to the same shipment cell but receive
distinct occurrence-bound handles after the Fork.  Payments retain distinct
cells \(d_L^p\) and \(d_R^p\).  The authority labels of
\(d_s,d_L^p,d_R^p\) are \(s,p_L,p_R\), respectively.  A later Restore of \(k\) creates approval
occurrence \(x_a'\) with \(q_{\rm cell}(x_a')=d_a\).  The authority policy
allows prefixes of \(rap_Ls\) and \(rap_Rs\), but no trace containing both
payment labels or a payment before \(a\).

\paragraph{The admission calculation}
Using the admission notation formalized in \cref{sec:admission}, write \(M_o\)
for outcome \(o\)'s policy-safe resolved completions.  At this cut each outcome
has one causal linearization, resolving to
\[
\begin{aligned}
 z_L&=\Fresh(x_L^p,d_L^p)\Fresh(x_L^s,d_s),\\
 z_R&=\Fresh(x_R^p,d_R^p)\Fresh(x_R^s,d_s).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(M_{o_L}=\{z_L\}\), \(M_{o_R}=\{z_R\}\), and
\(\widehat B^\dagger=\{(o_L,z_L),(o_R,z_R)\}\), with \(W^\dagger\) their
prefix closure.  On acceptance, these prefixes become the generated behavior
of the compiled monitor.  If policy excludes \(rap_Rs\), then
\(M_{o_R}=\varnothing\), and the Fork is rejected because pruning supplier
\(R\) would change its meaning.  \Cref{sec:admission} gives the shared-prefix
case where every outcome starts safe but no prefix-robust family exists.

\paragraph{The \Fresh race}
After the shared approval and before either payment, the Agent requests
\(\mathsf{MergeSelect}(g,L,\sigma)\).  If a protected use from the old
supplier-\(R\) arm commits \(x_R^p\) first, that occurrence durably selects
\(R\) and changes the receipt
ledger's authority trace \(\mathsf{lab}(\Delta)\) from \(ra\) to \(rap_R\).
Selecting \(L\) must therefore be re-derived
and rejected.  If the Merge cut wins, it closes the current
policy-domain epoch and rebinds every live gate before any later \(x_R^p\) can
commit.  Installing a new program while an old policy-domain epoch remains
active permits both payments.

\paragraph{The \Alias race}
Now consider the live Restore in \cref{fig:two-histories}.  Cloning \(k\)
creates a new logical approval occurrence \(x_a'\), a newly minted gate, and an
authenticated handle, but retains cell \(d_a\).  Because \(d_a\) is
already receipted, executing \(x_a'\) is an
\Alias that advances the restored continuation past approval while leaving
\(\Delta\) unchanged.  A concurrent join or replacement compiled before that
progress is stale even though its receipt comparison still succeeds.  The cut
must therefore compare the resolved-occurrence trace \(\chi\) in addition to
the receipt ledger.

The example exposes the whole problem.  Workspace equality cannot recover
which branch relation is live, and receipt equality cannot detect \Alias
progress.  Cell equality cannot determine the required obligation projection,
while a caller-supplied target automaton cannot certify any of these facts.
\Cref{sec:model} instead derives the target through a trusted history judgment.
