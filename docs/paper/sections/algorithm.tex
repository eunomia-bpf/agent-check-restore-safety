\section{Compilation and Durable Refinement}
\label{sec:kernel}

\subsection{Occurrence-sensitive compilation}

Compilation turns the greatest realization into the canonical program that
the trusted kernel can install.  Every derived pomset is finite, so
\(W=W^\dagger_{H,u}\) and \(B=B^\dagger_{H,u}\) are finite.  For
\(L/z=\{v\mid zv\in L\}\), the compiler emits the generated/marked residual
program with states \(q_z=(W^\dagger/z,B^\dagger/z)\).
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:canonical-program}
\begin{aligned}
 Q_{W,B} &= \{q_z\mid z\in W\},\\
 q^0&=q_\epsilon=(W^\dagger,B^\dagger),\\
 q_z\xrightarrow{a}q_{za}
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad za\in W .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Write \(\mathsf{Can}(W,B)\) for this derivative automaton.
Every state is reachable, and a state is marked exactly when
\(\epsilon\in B^\dagger/z\).
The transition label \(a\) contains the logical occurrence \(x\), semantic
cell \(d\), and fresh/alias mode.  Consequently an enabled cell cannot be
exercised through the gate of a different branch occurrence.

\begin{lemma}[Exact compiled realization]
\label{lem:compiled-language}
The generated and marked languages of the program in
\cref{eq:canonical-program} are exactly
\((W^\dagger_{H,u},B^\dagger_{H,u})\).  The program is the smallest deterministic marked
partial automaton for this pair up to state renaming.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
After \(z\), induction places the program in
\((W^\dagger/z,B^\dagger/z)\).  The rule enables \(a\) exactly when
\(za\in W^\dagger\), and
the state is marked exactly when \(z\in B^\dagger\).  Two retained states differ
on a generated or marked suffix, so every deterministic marked implementation
must distinguish them.  The construction is the paired-language form of
classical derivatives~\cite{brzozowski1964derivatives}.
\end{proof}

\(\mathsf{Compile}(\kappa,H,\Delta,\mathcal A,u)\) first runs the schema
judgment.  If that judgment has no derivation, \(\mathsf{Compile}\) returns a
structural error record identifying the failed check.  Otherwise it computes
\cref{eq:admission}.  An empty fixed point returns the descending chain and
ranked missing-witness causes described after
\cref{lem:greatest-realization}.  Success emits
\(\widehat B^\dagger\), its complete stabilization chain, all
persistent-coverage witnesses, the program over the projected marked language
\(B^\dagger\),
\(\mathsf{prog}_Z=\mathsf{Can}(W^\dagger,B^\dagger)\), and a seal
\[
 Z=(\omega,\zeta,\kappa_Z,\mathsf{cut}_Z,u,
    H_{\mathsf{prog}},\eta^-,\eta^+),
\]
where \(\kappa_Z=\kappa\), \(H_c=H\) is the authenticated pre-installation
history, and \(\eta^+\) is deterministically minted by the history judgment.

Let \(\mathsf{enc}\) be a canonical, length-delimited encoding.  Policy version
\(\kappa\) binds the issuer-authenticated encoding of \(\mathcal A\).  The
formal LTS uses domain-separated injective authenticated constructors
\(\mathsf{Seal}_{\rm prog}\) and \(\mathsf{Seal}_{\rm cut}\).  Equality of
symbolic seals therefore implies equality of their encoded payloads.  Define
\[
 H_{\mathsf{prog}}=
 \mathsf{Seal}_{\rm prog}(
   \mathsf{enc}(\mathsf{prog}_Z,\widehat B^\dagger_{H,u})).
\]
The cut seal is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cut-seal}
 \mathsf{cut}_Z=
 \mathsf{Seal}_{\rm cut}(\mathsf{enc}(\omega,\zeta,\kappa,H,\Delta,u,
     \mathcal C_{H,u},H_{\mathsf{prog}},\eta^-,\eta^+)).
\end{equation}
Because \(H\) contains \(\chi,\nu,\rho,\beta\), the seal binds the program to
both logical and epoch frontiers.  Compilation and inactive preloading are
untrusted, unobservable work.  A small checker re-derives the schema judgment,
recomputes
the indexed fixed point and its complete chain, reconstructs
\((W^\dagger,B^\dagger)\), and verifies
\[
 \mathsf{prog}_Z\cong\mathsf{Can}(W^\dagger,B^\dagger),
\]
where the isomorphism preserves the initial state, markedness, and every
labeled transition.  It then checks the program and cut seals before
installation.  Language equality alone is not accepted.  A byte implementation
may realize these constructors with domain-separated signatures or
collision-resistant digests over \(\mathsf{enc}\).  The refinement claim is
then computational up to forgery or collision probability.  The exact
bisimulation theorem uses the symbolic constructors.

\subsection{Durable kernel}

The compiled state for one policy domain is
\[
 S=(\kappa,H,\Delta,\mathsf{out},
    \mathsf{phase},\mathsf{bind},\mathsf{prog},\mathsf q,\mathsf{cert}),
\]
where epoch phase is
\(\mathsf{inactive}\), \(\mathsf{active}\), or \(\mathsf{closed}\).
\(\mathsf{cert}(\eta)\) is the checked installation record
\((H_c,u,W^\dagger,\widehat B^\dagger,Z)\) for an active epoch.  Its indexed
family is proof ghost state, while \(W^\dagger\), \(B^\dagger\), and the seal
are checked enforcement data.
The following invariant is required:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
 \item exactly the current epoch \(\eta\) is active and every live occurrence
       has one gate bound to it;
 \item inactive preloaded epochs own no live gate; arbitrary unreachable
       inactive programs may be discarded as preload garbage;
 \item the current certificate agrees with the admitted cut and projects to
       the active program's generated/marked residual after the current prefix;
 \item every resolved occurrence appears once in ordered \(\chi\);
 \item every \Fresh in \(\chi\) has exactly one receipt in \(\Delta\), and
       every \Alias names an earlier receipt;
 \item a closed epoch never becomes active again.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Atomic protected use}
Let the current history bind occurrence \(x\) to expected gate \(j\), owned by
\(\eta\), and let the supplied handle authenticate its expected cell \(d\).
\(\mathsf{VerifyHandle}(h,\rho(x),j,d)\) checks issuer authentication, scope,
effect digest, gate, and cell against the immutable registry record.
The kernel computes \(a=\Fresh(x,d)\) when \(d\notin D_\Delta\), and
\(a=\Alias(x,d)\) otherwise.  Write \(\Use_x=\mathsf{Use}(x,j,h)\).
Its one durable transition is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:use-rule}
\frac{
\begin{gathered}
 x\text{ enabled in }T\quad
 \mathsf{bind}(x)=(j,\eta)\\[-2pt]
 \eta=H.\mathsf{epoch}\quad
 \mathsf{phase}(\eta)=\mathsf{active}\\[-2pt]
 \mathsf{VerifyHandle}(h,\rho(x),j,d)\quad
 \mathsf q(\eta)\xrightarrow{a}_{\mathsf{prog}(\eta)}q'
\end{gathered}}
{S\xrightarrow{\Use_x/a}S'} .
\end{equation}
\(S'=\mathsf{Advance}(S,x,a,q')\) atomically residualizes the exact occurrence in \(T\),
appends \(a\) to \(\chi\), advances \(\nu\), and stores \(q'\).  For \Fresh it
appends the immutable labeled receipt to \(\Delta\) and enqueues the effect.
For \Alias it does not touch
\(\Delta\) and binds the occurrence to the cell's durable result future.
It also records the first durable progress through an open choice.  Thus
history, program state, receipt, result binding, and outbox cannot be torn apart
by a crash.

Request processing is total and ordered.  An already-resolved \(x\) is a
\Retry only when \(\mathsf{VerifyRetry}(h,x,j,d,\chi,\Delta)\) authenticates its
invocation capability in the \(\chi\)-to-receipt link.  A valid retry returns the linked stable
future/result without a transition.  An unresolved \(x\) instead takes
\cref{eq:use-rule} exactly when all premises hold.  A failed retry check or use
premise, including a missing transition, bad handle, unbound occurrence,
disabled branch, or closed epoch, returns \(\mathsf{deny}(x)\) without changing
state.  \Dispatch releases only the oldest outbox item.  Settlement and crash
preserve the security state.

\paragraph{Atomic installation}
At commit time the kernel ignores caller-supplied target or epoch data.  It
re-runs
\[
\Sigma_\zeta;H\vdash u\Downarrow
 (H',\mathcal C,\eta^-,\eta^+)
\]
over the current history and checks the following.  Here
\(\mathsf{ReCut}(S,u,Z)\) recomputes the right-hand side of
\cref{eq:cut-seal} from current state, and \(\mathsf{Verify}_Z\) includes
the program/seal checks stated above.
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:install-premise}
\begin{gathered}
 \kappa=\kappa_Z,\qquad
 \mathsf{ReCut}(S,u,Z)=\mathsf{cut}_Z,\\
 \mathsf{Verify}_Z(W^\dagger_{H,u},B^\dagger_{H,u},
    \widehat B^\dagger_{H,u}),\\
 \mathsf{Admit}(H,u,\Delta,\mathcal A),\\
 H.\mathsf{epoch}=\eta^-,\qquad
 \mathsf{phase}(\eta^+)=\mathsf{inactive}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
If all checks hold, one domain transaction verifies that every target binding
in \(H'\) names a fresh gate owned by \(\eta^+\) and applies
\[
\begin{aligned}
 H&:=H',&
 \mathsf{phase}(\eta^-)&:=\mathsf{closed},\\
 \mathsf{phase}(\eta^+)&:=\mathsf{active},&
 \mathsf{bind}|_{X_{\sem{T'}}}&:=\beta',\\
 \mathsf{prog}(\eta^+)&:=\mathsf{prog}_Z,&
 \mathsf q(\eta^+)&:=q^0,\\
 \mathsf{cert}(\eta^+)&:=
   (H_c,u,W^\dagger,\widehat B^\dagger,Z).
\end{aligned}
\]
All unrelated inactive/closed records are preserved, and no stale binding can
pass the current-epoch guard.  This successful step is observed as
\(\mathsf{Edge}(u)\).  Failure is an internal stutter with no state change.

\paragraph{Remaining rules}
\(\mathsf{Preload}(\eta,Z)\) is a \(\tau\)-step that may change only an
inactive, unbound epoch.  Failed installation is a state-preserving
\(\tau\)-step.  Authenticated retry and result delivery are \(\tau\)-steps
that return the linked stable future without changing the security core.
Settlement monotonically fills that future, and crash is a security-state
self-loop.  \(\Dispatch(d)\) is enabled only for the outbox head and marks
exactly that entry released.  These clauses, together with the total denial
rule above, make the compiled LTS transition relation explicit.

Both frontier comparisons are semantic, not merely defensive metadata.  If
an old use linearizes first, either \(\Delta\) or
\(\chi\) changes and the compiled seal is stale.  If installation linearizes
first, the current-active-epoch premise of \cref{eq:use-rule} fails for every
old binding.
Pre-cut progress cannot be absent from the proved state yet commit on the
post-cut side.

\begin{lemma}[History-cut linearization]
\label{lem:cut-linearization}
In every reachable well-formed compiled state, a successful \Install and a
protected use in the same policy domain have a unique order.  If
the use commits first, that seal cannot install.  If \Install commits first,
the old use cannot resolve fresh or alias.  Steps in independent domains
commute by product semantics.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The use and installation rules are serializable transactions over the same
history and epoch records.  A fresh use changes
\((\Delta,\chi,\nu)\).  An alias changes \((\chi,\nu)\).  Since
\(\mathsf{cut}_Z\) authenticates \(H\) and \(\Delta\), re-derivation after
either use disagrees with the seal.  In the other order, \Install closes the
old policy-domain epoch before activating \(\eta^+\), so the current-active-epoch
premise of \cref{eq:use-rule} is false for every old binding.  Disjoint cells,
logs, and policies give the product case.  Atomic persistence of \(H'\), the
target bindings, \(q^0\), and the checked active certificate restores the
kernel invariant.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Decidability and trust}

The checker can enumerate indexed linearizations for small contracts or
construct their asynchronous product with the policy automaton, then iterate
\(\widehat\Phi\) at most \(|\widehat B_0|\) strict-removal rounds.  Parallel
pomsets make the worst case exponential.  The result is finite decidability for
bounded protected slices, not a polynomial claim.  The trusted computing base
is the history, protected-call, and edit-schema registries, ledger and atomic
rules, policy issuer, certificate verification, and complete mediation.
Compiler search, automaton minimization, and preloading remain outside it
because \(\mathsf{Verify}_Z\) and \Install re-establish their claims.
