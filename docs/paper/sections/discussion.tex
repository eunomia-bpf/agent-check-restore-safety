\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Scope}
Snapshots cannot retract external effects or receipts, and total rollback is
wrong when histories remain live.  The problem extends to workflows,
deployments, notebooks, search, and RL; agents make it routine by constructing
the graph online around external tools.  Edge-local certificates compose until
receipt growth forces readmission.  Choose-one hermetic rollouts can defer
protected authority until winner selection; rollouts that commit externally
in parallel or join durable effects require admission at the corresponding
history edge.

\paragraph{Assumptions and nonclaims}
Atomic redemption protects a cell; joint control protects correlations.
$\mathsf{RawPhysical}$ composes declared controllers, so hidden coordination may
reject safely whereas an unmodeled bypass is unsound.  $\Gamma$ binds one
co-durable epoch: receipt growth or controller/version change requires a new
decision.  Lean proves the finite manifest theorem; unverified Python
recomputes full $\Gamma$, consumes no prior seal, and treats coverage as
attested.  Truthful evidence, mediation, freshness, and redemption remain
runtime obligations.  The contract-only arity is worst-case tight, though
concrete $E,L$ or proved structure may lower it.  The seal neither authorizes
effects nor proves exactly-once, liveness, reward correctness, or safe learning.

\section{Conclusion}

History admission derives the correlated future of Fork, Restore, or Merge,
conditions it on the durable past, normalizes redemption cells separately from
controllers, and computes the co-liveness observation arity sufficient for its
contract; an arbitrary-$k$ family shows this bound worst-case tight.  It issues
a history-admission seal only when
$\mathsf{Required}\subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}
\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}$; sound runtime use additionally requires
$\mathsf{Required}\subseteq\mathsf{Actual}
\subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}$.
