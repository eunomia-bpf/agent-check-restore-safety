\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{When history admission matters}
If every protected effect can synchronously consult one atomic service, the
reference monitor is already safe and maximally permissive one commit at a
time.  History admission is useful when a runtime wants to detach authority,
avoid a global round trip, reserve a multi-effect outcome in advance, or
promise that a parallel/join continuation remains executable.  A choose-one
Agent may instead retain authority at its parent until selection.

\paragraph{Trusted and effect boundary}
Stable keys and installer-minted identifiers must determine the two
quotients; the edge constructor must derive its target relation; and actual
controllers must refine the manifest.  A production installer must bind the
certificate atomically to the observed receipts and epoch, closing the old
epoch.  The prototype establishes none of these facts.  External delivery
additionally needs an outbox, idempotent/queryable sink, or conservative
issue-time charging.  We prove neither exactly-once effects nor that an
authorized Agent action is useful, truthful, confidential, or aligned.

\paragraph{Finite representation}
The compiler enumerates small families and fails closed at fixed caps.  Exact
projection removes irrelevant high-arity behavior, but no uniformly
low-order summary covers every policy.

\paragraph{Ethics}
The artifact uses synthetic manifests and finite models, recruits no users,
and releases no private histories.  Certificates decline to authorize effects.

\section{Conclusion}

Agent history admission answers when Fork, Restore, or Merge may replace
per-effect global-monitor calls with an installed controller epoch.  Exactly
when the epoch contains every required endpoint and no trace outside the
monitor language, it is both transparent and monitor-sound.  The same
characterization exposes necessary coordination and the edge, receipt,
identity, and co-liveness evidence an exact compiler cannot generally erase.
