\section{Admission and Refinement Results}
\label{sec:results}

The results form a refinement chain rather than one monolithic safety theorem.
A typed operation generates a correlated future; the durable prefix filters
that future; a controller manifest overapproximates what can be physically
combined; and a runtime must refine that manifest:
\[
\begin{gathered}
  op\longmapsto(\mathsf{Candidate},\mathsf{Required})
    \longmapsto\mathsf{Admitted},\\[-2pt]
  \mathsf{Required}\subseteq\mathsf{Actual}
    \subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}.
\end{gathered}
\]
All results below are mechanized for finite carrier types.  For compactness,
write
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \mathsf{PCM}(\mathcal A,D,\mathcal F,\ell)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad{}&D\in\mathcal A\\[-2pt]
  &{}\land \forall X\in\mathcal F.\ \mathsf{PrefixOK}(X).
\end{aligned}
\]
This is the family-level prefix configuration morphism used by the Lean
development.  Its premises are statements about the supplied finite model,
not proofs that an adapter has described a real runtime completely.

\subsection{Typed generation and the greatest semantic residual}

The first result rules out caller-chosen target families that already encode
the desired conclusion.  The operation type determines whether its two arm
contracts remain alternatives or may become durable jointly.

\begin{theorem}[Typed-family generation]
\label{thm:typed-generation}
For every valid instance of the six operators in \cref{sec:semantics}, both
$\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$ and $\mathsf{Required}_{op}$ are nonempty,
contain $\varnothing$, and are downward closed.  Moreover,
\[
  \mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The proof is by the operator grammar.  Union preserves well-formed exclusive
choice.  Tensor preserves downward closure by restricting each arm witness to
the desired subconfiguration.  The required-subset result then follows from
monotonicity of union and tensor.  It does not validate the adapter-supplied
leaf contracts; their validity is a premise.

\begin{theorem}[Greatest fixed-frontier residual]
\label{thm:greatest-residual}
Assume $D\in\mathcal A$ and fix $op$, $D$, $\ell$, and
$\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$.  The family $\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$ from
\cref{eq:admitted-future} satisfies
\begin{align*}
  &\mathsf{PCM}(\mathcal A,D,\mathsf{Admitted}_{op},\ell),\\[-2pt]
  &\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}.
\end{align*}
For every $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$,
\[
  \mathsf{PCM}(\mathcal A,D,\mathcal H,\ell)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \mathcal H\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  &\mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}\\[-2pt]
  &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \exists\mathcal H.\quad
    \mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathcal H\\[-2pt]
  &\hspace{26mm}{}\land
    \mathcal H\subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}\\[-2pt]
  &\hspace{26mm}{}\land
    \mathsf{PCM}(\mathcal A,D,\mathcal H,\ell).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

Thus the filter is not merely one safe repair: it is the greatest repair in
the fixed-candidate, fixed-prefix, fixed-lineage, pruning-only space.  The
family condition also has an exact policy interpretation.  When
$\mathcal A$ is well formed and the target family contains $\varnothing$,
$\mathsf{PCM}$ is equivalent to preservation of every natural-number additive
capacity policy after adding the already durable atoms $D$.  This is a
characterization, not an assumption that a deployed policy is presented as a
numeric vector: the family side retains arbitrary downward-closed
correlations.

\begin{theorem}[Four-way semantic classification]
\label{thm:semantic-classifier}
Given a valid typed operation and an old admitted envelope, the classifier has
the following sound interpretation:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=*,style=nextline]
  \item[$\mathsf{Inherit}$]
    A structural refinement transports the old certificate.
  \item[$\mathsf{ReadmitOK}$]
    No such refinement is supplied, but
    $\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}=\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$.
  \item[$\mathsf{NeedsMechanism}$]
    $\mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}
    \subsetneq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$.
  \item[$\mathsf{Reject}$]
    No $\mathcal H$ between $\mathsf{Required}_{op}$ and
    $\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$ satisfies
    $\mathsf{PCM}(\mathcal A,D,\mathcal H,\ell)$.
\end{description}
The three underlying semantic classes---complete admission, mechanism-needed
pruning, and pruning rejection---are exhaustive and pairwise exclusive;
$\mathsf{Inherit}$ and $\mathsf{ReadmitOK}$ split complete admission according
to certificate provenance.  In the rejection case, some configuration in
$\mathsf{Required}_{op}\setminus\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$ is a witness.
\end{theorem}

In particular, $\mathsf{NeedsMechanism}$ is not a permissive result.  It says
exactly that the required contract is preservable if the runtime first
installs a restriction realizing an admitted subfamily and then submits that
new realization for admission.

\subsection{Structural inheritance composes locally}

Full filtering is unnecessary when a target is structurally related to an
already admitted envelope at the same receipt frontier.  The certificate must
account for every target configuration rather than merely each cell in
isolation.

\begin{theorem}[Structural transport and composition]
\label{thm:structural-transport}
Let $p$ map target cells into the cells of an admitted envelope.  If $p$ is
injective on every target configuration, maps every such configuration into
the old future, commutes with lineage, and advances the envelope version, then
\[
  \mathsf{PCM}(\mathcal A,D,\mathsf{Candidate}_{op},\ell)
\]
is transported from the old certificate.  If a second refinement satisfies
the same obligations into the first target, the two refinements compose,
including version order, configuration injectivity, and the lineage square.
\end{theorem}

This theorem supports an execution graph that is discovered online: each
Fork, Restore, or Merge edge can carry a local refinement, and a path
certificate is obtained by composition without declaring the eventual graph
in advance.
It deliberately preserves $D$ definitionally.  If a new receipt grows the
frontier to $D'$, the theorem supplies no certificate for $D'$; admission must
be rerun.  In particular, configurations containing a restored cell whose
lineage atom has entered the new durable prefix are removed even when its
snapshot-only parent map still exists.

\subsection{Exact controller-readiness boundary}

Semantic admission does not imply that independently live controllers preserve
the admitted correlations.  The next result characterizes the additional
manifest-level condition.

Let $\mathsf{MNF}(\mathcal F)$ denote the minimal nonfaces of a downward-closed
family $\mathcal F$: forbidden configurations on its support whose every proper
subset belongs to $\mathcal F$.
In this subsection, abbreviate $\mathcal R=\mathsf{Required}_{op}$,
$\mathcal F=\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$, and
$\mathcal P=\mathsf{RawPhysical}$.  Write $\mathsf{NoEscape}$ when every
$C\in\mathcal P$ is contained in $\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal F)$, and
$\mathsf{NoMNF}$ when no $K\in\mathsf{MNF}(\mathcal F)$ belongs to
$\mathcal P$.

\begin{theorem}[Deployment readiness by complete obstructions]
\label{thm:deployment-obstructions}
If every controller-local family $L_g$ is downward closed, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \mathsf{DeploymentReady}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad{}&
  \mathcal R\subseteq\mathcal P\\[-2pt]
  &{}\land\mathsf{NoEscape}\land\mathsf{NoMNF}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Without the downward-closure premise, readiness still has the exact failure
decomposition
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \neg\mathsf{DeploymentReady}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad{}&
  \mathcal P\setminus\mathcal F\neq\varnothing\\[-2pt]
  &{}\lor\ \mathcal R\setminus\mathcal P\neq\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both differences may be nonempty simultaneously.
\end{theorem}

The first obstruction prevents the raw product from escaping the modeled
support; the second is complete for correlations of arbitrary arity, not only
pairwise conflicts.  The result also embeds the older functional-partition
criterion as a special case.  For a well-formed admitted family, the canonical
functional cell-to-controller adapter's raw cover product is extensionally the
maximal asynchronous local product.  Its readiness condition reduces to
\[
  \mathcal R\subseteq\mathcal F
  \quad\land\quad
  \mathsf{blockOf}\text{ is constant on }\mathsf{MustCoordinate},
\]
where $\mathsf{MustCoordinate}$ is the equivalence closure generated by
co-membership in a minimal nonface.  This reduction does not assert a unique
finest layout for arbitrary overlapping access relations.

\subsection{Honest negative evidence and higher-order GateCuts}

The set differences above explain whether readiness fails, but a controller
repair needs a mechanism-level witness.  Such a witness must not call every
forbidden union a coordination failure.

\begin{theorem}[Cover-level failure classification]
\label{thm:cover-triage}
Fix one valid cover plan $(G_0,(X_g)_{g\in G_0})$ whose union
$C=\bigcup_{g\in G_0}X_g$ is not admitted.  Exactly one of the following holds
for this plan:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=*,style=nextline]
  \item[$\mathsf{OutsideSupport}$]
    $C\nsubseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mathsf{Admitted}_{op})$;
  \item[$\mathsf{LocalOverpermission}$]
    $C$ stays on the admitted support, but some $X_g$ is not admitted;
  \item[$\mathsf{CorrelationCut}$]
    $C$ stays on the admitted support and every $X_g$ is admitted, but their
    co-live union is not.
\end{description}
\end{theorem}

The cases are exhaustive and pairwise exclusive only after fixing the cover
plan.  A bad physical configuration may have several covers, so the theorem
does not claim one globally unique cause.  Only the third case attributes the
failure to controller correlation.  The executable compiler may further label
a correlation witness $\mathsf{GateClone}$ using declared origin metadata;
the Lean theorem establishes the underlying $\mathsf{CorrelationCut}$ and does
not mechanize provenance truth.

\begin{theorem}[Physical GateCut extraction]
\label{thm:gatecut-extraction}
Assume the local families are downward closed and locally sound: each declared
local choice is accessible through its controller and belongs to
$\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$.  Every support-contained physical overpermission
$C$ contains a configuration $K\subseteq C$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item $K$ is a minimal nonface of $\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$;
  \item $K$ is realized by a valid restriction of the same physical cover; and
  \item every restricted controller-local piece is admitted.
\end{enumerate}
For a well-formed admitted family, such a GateCut necessarily has at least two
distinct active controllers with nonempty contributions.
\end{theorem}

The arity of $K$ matters.  In the mechanized $U(2,3)$ fixture,
\[
  \mathcal U_{2,3}=\{X\subseteq\{d_0,d_1,d_2\}\mid |X|\leq2\}.
\]
Every pair is admitted, while the triple is a minimal nonface.  Two co-live
controllers, one choosing subsets of $\{d_0\}$ and the other choosing subsets
of $\{d_1,d_2\}$, each have a locally sound family; together they physically
realize the forbidden triple.  Thus a graph built only from forbidden pairs
has no edge, yet a real GateCut exists.  The fixture also lets both controllers
access every cell, showing that redemption-cell identity alone does not
determine the controller correlation boundary.

\subsection{Runtime refinement transports the safety certificate}

The compiler checks $\mathsf{RawPhysical}$, not an unobserved runtime family.
The final theorem therefore keeps the deployment assumptions explicit.

\begin{theorem}[Runtime prefix-certificate transport]
\label{thm:runtime-transport}
Suppose the manifest is deployment-ready, the admitted family has a durable
prefix certificate, and the runtime establishes
\[
  \mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Actual}
  \subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}.
\]
Then
\[
  \mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Actual}
  \subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}
\]
and
\[
  \mathsf{PCM}(\mathcal A,D,\mathsf{Actual},\ell).
\]
\end{theorem}

The proof is inclusion composition followed by monotonicity of the target
family in $\mathsf{PCM}$.  Its importance is the boundary: the theory tells a
runtime exactly which two refinement obligations suffice, but the compiler
does not prove truthful manifests, complete mediation, or either inclusion
involving $\mathsf{Actual}$.

\subsection{A limited durable-commitment substrate}

The remaining question is why $D$ can be treated as a monotone prefix.  We use
a separate operational model for that fact rather than placing
Fork/Restore/Merge inside the receipt protocol.

\begin{theorem}[Monotone commitment core]
\label{thm:commitment-core}
Starting from a safe commitment state, every closed step---fresh \Prepare{},
Replay, Retry, Crash, or Settle---preserves the commitment invariant.
The receipt ledger is append-only, and external phase either stutters or moves
from prepared to settled.  A fresh \Prepare{} adds exactly one committed
receipt; every other label adds zero.  Hence, over a finite trace, committed
receipt cardinality grows by exactly the number of fresh labels, and the set of
qualified atoms named by the ledger is monotone.
\end{theorem}

This is enough to supply successive durable frontiers to admission.  Its scope
is intentionally smaller than the end-to-end agent claim: Crash is an atomic
controller stutter, topology-changing history operations require the separate
refinement results above, and no theorem here establishes exactly-once effects
at an external sink.
