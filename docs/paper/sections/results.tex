\section{Exact Agent History Realization}
\label{sec:results}

We now join the structural, language, and durable levels in one result.
Let \(I_0,S_0\) be full bootstrap states over the same well-formed durable
core and admitted initial cut: both have empty resolved prefix and identical
history, receipts, outbox, and target bindings; \(S_0\) additionally has the
verified program at \(q^0\), with exactly the target generation active.
Write \(\mathsf{Ideal}(I_0)\) for the atomic history machine of
\cref{sec:ideal}, and \(\mathsf{Compiled}(S_0)\) for the kernel of
\cref{sec:kernel} with arbitrary internal compilation, preloading, failed
installs, retries, settlement, and crashes.

The observable alphabet is
\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal O=\{&
 \mathsf{Edge}(u),\Fresh(x,d),\Alias(x,d),\\[-2pt]
 &\mathsf{deny}(x),\Dispatch(d)\}.
\end{split}
\]
Compiler/preload steps, failed stale installs, retries, settlement, result
delivery, and crash recovery are \(\tau\).
This choice observes logical alias progress even though its authority
projection is empty.
The observation projection maps a successful
\(\mathsf{Use}(x,j,h)/a\) to \(a\), a successful \Install to
\(\mathsf{Edge}(u)\), and invalid use and head release to
\(\mathsf{deny}(x)\) and \(\Dispatch(d)\), respectively.

\begin{theorem}[Exact Agent History Realization]
\label{thm:exact-history}
Let \(I_0,S_0\) be bootstrap states as above and assume the trusted boundary
of \cref{sec:model}.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
 \item \textbf{Exact local realizability.}  For every reachable well-formed
 security core \((\kappa,H,\Delta,\mathsf{out},\mathcal A)\) and operation
 with a trusted derivation \(H\vdash u\Downarrow(\cdots)\), the following are
 equivalent:
 \[
 \begin{aligned}
  &\mathsf{Admit}(H,u);\\
  &\exists (L,B).\ (L,B)\text{ realizes }(H,u);\\
  &\mathsf{Compile}(H,u,\Delta)\text{ emits a verifiable seal}.
 \end{aligned}
 \]
 If they hold, the compiled resolved-event language is exactly \(W_{H,u}\),
 its marked completions are \(B^\star_{H,u}\), and every realization is
 componentwise contained in this pair.  If they fail, a returned
 outcome \(o\) with \(M_o(H,u)=\varnothing\) proves that no realization exists for the
 fixed history, policy, log, and registered contracts.

 \item \textbf{Global implementation equivalence.}
 \[
   \mathsf{Compiled}(S_0)
   \ \approx_{\mathcal O}\
   \mathsf{Ideal}(I_0)
 \]
 by weak bisimulation.  The equivalence covers arbitrary finite or infinite
 interleavings of all six history operations, fresh commitments, logical
 aliases, retries, ordered dispatch, settlement, and crashes.
\end{enumerate}

Consequently, at every reachable prefix: the authority trace satisfies
\(\mathcal A\); every active cut remains completion-nonblocking; every live
gate has one authoritative owner; a closed generation cannot make a fresh or
alias transition; each logical occurrence linearizes on exactly one side of a
successful history cut; and released effects follow fresh receipt order.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Local equivalence.}
\Cref{lem:greatest-realization} proves
\(\mathsf{Admit}\) iff a realization exists and identifies its greatest
language \(W\).  On admission, finiteness of the contracts makes \(W\) finite,
so \cref{lem:compiled-language} emits an exact program and the checker accepts
its symmetric-difference and cut-digest checks.  Conversely, a verifiable
seal includes outcome coverage, \(B^\star\), and an exact program for \(W\), hence witnesses
a realization.  If admission fails, any realization would have to cover the
reported \(o\), contradicting \(M_o=\varnothing\).

\emph{Bisimulation relation.}
Relate an ideal state \(I\) and compiled state \(S\) when:
(i) \(\kappa,H,\Delta\), all receipt phases, and the ordered outbox agree;
(ii) their active cut and resolved prefix are
\((W,B^\star),r\), and the compiled program state is
\((W/r,B^\star/r)\);
(iii) \(\beta\) equals the kernel's gate/occurrence binding, every designated
live owner is the unique active generation, and no closed source owns a live
gate; and
(iv) the ordered occurrence/receipt invariant following
\cref{eq:use-rule} holds.  The bootstrap construction satisfies this
relation.

\emph{Protected occurrences.}
Suppose \(x\) is enabled.  Equal \(H\), registry data, and \(\Delta\) compute
the same resolved label \(a\).  The compiled program has an \(a\)-transition
iff \(ra\in W\) by \cref{lem:compiled-language}, exactly the ideal guard.
Both atomic steps residualize the same occurrence, append \(a\) to \(\chi\),
advance the view and residual state, and, only for \Fresh, append the same
receipt and outbox item.  The successor states are related.  Disabled,
misbound, or closed calls produce the same denial.  This proves both
simulation directions for \Fresh, \Alias, and denials.

\emph{History edges.}
For an ideal \(\mathsf{Edge}(u)\), determinism of
\cref{lem:history-derivation} gives one \(H'\), contract, source closure, and
target.  Local realizability lets the compiled side take internal
compile/check/preload steps and then a successful \Install.  Its transaction
persists the same \(H'\), closes exactly the derived owners, and starts the
exact program at \((W/\epsilon,B^\star/\epsilon)\); the successors are
related.
Conversely, every successful install re-derives the same judgment and verifies
\(\mathsf{Admit}\), so the ideal edge exists and matches it.

If a protected occurrence wins before installation, a fresh step changes
\(\Delta,\chi,\nu\) and an alias changes \(\chi,\nu\); in either case the old
digest fails and the install stutters.  If installation wins, every old
affected owner is closed, so an old use has no transition.  Thus every
fresh/alias/install race is covered without an intermediate unmatched state.
This is exactly \cref{lem:cut-linearization}; its source-isolation case also
shows that independent scopes preserve the relation.

\emph{Remaining transitions.}
Both machines dispatch the same outbox head.  Retry, result delivery,
settlement, failed install, and crash recovery preserve the relation through
matching \(\tau\)-steps.
The relation is re-established at \(\epsilon\) after every successful edge,
so coinduction covers an arbitrary edge sequence.  Policy safety and
completion nonblocking follow from membership in \(W\); owner and
closed-generation facts follow from the relation invariant.  Atomic ordering
of \cref{eq:use-rule} and \Install assigns each occurrence to one cut side,
and FIFO dispatch gives the final claim.
\end{proof}

The theorem identifies a security boundary that does not exist for a
single immutable execution: an Agent may rewrite a live logical past exactly
when every outcome induced by that rewrite remains realizable, and the same
criterion can be enforced while effects and history cuts race.  The residual
construction and durable kernel are witnesses for this one history-realization
theory, from typed Fork/Restore/Merge semantics to occurrence-level effects.

\subsection{Why the boundary needs both histories}

The sufficient cut observation is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cut-observation}
 \alpha(H,u)=
 (\mathcal C_{H,u},\rho,\beta,
  \Delta,\chi,\Src_{H,u},\kappa,\nu).
\end{equation}
This tuple is a sufficient semantic observation, not a claim that one literal
encoding is componentwise minimal.  Its distinctions have independent roles.
Causal order distinguishes \(a\prec b\) from \(b\prec a\) under a policy
admitting only \(ab\); history mode distinguishes \(a\oplus b\) from
\(a\otimes b\) under one-use authority.  Cell identity distinguishes two
copied occurrences of one effect from two effects.  The receipt frontier
decides whether the next occurrence is fresh, whereas the ordered
logical-occurrence frontier records receipt-silent alias progress through a
choice or join.  Finally, owner bindings determine which still-live
generations must lose eligibility at the cut.  These examples explain why a
workspace hash, receipt log, or controller epoch alone cannot implement the
theorem; an implementation may encode the same information differently.

\subsection{What rejection means}

A witness \(o\) is not a compiler timeout or a suggested branch to delete.
It identifies a pomset forced by the typed history operation whose every causal
linearization violates the fixed durable residual.  Changing the operation,
policy grant, checkpoint, or registered leaf contract creates a new problem
instance.  With those inputs fixed, restricting runtime behavior cannot
restore outcome coverage.  This exact negative direction is what permits
history admission to serve as an API boundary rather than a best-effort
planner.
