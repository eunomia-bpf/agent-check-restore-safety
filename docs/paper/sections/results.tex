\section{A Contract-Indexed Evidence Boundary}
\label{sec:results}

The paper's main claim is the following evidence theorem.  Subsequent results
derive its inputs from a typed history edge, prove sufficiency, and construct
the two independent lower bounds.

\begin{theorem}[Exact manifest-relative view and uniform lower bounds]
\label{cor:evidence-boundary}
Let a valid typed history edge and durable receipt prefix derive finite
normalized families $(\mathcal R,\mathcal F)$, and fix access $E$ and
downward-closed local families $L$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item for any downward-closed $\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2$, equality of
  $\Pi_{\leq r(\mathcal R,\mathcal F)}(\Gamma_1)$ and
  $\Pi_{\leq r(\mathcal R,\mathcal F)}(\Gamma_2)$ implies identical
  structural-readiness Booleans;
  \item at the raw-manifest stage, erasing either the handle-to-cell or the
  gate-use-to-controller quotient admits a pair with the same raw fields and
  retained quotient but opposite readiness decisions; and
  \item for every $k\geq1$, there is a fixed normalized
  $(\mathcal R,\mathcal F,E,L)$ and two epoch families with the same
  $\Pi_{\leq k}$ but opposite decisions, where
  $r(\mathcal R,\mathcal F)=k+1$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus $(\mathcal R,\mathcal F,E,L,\Pi_{\leq r}(\Gamma))$ determines the exact
manifest-relative Boolean after normalization; the identity results state
what cannot uniformly be erased before that normalized view is derived.  This
is neither a runtime-safety theorem without refinement nor a claim that the
view is a unique or pointwise-minimal encoding.
\end{theorem}

If a ready manifest is faithfully realized by the runtime, its certificate
then transports along
\[
\begin{gathered}
  op\longmapsto(\mathsf{Candidate},\mathsf{Required})
    \longmapsto\mathsf{Admitted},\\[-2pt]
  \mathsf{Required}\subseteq\mathsf{Actual}
    \subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}.
\end{gathered}
\]
All results below are mechanized for finite carrier types.  For compactness,
write
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \mathsf{PCM}(\mathcal A,D,\mathcal F,\ell)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad{}&D\in\mathcal A\\[-2pt]
  &{}\land \forall X\in\mathcal F.\ \mathsf{PrefixOK}(X).
\end{aligned}
\]
This is the family-level prefix configuration morphism used by the Lean
development.  Its premises are statements about the supplied finite model,
not proofs that an adapter has described a real runtime completely.

\subsection{Typed generation and greatest admitted future}

The first result rules out caller-chosen target families that already encode
the desired conclusion.  The operation type determines whether its two arm
contracts remain alternatives or may become durable jointly.

\begin{theorem}[Typed-family generation]
\label{thm:typed-generation}
For every valid instance of the six runtime labels---two choice, three tensor,
and one replacement---in \cref{sec:semantics}, both
$\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$ and $\mathsf{Required}_{op}$ are nonempty,
contain $\varnothing$, and are downward closed.  Moreover,
\[
  \mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The proof is by the operator grammar.  Union preserves well-formed exclusive
choice.  Tensor preserves downward closure by restricting each arm witness to
the desired subconfiguration.  The required-subset result then follows from
monotonicity of union and tensor.  It does not validate the adapter-supplied
leaf contracts; their validity is a premise.

\begin{theorem}[Greatest fixed-prefix admitted future]
\label{thm:greatest-residual}
Assume $D\in\mathcal A$ and fix $op$, $D$, $\ell$, and
$\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$.  The family $\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$ from
\cref{eq:admitted-future} satisfies
\begin{align*}
  &\mathsf{PCM}(\mathcal A,D,\mathsf{Admitted}_{op},\ell),\\[-2pt]
  &\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}.
\end{align*}
For every $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}$,
\[
  \mathsf{PCM}(\mathcal A,D,\mathcal H,\ell)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \mathcal H\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  &\mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}\\[-2pt]
  &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \exists\mathcal H.\quad
    \mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathcal H\\[-2pt]
  &\hspace{26mm}{}\land
    \mathcal H\subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}\\[-2pt]
  &\hspace{26mm}{}\land
    \mathsf{PCM}(\mathcal A,D,\mathcal H,\ell).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

Thus the admitted future is greatest in the fixed-candidate, fixed-prefix,
fixed-lineage, pruning-only space; its downward-closed family retains
correlations rather than assuming a rectangular numeric capacity policy.

\subsection{Exact controller-readiness boundary}

Semantic admission does not imply that independently live controllers preserve
the admitted correlations.  First, both identity maps carry independently
necessary information for an exact decision.

\begin{proposition}[Independent identity erasures]
\label{prop:identity-erasure}
There is a fixed two-handle, two-gate raw manifest $M$ and collapsing/identity
maps $q_{\mathrm{cell}}^{=},q_{\mathrm{cell}}^{\ne}$ and
$q_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{=},q_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{\ne}$ such that all four normalized
worlds are well structured and, for $x,y\in\{=,\ne\}$,
\[
  \mathsf{Ready}_{M}(q_{\mathrm{cell}}^{x},q_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^{y})
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x={=}\ \lor\ y={=}.
\]
An observation retaining $M$ and either quotient while erasing the other thus
equates a ready world with the nonready $(\ne,\ne)$ world.  Hence no exact
structural-readiness decider on this manifest class factors through either
erasure.
\end{proposition}
The fixture is concrete.  Its raw future permits either of two handles but
not their pair; raw gate use $j_i$ may choose nothing or its matching handle,
and every subset of the two uses is co-live.  If the handles alias, the
normalized future and product are the powerset of one cell, regardless of
controller identity.  If the gate uses share one controller, their
union-normalized local family retains the two alternatives, regardless of
cell identity.  Only when both handles and controllers are distinct does the
physical product contain the forbidden pair.  The four decisions are
therefore ready, ready, ready, and nonready.
This is information irredundancy, not uniqueness of representation: a
fail-closed checker may reject both worlds, and equivalent evidence may replace
either literal map.  The result is manifest-relative and assumes atomic
redemption.  Its model-level fixture unions gate-use families folded to one
controller; the prototype accepts the stricter subclass whose same-anchor uses
already agree on normalized $L_g$.

Let $\mathsf{MNF}(\mathcal F)$ denote the minimal nonfaces of a downward-closed
family $\mathcal F$: forbidden configurations on its support whose every proper
subset belongs to $\mathcal F$.
In this subsection, abbreviate $\mathcal R=\mathsf{Required}_{op}$,
$\mathcal F=\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$, and
$\mathcal P=\mathsf{RawPhysical}$.  Write $\mathsf{NoEscape}$ when every
$C\in\mathcal P$ is contained in $\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal F)$, and
$\mathsf{NoMNF}$ when no $K\in\mathsf{MNF}(\mathcal F)$ belongs to
$\mathcal P$.

\begin{theorem}[Deployment readiness by complete obstructions]
\label{thm:deployment-obstructions}
If every controller-local family $L_g$ is downward closed, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \mathsf{DeploymentReady}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad{}&
  \mathcal R\subseteq\mathcal P\\[-2pt]
  &{}\land\mathsf{NoEscape}\land\mathsf{NoMNF}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Without the downward-closure premise, readiness still has the exact failure
decomposition
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \neg\mathsf{DeploymentReady}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad{}&
  \mathcal P\setminus\mathcal F\neq\varnothing\\[-2pt]
  &{}\lor\ \mathcal R\setminus\mathcal P\neq\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both differences may be nonempty simultaneously.
\end{theorem}

The first obstruction prevents the raw product from escaping the modeled
support; the second is complete for correlations of arbitrary arity, not only
pairwise conflicts.  For the canonical functional cell-to-controller adapter,
this also recovers the older partition criterion: required behavior is
admitted and controller blocks are constant on the equivalence closure
generated by co-membership in a minimal nonface.  It asserts no unique finest
layout for arbitrary overlapping access relations.

\paragraph{Greatest co-liveness pruning}
For fixed $E,L$, write
$\mathsf{Raw}(\Delta)=\mathsf{RawPhysical}(E,L,\Delta)$ and define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:principal-coliveness-repair}
  \Gamma^{\star}_{\mathcal F}
  =\{G\in\Gamma\mid\mathsf{Raw}(2^G)\subseteq\mathcal F\}.
\end{equation}
The powerset makes group safety survive controller removal.

\begin{proposition}[Greatest co-liveness pruning]
\label{prop:principal-coliveness-repair}
If $\Gamma$ is downward closed, so is
$\Gamma^{\star}_{\mathcal F}\subseteq\Gamma$, and
$\mathsf{Raw}(\Gamma^{\star}_{\mathcal F})\subseteq\mathcal F$.
Every downward-closed $\Delta\subseteq\Gamma$ with
$\mathsf{Raw}(\Delta)\subseteq\mathcal F$ satisfies
$\Delta\subseteq\Gamma^{\star}_{\mathcal F}$.  Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R\subseteq\mathsf{Raw}(\Gamma^{\star}_{\mathcal F})
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists\Delta\subseteq\Gamma.\;&\Delta\text{ is downward closed}\\[-2pt]
 &{}\land\mathcal R\subseteq\mathsf{Raw}(\Delta)\subseteq\mathcal F.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proposition}

With $\mathcal F=\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$,
\cref{thm:greatest-residual} makes these two filters greatest among repairs
that first prune the candidate prefix-safely and then prune submitted $\Gamma$
at fixed $E,L$.  Losing required behavior therefore proves this restricted
repair space infeasible.  When the executable receives only
$P=\Pi_{\leq r}(\Gamma)$, the claim is relative to $P$, not hidden full
$\Gamma$.

\subsection{Honest negative evidence and higher-order correlation cuts}

The set differences above explain whether readiness fails, but a controller
repair needs a mechanism-level witness.  Such a witness must not call every
forbidden union a coordination failure.

\begin{theorem}[Cover-level failure classification]
\label{thm:cover-triage}
Fix one valid cover plan $(G_0,(X_g)_{g\in G_0})$ whose union
$C=\bigcup_{g\in G_0}X_g$ is not admitted.  Exactly one of the following holds
for this plan:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=*,style=nextline]
  \item[$\mathsf{OutsideSupport}$]
    $C\nsubseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mathsf{Admitted}_{op})$;
  \item[$\mathsf{LocalOverpermission}$]
    $C$ stays on the admitted support, but some $X_g$ is not admitted;
  \item[$\mathsf{CorrelationCut}$]
    $C$ stays on the admitted support and every $X_g$ is admitted, but their
    co-live union is not.
\end{description}
\end{theorem}

The cases are exhaustive and pairwise exclusive only after fixing the cover
plan.  A bad physical configuration may have several covers, so the theorem
does not claim one globally unique cause.  Only the third case attributes the
failure to controller correlation.  The compiler uses declared origin metadata
to subtype it as $\mathsf{GateClone}$ for one origin or $\mathsf{GateCut}$ for
several; Lean establishes only the underlying $\mathsf{CorrelationCut}$, not
provenance truth.

\begin{theorem}[Physical correlation-cut extraction]
\label{thm:correlation-cut-extraction}
Assume the local families are downward closed and locally sound: each declared
local choice is accessible through its controller and belongs to
$\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$.  Every support-contained physical overpermission
$C$ contains a configuration $K\subseteq C$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item $K$ is a minimal nonface of $\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$;
  \item $K$ is realized by a valid restriction of the same physical cover; and
  \item every restricted controller-local piece is admitted.
\end{enumerate}
For a well-formed admitted family, such a correlation-cut witness necessarily has at least two
distinct active controllers with nonempty contributions.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Contract-indexed observation bound}

Let $\Pi_{\leq k}(\Gamma)$ contain every controller set of size at most $k$
that is contained in some member of $\Gamma$.  For
$\mathcal R=\mathsf{Required}_{op}$ and
$\mathcal F=\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$, define the contract-only bound
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:required-coliveness-arity}
\begin{aligned}
 r(\mathcal R,\mathcal F)=\max\{&\max_{C\in\mathcal R}|C|,
 \max_{K\in\mathsf{MNF}(\mathcal F)}|K|,\\[-2pt]
 &\mathbf 1[\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal F)\ne\mathcal D]\},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with empty maximum zero; the indicator retains unary support escape.

\begin{theorem}[Exact projected admission and worst-case arity lower bound]
\label{thm:observation-arity}
Fix $E,L$ with every $L_g$ downward closed.  Two downward-closed epoch
families $\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2$ with equal
$\Pi_{\leq r(\mathcal R,\mathcal F)}$ have the same readiness decision.
Consequently, taking $P=\Pi_{\leq r(\mathcal R,\mathcal F)}(\Gamma)$, evaluating
$(E,L,P)$ yields the same readiness Boolean as $(E,L,\Gamma)$ without
enumerating higher-order members; optional full-family fidelity may remain
unknown.  Conversely, for every $k\geq1$, two realizations of
$U(k,k{+}1)=\{X\subseteq\{d_0,\ldots,d_k\}:|X|\leq k\}$ share controller
identities, complete access, locally sound singleton families, and
$\Pi_{\leq k}$, yet only the realization excluding joint liveness of all
$k{+}1$ controllers is ready.  Here $r(U,U)=k{+}1$, so no arity-$k$ checker is
exact on all downward-closed realizations.
\end{theorem}

For sufficiency, fix one cover of a configuration $C$.  Choose at most one
contributing controller for each cell of $C$ and restrict every selected local
piece to the cells assigned to it; downward closure yields a cover with at
most $|C|$ controllers.  Hence coverage of each $C\in\mathcal R$ is determined
through arity $|C|$.  If a physical $C$ is not in $\mathcal F$, finiteness and
downward closure expose either one cell outside
$\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal F)$ or a minimal nonface $K\subseteq C$.
Restricting and compressing the same cover yields a witness using at most one
or $|K|$ controllers, respectively.  These are exactly the three maxima in
\cref{eq:required-coliveness-arity}.

For the lower construction, use $k+1$ cells and $k+1$ controllers, with
identity access and singleton local families.  The epoch family
$U(k,k+1)$ and the full powerset have identical $k$-projections; their raw
products are respectively $U(k,k+1)$ and the full powerset, so only the former
is ready for $\mathcal R=\mathcal F=U(k,k+1)$.  The skeleton distinction is
classical~\cite{emtander2010hypergraphs,wang2024hypergraph}; the result here
lifts it through fixed-$E,L$ cover realizability and required coverage.
Concrete $E,L$ may permit less evidence.

\Cref{cor:evidence-boundary} follows by combining
\cref{prop:identity-erasure,thm:deployment-obstructions,thm:observation-arity}.
Equivalent identity evidence may replace either
literal map, restricted $E,L$ may permit lower arity, and a fail-closed checker
may reject with less information.

\subsection{Runtime-refinement certificate transport}

The compiler checks $\mathsf{RawPhysical}$, not an unobserved runtime family.
The final theorem therefore keeps the deployment assumptions explicit.

\begin{theorem}[Runtime prefix-certificate transport]
\label{thm:runtime-transport}
Suppose the manifest is deployment-ready, the admitted family has a durable
prefix certificate, and the runtime establishes
\[
  \mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Actual}
  \subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}.
\]
Then
\[
  \mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Actual}
  \subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}
\]
and
\[
  \mathsf{PCM}(\mathcal A,D,\mathsf{Actual},\ell).
\]
\end{theorem}

The proof is inclusion composition followed by monotonicity of the target
family in $\mathsf{PCM}$.  Its importance is the boundary: the theory tells a
runtime exactly which two refinement obligations suffice, but the compiler
does not prove truthful manifests, complete mediation, or either inclusion
involving $\mathsf{Actual}$.
