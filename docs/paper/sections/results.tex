\section{Exact Agent History Realization}
\label{sec:results}

We now join the structural, language, and durable levels in one result.
Let \(I_0,S_0\) be bootstrap states over the same well-formed security core
and admitted initial cut.  They have empty resolved prefix and identical
history, receipts, and outbox.  In both states, every live gate belongs to the
domain's single current epoch.  State \(S_0\) additionally has the verified program
at \(q^0\), with exactly that epoch active.  Write
\(\mathsf{Ideal}(I_0)\) for the atomic history machine of
\cref{sec:ideal}, and \(\mathsf{Compiled}(S_0)\) for the reference monitor of
\cref{sec:kernel}, including internal compilation, preloading, failed
installs, retries, settlement, and crashes.

The observable alphabet is
\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal O=\{&
 \mathsf{Edge}(u),\Fresh(x,d),\Alias(x,d),\\[-2pt]
 &\mathsf{deny}(x),\Dispatch(d)\}.
\end{split}
\]
Compiler and preload steps, failed stale installs, retries, settlement,
result delivery, and crash recovery are \(\tau\).  Logical alias progress is
observable even though its authority projection is empty.  The observation
projection maps a successful \(\mathsf{Use}(x,j,h)/a\) to \(a\), a successful
\Install to \(\mathsf{Edge}(u)\), and invalid use and head release to
\(\mathsf{deny}(x)\) and \(\Dispatch(d)\), respectively.

For a fixed full admission instance and schema-valid derivation, an
\emph{exact atomic reference-monitor realization} is a successful
single-domain installation satisfying \cref{eq:install-premise}, with
\(\widehat B^\dagger_{H,u}\ne\varnothing\) and a checked indexed witness, that
changes \(\eta^-\) to \(\eta^+\), persists the derived history, and activates
the derivative-correct deterministic program
\(\mathsf{Can}(W^\dagger_{H,u},B^\dagger_{H,u})\), up to checked state
renaming.

\begin{theorem}[Exact Agent History Realization]
\label{thm:exact-history}
Assume the trusted boundary of \cref{sec:model}.  Let
\((\kappa,H,\Delta,\mathsf{out},\mathcal A)\) be a reachable well-formed
security core and let the immutable schema registry derive
\[
 \Sigma_\zeta;H\vdash u\Downarrow
 (H',\mathcal C_{H,u},\eta^-,\eta^+).
\]

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
 \item \textbf{Schema fidelity.}
 The derived history \(H'\) is unique and well formed.  Its preserved context
 and regional family \(\mathcal P_u\) retain every inherited obligation not
 discharged by an authorized retirement.  In each certified region,
 \(\mu_r\) is one global occurrence bijection and
 \(\pi_r:O_{\mathcal D_r}\twoheadrightarrow O_{\mathcal C_r}\).  Occurrence
 incidence and causal order are preserved and reflected, and cell identity
 and lineage are preserved.  No shared occurrence is split by outcome or
 relabeled as a suffix.  Every \((r,o)\in\mathsf{Obl}_u\) has a checked
 full-target witness \(t\) satisfying \(\mathsf{Lift}_u(t,r,o)\).  Context
 outcomes bypassing the edit hole are preserved by identity.

 \item \textbf{Exact prefix-robust admission.}
 The following conditions are equivalent.
 \[
 \begin{aligned}
  &\widehat B^\dagger_{H,u}\ne\varnothing;\\
  &\exists (L,\widehat B).\ (L,\widehat B)
       \text{ realizes }(H,u);\\
  &\text{an exact atomic reference-monitor realization exists};\\
  &\mathsf{Compile}(\kappa,H,\Delta,\mathcal A,u)
       \text{ emits a verifiable seal}.
 \end{aligned}
 \]
 When these conditions hold, the greatest indexed realization is exactly
 \((W^\dagger_{H,u},\widehat B^\dagger_{H,u})\), its runtime marked
 language is \(B^\dagger_{H,u}=\pi_2\widehat B^\dagger_{H,u}\), and every
 realization \((L,\widehat B)\) satisfies
 \(L\subseteq W^\dagger_{H,u}\) and
 \(\widehat B\subseteq\widehat B^\dagger_{H,u}\).  In particular, after every
 \(z\in W^\dagger_{H,u}\), every
 \(o\in\mathsf{Compat}(z)\) still has a safe marked extension
 \((o,b)\in\widehat B^\dagger_{H,u}\) with \(z\preceq_{\rm pref}b\).
 At \(z=\epsilon\), every required \((r,o)\) and preserved context outcome
 therefore has a full-target lift carrying a safe completion.  After a
 nonempty prefix, persistent coverage applies exactly to target outcomes still
 compatible with that prefix.

 If admission fails, the complete descending chain
 \(\widehat B_0,\widehat B_1,\ldots,\widehat B_n=\varnothing\), together
 with each ranked cause
 \((i,(o_{\rm rem},b),z,o_{\rm miss})\) satisfying
 \((o_{\rm rem},b)\in\widehat B_i\setminus\widehat B_{i+1}\),
 \(z\preceq_{\rm pref}b\),
 \(o_{\rm miss}\in\mathsf{Compat}(z)\), and no extension of \(z\) in
 \(\{b'\mid(o_{\rm miss},b')\in\widehat B_i\}\), is a finite certificate that no
 realization exists.

 \item \textbf{Durable implementation refinement.}
 For bootstrap states \(I_0,S_0\) as above,
 \[
   \mathsf{Compiled}(S_0)
   \ \approx_{\mathcal O}^{\mathsf{di}}\
   \mathsf{Ideal}(I_0)
 \]
 by divergence-insensitive weak bisimulation.  Every successful history edge
 is one domain-wide cut.  It closes \(\eta^-\), persists the
 schema-derived history, rebinds every live target gate, and activates the
 single fresh epoch \(\eta^+\) in one reference-monitor transaction.  The
 theorem makes no within-domain partial-fencing claim.  Domains with disjoint
 cells, receipt logs, and policies compose only by asynchronous product.
\end{enumerate}

Consequently, at every reachable prefix the authority trace remains in
\(\mathcal A\), and the active cut remains completion-nonblocking while
preserving every still-compatible indexed outcome.  Only the current
policy-domain epoch can authorize a fresh or alias transition, and each logical
occurrence linearizes on one side of a successful history cut.  Released
effects follow fresh receipt order.  Persistent outcome coverage is an
existence property and provides no fairness guarantee.  In particular, the
theorem does not assert that an enabled occurrence, history edge, dispatch, or
internal computation eventually happens.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Schema fidelity.}
\Cref{lem:history-derivation} gives determinism, well-formedness, authorized
retirement, and domain-wide rebinding.  Its context identity check and
regional \(\mathcal P_u\) certificates give the stated global occurrence maps,
indexed-outcome surjections, checked \(\mathsf{Lift}_u\) witnesses, and
preservation properties.
Thus the Agent supplies only structural identifiers and a schema identifier.
It cannot choose a target contract that drops pending obligations.

\emph{Local equivalence.}
\Cref{lem:greatest-realization} proves that
\(\widehat B^\dagger_{H,u}\ne\varnothing\) iff a realization exists,
identifies \((W^\dagger,\widehat B^\dagger)\) as the greatest one, and
supplies persistent
coverage at every retained prefix.  Finiteness of the derived contracts
makes the fixed point finite.  On admission,
\cref{lem:compiled-language} emits the canonical derivative program for those
generated and projected marked languages, while the checker re-derives the
trusted schema judgment, the indexed fixed point, its witnesses, and the cut
seal.  One atomic installation therefore supplies an exact reference-monitor
realization.  Conversely, a
verifiable seal or exact realization includes a nonempty marked language
whose checked indexed witness is \(\widehat B^\dagger\), so admission holds.
At \(\epsilon\), fixed-point coverage supplies a safe pair for every target
outcome.  The checked regional and context lifts therefore supply a safe
full-target preimage for every required obligation instance.
On rejection, let \(P\) be any post-fixed family.  Forward induction maintains
\(P\subseteq\widehat B_i\).  The recomputed base gives the initial inclusion,
and the checked missing-witness cause for every removed pair excludes it from
\(P\).  Hence \(P\subseteq\widehat B_{i+1}\).  An empty final set excludes every
nonempty post-fixed family and therefore every realization.

\emph{Bisimulation relation.}
Relate an ideal state \(I\) and compiled state \(S\) when:
(i) \(\kappa,H,\Delta\), receipt phases/result futures, and the ordered outbox
agree;
(ii) the ideal active cut is
\((H_c,u,W^\dagger,\widehat B^\dagger)\), the compiled current-epoch
certificate is exactly \((H_c,u,W^\dagger,\widehat B^\dagger,Z)\), their
resolved prefix is \(r\), and
\(\mathsf q(H.\mathsf{epoch})=q_r=(W^\dagger/r,B^\dagger/r)\);
(iii) \(\beta\) equals the kernel binding on live occurrences, exactly
\(H.\mathsf{epoch}\) is active, every live gate is bound to it, and every
earlier epoch is closed; unreachable programs and records in inactive,
unbound epochs are ignored; and
(iv) the ordered occurrence/receipt invariant following
\cref{eq:use-rule} holds.  The bootstrap construction satisfies this
relation.

\emph{Protected occurrences.}
Suppose \(x\) is enabled.  Because the two states agree on \(H\), registry data,
and \(\Delta\), they compute the same resolved label \(a\).  The compiled
program has an \(a\)-transition iff \(ra\in W^\dagger\) by
\cref{lem:compiled-language}, exactly the ideal guard.  Both atomic steps
residualize the same occurrence, append \(a\) to
\(\chi\), advance the view and residual program state, and, only for \Fresh,
append the same receipt and outbox item.  \Cref{lem:residual-coherence}
re-establishes the greatest fixed point for the residual contract.  Any failed
guard, including a missing program transition, disabled occurrence,
misbinding, or closed epoch, produces the same state-preserving denial.  This
proves both simulation directions for \Fresh, \Alias, and denials.

\emph{History edges.}
For an ideal \(\mathsf{Edge}(u)\), determinism of
\cref{lem:history-derivation} gives one
\((H',\mathcal C_{H,u},\eta^-,\eta^+)\) and the same checked schema
certificates.  Local realizability lets the compiled side take internal
compile, check, and preload steps and then a successful \Install.  Its single
domain transaction persists the same \(H'\), closes \(\eta^-\), rebinds all
live target gates, activates \(\eta^+\), starts the exact program at
\((W^\dagger/\epsilon,B^\dagger/\epsilon)\), and atomically records
\((H_c,u,W^\dagger,\widehat B^\dagger,Z)\) as its certificate.  The successors
are related.
Conversely, every successful install re-derives that judgment and verifies
\(\mathsf{Admit}\), so the ideal edge exists and matches it.

If a protected occurrence commits before installation, a fresh step changes
\(\Delta,\chi,\nu\), while an alias changes \(\chi,\nu\).  In either case the old
seal is stale.  If installation commits first, it closes the old policy-domain
epoch, so no gate bound to \(\eta^-\) can resolve either fresh or
alias.
\Cref{lem:cut-linearization} therefore orders every same-domain
use/install race without an intermediate unmatched state.  Only domains with
disjoint cells, logs, and policies commute, by their asynchronous-product
semantics.

\emph{Remaining transitions.}
Both machines dispatch the same outbox head.  Preloading changes only ignored
inactive garbage.  Retry, result delivery, settlement, failed installation,
and crash recovery preserve the relation through matching \(\tau\)-steps.
The relation is re-established at
\(\epsilon\) after every successful edge, so coinduction gives weak
bisimulation.  The bisimulation is divergence insensitive, so finite internal
paths may be collapsed without implying termination, progress, or scheduler
fairness.  Policy safety, persistent outcome coverage, and completion
nonblocking follow from \(W^\dagger\).  Epoch exclusion follows from the
relation invariant and the atomic cut, while FIFO dispatch gives the final
ordering claim.
\end{proof}

The theorem exposes one Agent-specific security boundary.  An edit may rewrite
a live logical past only through a trusted schema that preserves inherited
obligations or discharges them through authorized typed retirement.  Subject
to that schema judgment, the rewrite exists exactly when the greatest
prefix-robust family is nonempty.  The same
object \(\widehat B^\dagger\) both decides the history edge and, by projection,
generates the canonical program installed in the reference monitor that
mediates every later protected occurrence.  Fork, Restore, and
Merge are therefore instances of one history-admission theory rather than
independent mechanisms whose guarantees are merely combined.

\subsection{Dual-history cut observation}

The implementation authenticates the sufficient cut observation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cut-observation}
 \alpha(H,u)=
 (\omega,\zeta,\kappa,H,\Delta,u,
  \mathcal C_{H,u},H_{\mathsf{prog}},\eta^-,\eta^+).
\end{equation}
The schema and policy versions fix the meaning of the edit and of authority.
Inside \(H\), causal order distinguishes \(a\prec b\) from \(b\prec a\), while
frontier mode distinguishes \(a\oplus b\) from \(a\otimes b\).  The registry
\(\rho\) distinguishes two restored occurrences of one semantic cell from two
different effects.  The receipt frontier \(\Delta\) decides whether the
next occurrence is fresh, whereas the ordered logical frontier \(\chi\)
records receipt-silent alias progress through a choice or join.  Finally,
\(\beta\) and \((\eta^-,\eta^+)\) bind the entire policy domain to one side of
the atomic epoch cut.  A workspace hash, receipt log, or epoch identifier
alone therefore omits a distinction used by the theorem.

\subsection{Rejection semantics}

Rejection is not a compiler timeout and need not identify an outcome with no
initially safe completion.  At rank \(i\), a cause
\((i,(o_{\rm rem},b),z,o_{\rm miss})\) says that indexed completion
\((o_{\rm rem},b)\) must be removed because, after its prefix \(z\), the raw
contract still admits outcome \(o_{\rm miss}\) but \(\widehat B_i\) contains no
surviving \(o_{\rm miss}\)-indexed safe extension.  Later ranks expose the
cascading loss of coverage.  An empty stabilized family proves that no
nonempty post-fixed realization
exists, even when every \(M_o\) was initially nonempty.  Changing the edit
schema, policy grant, checkpoint, or registered contract creates a new
instance.  With those inputs fixed, silently pruning runtime behavior cannot
repair the rejected history edge.
