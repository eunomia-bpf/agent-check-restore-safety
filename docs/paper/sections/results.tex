\section{Security Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section establishes two exact runtime boundaries.
Boundary~I characterizes when fixed-topology batch reservation compiles into a product of noncommunicating branch-local budgets.
Boundary~II characterizes when a promotion batch may be decomposed in every owner-group order under immediate cleanup.
Headroom, residual, guard-closure, and support results provide their constructions; lifecycle preservation supplies the common soundness layer.

\subsection{What authorization information is necessary?}

Fix a common action signature---branch labels, authority coordinates, claim schema, and candidate bindings---and consider only well-formed states satisfying \(\ac\).
For an action class \(\mathcal A\), let \(\mathsf{str}_\Sigma(a)\) be its non-quantitative admission predicate (fresh binding, open epochs, and supported owner), and write \(\Sigma\equiv_{\mathcal A}\Sigma'\) when the two states make the same admission decision for every action in the common signature.
A summary is \emph{fully abstract} when summary equality coincides with this decision equivalence.
It is \emph{controller-sufficient} when the same admitted action also produces the same successor summary.

\begin{lemma}[Single-branch Reserve summary]
\label{thm:headroom}
For a claim proposal of demand \(w\) in a well-formed authority-continuous state,
\begin{align*}
&\mathsf{Adm}_\Sigma(\mathsf{reserve}(b,w))\\
&\qquad\Longleftrightarrow
\mathsf{str}_\Sigma(\mathsf{reserve}(b,w))\land w\le H_\Sigma(b).
\end{align*}
If proposals contain every natural demand in every coordinate, the predicate \(\mathsf{str}_\Sigma\) together with \(H_\Sigma\) is fully abstract for single-branch Reserve.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Adding \(w\) changes exactly the configurations containing \(b\).
All remain safe iff \(w\) is bounded by their componentwise minimum slack, which is \cref{eq:headroom}.
Equality of \(H\) therefore gives equal decisions.
If coordinate \(k\) differs, a demand strictly above the smaller and at most the larger headroom distinguishes the states.
\end{proof}

Fully abstract for one step does not mean suitable for iteration.

\begin{example}[Headroom is not controller-sufficient]
\label{prop:headroom-update}
No deterministic updater using only the current single-Reserve structural predicate, \(H\), and an accepted Reserve can always produce exact successor headroom.
\end{example}

Let \(G=1\), \(d=q=0\), and use two branches.
Give both states the same open epochs and binding predicate.
Exclusive choice and parallel composition then have the same structural Reserve predicate and \(H=(1,1)\), and both accept one unit for \(b_1\).
Afterwards their headroom is respectively \((0,1)\) and \((0,0)\): the choice still permits a disjoint promise to \(b_2\), whereas parallel composition does not.

The missing information is correlation.

\begin{lemma}[Correlated residual controller]
\label{thm:residual}
A simultaneous reservation batch \(x\) is admitted exactly when \(\mathsf{str}_\Sigma(x)\) and \(x\in\mathcal R_\Sigma\).
For coordinate-complete batch proposals, \(\mathsf{str}_\Sigma\) together with \(\mathcal R_\Sigma\) is fully abstract.
Moreover, in the fixed-topology Reserve-only fragment with a fixed structural signature and fresh claim IDs,
\begin{equation}
x\in\mathcal R_\Sigma
\Longrightarrow
\mathcal R_{\Sigma+x}
=\{y\mid x+y\in\mathcal R_\Sigma\}.
\label{eq:residual-derivative}
\end{equation}
Thus a sequence \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) is admitted iff \(\sum_i x_i\in\mathcal R_\Sigma\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substituting \(q(C)+\sum_{b\in C}x_b\) in \cref{eq:ac} gives exactly \cref{eq:residual}.
Equality of residual regions is consequently equivalent to equality of all batch decisions.
After accepting \(x\), each configuration's slack decreases by \(\sum_{b\in C}x_b\); rearranging the defining inequalities yields \cref{eq:residual-derivative}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Reserve information lower bound]
\label{cor:residual-lower-bound}
Fix the structural predicate and common signature.
Any summary from which a deterministic checker decides every batch Reserve exactly must distinguish every pair of states with different \(\mathcal R\).
Consequently \(\mathcal R\) is the coarsest behavioral quotient for this action class, up to an injective re-encoding; this is not a claim about minimum byte size.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
If \(\mathcal R_\Sigma\ne\mathcal R_{\Sigma'}\), a batch in their symmetric difference requires opposite decisions.
Equal summaries would force the deterministic checker to return the same decision, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

Let \(B^+=\{b\mid H_\Sigma(b)\ne\bot\}\) and define the product of branch-local capability budgets
\[
\Box(H_\Sigma)=
\{x\in\Nat^{B^+\times\calK}\mid
\forall b.\ 0\le x_b\le H_\Sigma(b)\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Boundary I: exact product factorization]
\label{thm:rectangularity}
The projection of \(\mathcal R_\Sigma\) onto branch \(b\) is exactly \([0,H_\Sigma(b)]\), and \(\mathcal R_\Sigma\subseteq\Box(H_\Sigma)\).
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_\Sigma=\Box(H_\Sigma)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall C\in\Phi.\quad
\sum_{b\in C}H_\Sigma(b)\le s_\Sigma(C).
\label{eq:rectangularity}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Setting every other branch demand to zero shows that every \(w\le H_\Sigma(b)\) occurs in the projection; the headroom definition excludes larger \(w\).
These singleton bounds give \(\mathcal R_\Sigma\subseteq\Box(H_\Sigma)\).
The reverse inclusion holds exactly when the greatest corner \((H_\Sigma(b))_{b\in B^+}\) satisfies every residual inequality, which is \cref{eq:rectangularity}; downward closure then admits the whole box.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Architectural consequence.}
Among checkers whose joint acceptance set is a Cartesian product of branch-local acceptance sets, concurrent soundness and completeness for every one-branch batch are compatible iff \cref{eq:rectangularity} holds.
Single-branch completeness forces local checker \(b\) to accept all of \([0,H_\Sigma(b)]\); independent composition therefore accepts \(\Box(H_\Sigma)\).
This is sound exactly when the box is contained in \(\mathcal R_\Sigma\), and \cref{thm:rectangularity} gives the criterion.
If it fails, a product-only checker must reject a sound singleton or accept an unsound batch; a runtime may instead coordinate, retain escrow, reduce local budgets, or restrict and re-admit topology.

The choice/parallel states in \cref{prop:headroom-update} have the same Reserve structural predicate and identical per-branch headroom but different residual regions: the batch assigning one unit to both branches belongs only to the choice residual.
The choice residual is rectangular and therefore admits exact product-local budgets; the parallel residual is not.
The result does not claim a full nonblocking supervisor; topology changes and external effects use the semantics of \cref{sec:semantics}.

\subsection{Partial observation has an exact price}

Let observation \(o\) identify a reachable knowledge set \(K(o)\) of authority states.
Define
\[
H_o^K(b)=\bigwedge_{\Sigma\in K(o)}H_\Sigma(b),
\qquad
\mathcal R_o^K=\bigcap_{\Sigma\in K(o)}\mathcal R_\Sigma.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Greatest knowledge-safe one-step checker]
\label{thm:knowledge}
Among memoryless observation-based checkers sound for every state in \(K(o)\), the unique pointwise-greatest checker accepts \(\mathsf{reserve}(b,w)\) iff every possible state satisfies its structural premises and \(w\le H_o^K(b)\); it accepts batch \(x\) iff every state satisfies the batch's structural premises and \(x\in\mathcal R_o^K\).
It is as precise as concrete-state admission in every possible state iff those states have the same action-class acceptance set.
When structural admissibility is constant over the fiber and the proposal space is coordinate-complete, this is equivalent to constancy of the relevant \(H\) or \(\mathcal R\) profile.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Soundness requires both structural admissibility and quantitative acceptance in every possible state, giving the intersection.
Any action outside it is unsafe in one member of the knowledge set, so no sound observation-based checker can additionally accept it.
Concrete precision is possible exactly when the members agree on all decisions.
Under a common coordinate-complete structural proposal space, \cref{thm:headroom,thm:residual} reduce this condition to profile constancy.
\end{proof}

Snapshot-only restore is one instance.
Replacing and live restore can reconstruct identical \(V_r\), but with one-unit capacity an old parallel branch holding one unit changes restored-branch headroom from one to zero.
The theorem requires fresh authenticated evidence sufficient to determine the residual; it does not require a centralized ledger specifically.
It is a one-step knowledge result, not a reinvention of general partial-observation supervisory control.

\subsection{Promotion forces a richer policy language}

\begin{proposition}[Choice/parallel is not promotion-closed]
\label{prop:nonclosure}
There exists a safe base contract whose exact safe promotion repair is not representable by any unique-leaf choice/parallel contract or pairwise conflict graph.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take
\[
P=b\Box(x\parallel y\parallel z),\qquad G=3,
\]
with one unit in each branch bundle.
After promoting \(b\)'s claim, the durable unit leaves capacity two on the right: every singleton and pair among \(x,y,z\) remains possible, but their triple does not.
A conflict graph that permits every pair has no edges and therefore also permits the triple.
Choice/parallel contracts induce such pairwise cograph compatibility, so none represents the repaired family.
\end{proof}

Pure structured repairs exist, but there is no unique largest one: choosing which one of the three pairs to conflict yields incomparable maximal repairs.

\begin{lemma}[Compact exact promotion closure]
\label{thm:guard-closure}
For any guarded contract \(T\) and finite tentative set \(U\) with \(a_U\le G\),
\begin{equation}
\front(T\wedge\gamma_U)
=\{C\in\front(T)\mid a_U+\sum_{b\in C}u_U(b)\le G\}.
\label{eq:guard-closure}
\end{equation}
This is the unique largest topology-only safe pruning for fixed claims, represented by one new vector row: at most \(|\calK|\) scalar inequalities, \(O(|B||\calK|)\) coefficients, and singleton lineage tests.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After moving \(U\) into \(D\), the left side of the inequality is exactly the promoted load of \(C\) by \cref{eq:promotion-load}.
Conjoining \(\gamma_U\) therefore retains exactly and all safe old configurations.
Any safe restriction can contain no configuration failing this inequality.
\end{proof}

The result is a closure theorem for authority promotion, not a claim that threshold predicates are new.
Under the controller's no-silent-policy-expansion rule, the installed row is frozen: if it were recomputed from current \(Q\), later withdrawal could reopen a future durably removed by promotion.
Such reopening can be authority-safe, but it is a new lifecycle expansion and requires explicit re-admission rather than mutation of an audited decision.

\begin{lemma}[Owner-support criterion]
\label{cor:support}
The exact repair preserves an individual support witness for every owner with a nonempty remaining bundle iff each such owner occurs in some configuration of \(\front(T\wedge\gamma_U)\).
If owner \(b\) has no such support, no safe subfamily of the exact repair can preserve it.
\end{lemma}

This does not preserve every old correlation: two owners can each retain a witness while their former joint configuration is removed.
An implementation therefore reports unsupported owners and removed maximal configurations (or declared correlation obligations), rather than claiming “no other cancellation.”

\subsection{Algebraic commutation versus operational order}

Promotion filters for a fixed batch commute as set intersections, but cleanup may tombstone the owner of a later effect.

\begin{theorem}[Boundary II: order-independent promotion]
\label{thm:serial}
Let \(U\) be a valid atomic batch of unique tentative claims and \(U_b=U\cap Q_b\) in the fixed source state.
Assume no interleaved lifecycle mutation, exact prefix guards, and immediate deterministic cleanup.
Every permutation of the nonempty owner groups \(U_b\) remains enabled and reaches the same \((D,Q,X,\front(T))\) as atomic promotion of \(U\) iff every promoted owner is supported in the final family \(\front(T\wedge\gamma_U)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For sufficiency, consider any owner-group prefix.
For every prefix \(S\subseteq U\), the final safe family is contained in the prefix-safe family because promotion never decreases load.
A final support witness for a later owner is therefore also present at every prefix, so cleanup cannot tombstone it.
The last installed batch row implies every prefix row, so final claim placement and guarded-contract denotation depend only on \(U\), not its order; implementations may delete the redundant prefix rows as a canonical normalization.

For necessity, suppose promoted owner \(b\) lacks final support and order every other owner group before \(U_b\).
If an earlier group becomes disabled, this is already a failing permutation.
Otherwise, after those groups promote, the support of \(b\) is exactly its final support: promoting \(b\)'s own claims would leave the load of every configuration containing \(b\) unchanged, moving equal demand from \(Q_b\) to \(D\).
Cleanup therefore tombstones \(b\) before \(U_b\), disabling the next group.
\end{proof}

The characterization is operational, not merely algebraic.
Let \(G=2\), let \(s\) and \(t\) be exclusive, and set \(Q_s=\{c_s:1\}\), \(Q_t=\{c_t:1,u:1\}\).
Promoting \(c_s\) first makes the \(t\)-configuration load three, tombstones \(t\), and prevents later promotion of \(c_t\).
Promoting \(c_t\) first leaves both configurations at load two, so \(c_s\) can subsequently promote.
If order-independent dispatch of both effects is required, the runtime must seal the fixed batch before either dispatch or otherwise coordinate a verified order; failure of final-owner support alone does not rule out every serial order.

\subsection{Concrete effect coverage}

For trace prefix \(\tau\), let \(\mathsf{Ops}(\tau)\) be the stable IDs appearing in its attempt labels, and let \(c_e\) be the unique claim paired with \(e\).
Repeated attempts use the same pair; \(\mathsf{actual}(e)\) is their aggregate durable outcome.

\begin{lemma}[Effect coverage]
\label{lem:coverage}
If every attempt is mediated and \(\mathsf{actual}(e)\le w(c_e)\), then
\[
\sum_{e\in\mathsf{Ops}(\tau)}\mathsf{actual}(e)
\le\sum_{c\in D}w(c)=d.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Abstract lifecycle preservation]
\label{thm:preservation}
If \(A\) is well formed and satisfies \(\ac\), and \(A\xrightarrow{u/\eta}A'\) is admitted by the rules of \cref{sec:semantics}, then \(A'\) is well formed and satisfies \(\ac\).
Every attempt label uses a ticket whose matching claim is already in \(D\); all retries of one stable operation ID use the same claim, and neither the ID nor claim can be rebound to a second logical operation.
Closed branch or grant epochs cannot authorize a new Reserve or Prepare; only a previously sealed one-shot operation may finish after closure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By cases on the admitted rule.
Checkpoint and ticket-only steps stutter on \((G,D,Q,T)\).
Restriction, abort, and revoke only remove configurations or tentative claims.
Simulation-certified targets satisfy \(\ac\) by \cref{eq:simulation} and source continuity; directly admitted merge and Reserve satisfy their checked closed formulas.
The fragment-conservation check on \(\rho\), global freshness, and terminalization preserve the claim partition.
Prepare preserves \(\ac\) by \cref{thm:guard-closure} and moves each unique claim from \(Q\) to \(D\) exactly once.
Ticket rules preserve durable history, bind every attempt to \(D\), and retain the stable ID across retry and recovery.
Epoch premises exclude new Reserve and Prepare after closure; dispatch of a previously sealed operation changes no authority field.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Concrete trace authority safety]
\label{thm:trace}
Assume an initially related concrete/abstract state; complete mediation of protected effects and authority-changing transitions; durable Prepare before external observation; and a concrete implementation that forward-simulates the admitted abstract rules or takes authority-preserving stutters.
Then for every concrete trace prefix and every currently permitted configuration \(C\),
\[
\sum_{e\in\mathsf{Ops}(\tau)}\mathsf{actual}(e)+q(C)\le G.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Induction over the simulated trace uses \cref{thm:preservation}.
The coverage lemma bounds distinct concrete operations by durable demand, and \cref{eq:ac} adds each permitted conditional bundle.
\end{proof}

The corollary is a refinement obligation, not evidence that today's optional hooks provide complete mediation.
It cannot prove that a binding or declared demand matches natural-language intent, that a bypassing tool path does not exist, or that a remote sink reports truthfully.
Those are explicit refinement and deployment obligations rather than facts hidden by the model.
