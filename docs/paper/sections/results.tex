\section{Canonical Causal Epoch Theorem}
\label{sec:results}

Write $\mathsf{Sound}_R(E)$ when
$\mathsf{FreshTraces}(E)\subseteq F_R$, and
$\mathsf{Pres}_R(E)$ when every $\omega\in\Omega_e$ has a linearization in
$\mathsf{FreshTraces}(E)$.  These predicates quantify over behavior, not a
caller-supplied controller manifest.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical Causal Epoch]
\label{thm:canonical-epoch}
Let $R$ be well formed, let $\delta\in\mathcal A$, and assume complete
mediation through the linearizable, crash-stable kernel rules in
\cref{sec:semantics}.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Exact feasibility.}
  \[
    \mathsf{Admit}(R)
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \exists E.\ \mathsf{Sound}_R(E)\land\mathsf{Pres}_R(E).
  \]
  If it holds, $\mathsf{Can}(R)$ is such an epoch and
  \[
    \mathsf{FreshTraces}(\mathsf{Can}(R))
      =\mathsf{FreshTraces}(\mathsf{GM}(R))=F_R .
  \]
  Hence it preserves every monitor-legal behavior, and every monitor-sound
  epoch has a language contained in its language.

  \item \textbf{Atomic-cut equivalence.}
  Installing a verified seal at its named
  $(\kappa,\nu,\delta,\mathsf{src})$ establishes
  \cref{eq:kernel-reference-relation}.  The compiled kernel and ideal
  history-cut monitor are bisimilar on \textnormal{\textsc{Prepare}} and
  \textnormal{\textsc{Install}}, with replay, retry, settlement, and crash as matching
  stutters.  A failed install has no state effect, and after a successful
  install no source epoch can make a fresh preparation.

  \item \textbf{Online composition.}
  For every finite interleaving of fresh preparations, stutters, and
  successfully installed admitted Fork/Restore/Merge requests
  $R_0,R_1,\ldots$ under the same $(\kappa,\mathcal A)$, the execution
  partitions at installs into traces
  $\sigma_i\in F_{R_i}$.  At every prefix,
  \[
    \delta_0\ell_0^*(\sigma_0)
      \ell_1^*(\sigma_1)\cdots\in\mathcal A ,
  \]
  and a fresh event belongs to exactly one side of every history cut.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Feasibility.}
If a sound, promise-preserving $E$ exists, each promised outcome has a
linearization in $\mathsf{FreshTraces}(E)\subseteq F_R$, which is precisely
\cref{eq:causal-admission}.  Conversely, when
$\mathsf{Admit}(R)$ holds, \cref{lem:residual-exact} gives
$\mathsf{FreshTraces}(\mathsf{Can}(R))=F_R$; every promise therefore has a
canonical-epoch execution.  Equality with the global monitor follows from
\cref{eq:gm-language}.  The maximality statement is the definition of monitor
soundness.

\emph{One cut.}
Relate the initial canonical state to the ideal monitor at $\epsilon$.
Suppose the relation holds after $\sigma$.  The canonical program enables $d$
iff $\sigma d\in F_R$ by \cref{eq:canonical-epoch}; this is exactly the ideal
monitor guard.  Both \Prepare{} steps append the same $\ell(d)$ and move to
states related at $\sigma d$.  The \textsc{Install} guards compare the same
durable fields.  On success both semantics close the complete source set,
activate the target at $\epsilon$, and advance the view; on failure both
stutter.  All remaining rules emit no fresh symbol and preserve the relation.
This proves bisimulation by induction on transitions.  In particular, an old
\Prepare{} before the cut changes the compared $\delta$, whereas after the
cut its closed-phase premise is false.

\emph{Many cuts.}
Within epoch $i$, the one-cut argument gives
$\sigma_i\in F_{R_i}$, so the definition of $F_{R_i}$ keeps the cumulative
receipt trace in $\mathcal A$ after every prefix.  A successful next install
is anchored to that exact trace and starts the relation at $\epsilon$.
Induction over successful installs proves the displayed invariant and unique
cut ownership.
\end{proof}

The theorem is one compiler-correctness result.  Its first clause prevents
``safe'' admission by deleting a promised causal outcome; its equality
prevents deleting optional monitor behavior; and its last two clauses connect
that language to the live controller generation that may actually prepare an
effect.

\subsection{Complete negative evidence}

If admission fails, choose
\[
 \omega\in\Omega_e
 \quad\text{with}\quad
 \mathsf{Lin}(\omega)\cap F_R=\varnothing .
\]
This is a complete witness, not merely a compiler timeout.

\begin{corollary}[No pruning repair]
\label{cor:no-pruning-repair}
For such an $\omega$, no monitor-sound epoch can preserve the request.  Adding
authority, changing the typed operation or its promise, or changing ledger
identity produces a new request; restricting behavior cannot repair this one.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every monitor-sound epoch has trace language contained in $F_R$, which
contains no linearization of $\omega$.
\end{proof}

In the procurement example, if $p_B$ prepares before selection, the new
durable residual may contain no completion of the promised supplier-$A$
outcome.  The stale seal fails first; recompilation then returns that outcome
as the witness instead of installing a controller that is certain to break
the promise later.

\subsection{Why the trace lift is strict}

The causal model is not a notational lift of endpoint families.  Consider
\[
  F_1=\{\epsilon,a,b,ab\},
  \qquad
  F_2=\{\epsilon,a,b,ba\}.
\]
Both have exactly the endpoint family
$\{\varnothing,\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\}$, yet one admits $a\prec b$ and the
other $b\prec a$.  The traces $ab$ and $ba$ have the same endpoint and
opposite verdicts under an order-sensitive policy.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint erasure is incomplete]
\label{prop:endpoint-incomplete}
No admission decider that factors only through
$\{\operatorname{set}(\sigma)\mid\sigma\in F_R\}$ is exact for the request
class in \cref{eq:raw-plan}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the same two-action endpoint family in two requests, promising
$a\prec b$.  Let the first policy admit $ab$ and the second admit only $ba$
(in addition to both one-event traces).  The endpoint observation agrees, but
\cref{eq:causal-admission} accepts only the first.
\end{proof}

Sequence and Merge barriers therefore add expressive power that qualified
endpoint names cannot recover without encoding the entire causal state into
each name.  When policy and plant are permutation invariant, quotienting by
endpoint sets recovers the older commutative corollary in
\cref{sec:validation}.

\subsection{Tightness of the atomic cut}

The program equality alone does not imply the online theorem.  Both durable
comparisons in \cref{eq:install-rule} have small counterexamples.

\paragraph{Receipt-frontier comparison}
Let $\mathcal A=\{\epsilon,x,y\}$, let an old source be able to prepare $x$,
and compile a target whose sole fresh action is $y$ at
$\delta=\epsilon$.  If old $x$ linearizes first but installation ignores the
changed receipt trace, target $y$ produces $xy\notin\mathcal A$.  Comparing
only a workspace or view version does not detect this race.

\paragraph{Complete source closure}
Use the same policy, let installation linearize first, and omit the old source
from the closed set.  Target $y$ and stale $x$ can again both prepare, in
either order, although the canonical target program is exact.  Therefore a
successful cut must compare and close the complete authoritative source set,
not only publish a fresh target identifier.

Policy and view versions prevent analogous ABA reuse: without them, a seal for
an earlier Restore can overwrite a later Fork whose receipt trace happens to
match.  Program and request hashes prevent rebinding a valid cut to different
plan or anchor data.  We do not claim these literal fields are the only
encoding; the counterexamples require their semantic distinctions or
equivalent information.

\subsection{The Agent-specific boundary}

For a fixed $F_R$, residual synthesis is classical.  For a fixed configuration
change with no protected race, compare-and-swap is classical.  The theorem
quantifies over their shared linearization boundary: the typed Agent edge
simultaneously changes the causal plant, the controller generation, and the
meaning of copied handles, while the receipt trace may irreversibly advance.
Its bisimulation fails if the cut is treated as an offline compiler call or if
the installer is left as a refinement assumption.  This online
raw-edge-to-durable-epoch statement is the claimed contribution.
