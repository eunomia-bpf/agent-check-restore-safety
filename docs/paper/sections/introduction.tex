\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Modern agent runtimes increasingly transform their own execution histories.
They fork alternatives for search, retain parallel descendants, restore saved
sessions, and merge results while the execution graph is still being
discovered.  Codex and Claude expose session or thread branching, and emerging
agent substrates make checkpointing and speculative execution first-class
operations~\cite{codexappserver,claudesessions,wang2026fork,wu2026crab}.
Unlike a conventional restart, these operations may intentionally keep several
continuations alive while tools mutate services outside any copied workspace.

However, individually safe continuations can become unsafe when a history edge
changes which outcomes may coexist.  Suppose three prospective commitments
$a,b,c$ are governed by a contract that permits every pair but forbids the
triple.  Each commitment is fresh, and three controllers may each expose only
one locally admitted singleton; nevertheless, a parallel Fork or live Restore
can make all three controllers co-live and thereby realize
$\{a,b,c\}$.  Per-effect authorization succeeds, every pairwise conflict check
succeeds, and no redemption cell is spent twice, yet the resulting configuration
violates the original contract.  Replaying an already durable cell after
Restore is the simpler unary instance of the same missing admission boundary.

The problem exists because security state is not one workspace snapshot.
One decision has an immutable durable receipt prefix and a correlated family of
prospective commitments; it also has a controller layout describing which
local choices can become durable during one controller epoch, while remote
writes and disclosures remain in external reality.  Whole-workspace rollback cannot undo an escaped effect,
must not rewind the receipt prefix, and implements the wrong semantics when
Fork, live Restore, or join Merge deliberately retains several descendants.
Nor can the complete future execution graph be named in advance: the runtime
must certify each topology-changing edge from its current contracts and then
compose those local certificates.

Existing mechanisms each cover only part of this boundary.  Rollback recovery,
state continuity, and ACRFence preserve or check the past but do not derive the
co-durable future of six distinct history operators
~\cite{elnozahy2002rollback,parno2011memoir,zheng2026acrfence}.
Consumable capabilities, revocable agent capabilities, and commit-time
authorization can protect one use or one effect witness, but several valid
cells may still form a forbidden union
~\cite{bowers2007consumable,santos2026lingering,santos2026committime}.
General history-sensitive monitors such as FORGE can enforce declared policies
over observed causal predicates~\cite{palumbo2026forge}; they do not themselves
derive a counterfactual future family for a proposed Fork/Restore/Merge or
verify that independently live controllers realize only that family.

We observe that a sound history boundary requires two independent
normalizations.  The first maps copied handles to semantic \emph{redemption
cells}, the units at which one fresh durable commitment is consumed.  The
second maps gate uses to authoritative \emph{controller instances}, the units
whose joint state preserves correlations among several cells.  Conflating the
two misses the example above: atomic redemption can be correct for every cell
while distinct controllers product-compose a forbidden configuration.  A
downward-closed family $\Gamma$ records which controllers may remain
authoritative during one co-durable epoch.  Write
$\mathsf{Actual}$ for configurations the installed runtime can make durable and
$\mathsf{RawPhysical}$ for the manifest-induced controller product.  This leads
to the central refinement chain
\[
  \mathsf{Required}\subseteq\mathsf{Actual}
  \subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted},
\]
where the typed history operation derives the required behavior, the durable
prefix derives the admitted future, the manifest derives the raw controller
product, and a concrete runtime must refine that manifest.

We present a theory-guided \emph{history-admission compiler} for this boundary.
Six typed Fork/Restore/Merge operators derive candidate and required families;
durable-prefix filtering computes the greatest prefix-compatible admitted
future; and relational controller-cover analysis checks required coverage and
physical overpermission.  The compiler emits a certificate or typed negative
witness, and an independent verifier recomputes the decision and accepts only
a canonical match.  Lean checks a contract-indexed co-liveness observation
arity sufficient for exact readiness and an arbitrary-$k$ family making every
smaller arity insufficient in the worst case.  A constructive corollary returns
the greatest downward-closed pruning of submitted $\Gamma$ whose fixed-$E,L$
product is admitted and decides whether it preserves $\mathsf{Required}$.
The prototype records full $\Gamma$, its coverage attestation, the arity, and
this non-installed proposal; 31 focused tests (55 total) and a 2,166-case
oracle cross-check its semantics.  Public and private trajectories expose the
workload and missing adapter fields, but provide no safety labels.
The tool is neither a checkpoint engine nor an effect authorizer: truthful
manifests, complete mediation, controller installation, and both inclusions
involving $\mathsf{Actual}$ remain integration obligations.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
This paper makes three contributions.
\nopagebreak[4]

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item A typed security model for history-transforming computation.  It derives
  correlated may/required futures for choice and parallel Fork, replacing and
  live Restore, and select and join Merge; computes the greatest admitted future
  compatible with an immutable receipt prefix; and supports compositional
  inheritance over an online graph
  (\cref{sec:model,sec:semantics,sec:results}).

  \item A controller-realization theory that separates cell identity from
  controller identity.  For overlapping access it characterizes readiness and
  obstructions, yields the greatest submitted-$\Gamma$ pruning at fixed $E,L$,
  extracts minimal correlation cuts, and gives a sufficient contract-indexed
  observation arity with an arbitrary-arity tightness family.  It recovers
  functional partitions as a special case and transports prefix safety under
  explicit runtime refinement premises (\cref{sec:results}).

  \item A small compiler and independent verifier whose certificate and witness
  schema follows the proof decomposition.  Lean~4 checks the theorem spine;
  31 focused compiler/verifier tests (55 across the complete artifact) and an
  exhaustive 2,166-instance oracle cross-check the implementation; public
  TraceLab and the private paper-formation lineage
  characterize workload and adapter observability without supplying safety
  labels (\cref{sec:algorithm,sec:validation}).
\end{enumerate}
