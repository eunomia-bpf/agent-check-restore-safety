\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Agent runtimes fork alternatives, retain parallel descendants, restore saved
sessions, and merge results while discovering the execution graph online.
Codex, Claude, and emerging substrates expose these operations
~\cite{codexappserver,claudesessions,wang2026fork,wu2026crab}.  Unlike restart,
they may keep several continuations live while tools mutate external services.

Individually safe continuations can become unsafe when an edge changes which
outcomes may coexist.  Suppose a contract permits every pair of fresh
commitments $a,b,c$ but forbids the triple.  Three locally safe controllers can
realize $\{a,b,c\}$ after parallel Fork or live Restore: per-effect and pairwise
checks pass and no cell is spent twice, yet the contract fails.  Replaying an
already durable cell after Restore is the unary instance.

Security state is therefore not one workspace snapshot: it includes an
immutable receipt prefix, a correlated future, a controller layout, and escaped
external effects.  Whole-workspace rollback cannot undo remote reality, must
not rewind receipts, and implements the wrong semantics when several
descendants should survive.  Because the graph is not known in advance, the
runtime must certify current edges and compose their certificates.

Existing mechanisms each cover only part of this boundary.  Rollback recovery,
state continuity, and ACRFence preserve or check the past but do not derive the
co-durable future of a typed choice, product, or replacement edge
~\cite{elnozahy2002rollback,parno2011memoir,zheng2026acrfence}.
Consumable capabilities, revocable agent capabilities, and commit-time
authorization can protect one use or one effect witness, but several valid
cells may still form a forbidden union
~\cite{bowers2007consumable,santos2026lingering,santos2026committime}.
General history-sensitive monitors such as FORGE can enforce declared policies
over observed causal predicates~\cite{palumbo2026forge}; they do not themselves
derive a counterfactual future family for a proposed Fork/Restore/Merge or
verify that independently live controllers realize only that family.

Exact structural admission cannot in general erase either of two identity
relations.
$q_{\mathrm{cell}}$ maps copied handles to atomic redemption cells;
$q_{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ maps gate uses to authoritative controller instances.  A
mechanized two-by-two construction fixes every other raw field and shows that
erasing either map while retaining the other conflates ready and nonready
worlds (\cref{prop:identity-erasure}).  A
downward-closed family $\Gamma$ records which controllers may remain
authoritative during one co-durable epoch.  Write
$\mathsf{Actual}$ for configurations the installed runtime can make durable and
$\mathsf{RawPhysical}$ for the manifest-induced controller product.  This leads
to the central refinement chain
\[
  \mathsf{Required}\subseteq\mathsf{Actual}
  \subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted},
\]
where the typed history operation derives the required behavior, the durable
prefix derives the admitted future, the manifest derives the raw controller
product, and a concrete runtime must refine that manifest.

Our theory-guided \emph{history-admission compiler} derives typed futures,
filters them against receipts, and checks controller-cover realization.  It
emits a certificate or typed witness; an independent verifier recomputes the
canonical result.  The contract derives an arity $r$: exact readiness needs
only $\Pi_{\leq r}(\Gamma)$, while an arbitrary-$k$ cover family makes smaller
arity insufficient in general.  The prototype accepts full $\Gamma$, its exact
$r$-projection, or a diagnostic upper bound.  Its 38 focused tests (62 total)
and 2,166-case oracle cross-check the semantics; trajectories characterize
adapter gaps, not safety.  Truthful manifests, mediation, installation, and
$\mathsf{Actual}$ refinement remain runtime obligations.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
This paper makes three contributions.
\nopagebreak[4]

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item A typed security model for history-transforming computation.  It derives
  correlated may/required futures for choice and parallel Fork, replacing and
  live Restore, and select and join Merge; computes the greatest admitted future
  compatible with an immutable receipt prefix; and supports compositional
  inheritance over an online graph
  (\cref{sec:model,sec:semantics,sec:results}).

  \item An exact-evidence theorem for controller realization.  At fixed $E,L$,
  the contract-derived $r$-projection of $\Gamma$ decides readiness exactly;
  an arbitrary-$k$ well-formed cover rules out every uniformly lower-arity
  projection.  Fixed-manifest collisions independently rule out erasing cell-
  or controller-identity information.  Obstruction extraction and fixed-$E,L$
  pruning provide constructive witnesses and repairs
  (\cref{sec:results}).

  \item A small compiler and independent verifier whose certificate and witness
  schema follows the proof decomposition.  Lean~4 checks the theorem spine;
  38 focused compiler/verifier tests (62 across the complete artifact) and an
  exhaustive 2,166-instance oracle cross-check the implementation; public
  TraceLab and the private paper-formation lineage
  characterize workload and adapter observability without supplying safety
  labels (\cref{sec:algorithm,sec:validation}).
\end{enumerate}
