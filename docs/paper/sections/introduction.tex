\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Tool-using agents increasingly construct their execution graph while they
work.  They fork alternatives, keep descendants live in parallel, restore
saved sessions, and merge results, while tool calls mutate services outside
any restorable workspace.  Codex, Claude, and emerging agent substrates
already expose parts of this lifecycle
~\cite{codexappserver,claudesessions,wang2026fork,wu2026crab}.  A security
decision must therefore follow not only the agent's current state, but also
which continuations an Agent history edge makes jointly durable.

An Agent history edge can be safe one commitment at a time yet unable to
deliver the future that the edge promises.  Consider authority that permits
every pair of fresh commitments $a,b,c$, but forbids their triple.  After $a$
is durable, the current continuation requires $b$ and a saved continuation
requires $c$; the agent requests $\mathsf{RestoreLive}(N,K)$ so that both
continuations remain live.  A serialized monitor may accept either $b$ or $c$
and reject the other, so it prevents the forbidden triple but discovers only
after installing the edge that its required join cannot complete.  The
ternary-conflict family itself is classical
~\cite{vanglabbeek2009configuration}; the Agent-specific question is when a
history edge promises to preserve it under detached authority.

The mismatch is between two kinds of quantification.  Commit authorization
asks whether one next effect is safe relative to the durable prefix.
An Agent history edge instead declares a family of possible and required
future configurations, and Fork, Restore, or Merge changes whether its arms
are alternatives or may commit together.  Installing the edge also creates a
controller topology whose independently allowed local choices may realize a
larger family than any one gate exposes.  A workspace snapshot records none of
the durable receipt prefix, future correlation, or authoritative controller
identity needed to relate these two views.

The strongest baseline is a single serialized atomic commit monitor.  Under
complete mediation, it atomically checks each proposed commitment against the
current durable prefix and advances that prefix only on success; this
guarantees that no forbidden configuration becomes durable.  We do not claim
stronger safety than that monitor.  Rollback and continuity mechanisms protect
reconstructed past state
~\cite{elnozahy2002rollback,parno2011memoir,zheng2026acrfence}, while
consumable capabilities and commit-time authorization can realize the
serialized boundary for individual commitments
~\cite{bowers2007consumable,santos2026lingering,santos2026committime}.
History-sensitive monitors can mediate richer declared policies
~\cite{palumbo2026forge}.  What these mechanisms do not by themselves decide
is whether a proposed Agent history edge can be installed in a particular
controller topology without either exceeding that reference monitor or
silently losing a future that the edge declares required.

We observe that an Agent history edge can be treated as a
\emph{monitor-compilation request}.  Its type first derives correlated
candidate and required futures; the durable prefix then selects exactly the
candidate configurations that the serialized reference monitor could accept.
The edge is implementable by a controller topology precisely when that
topology realizes every required configuration and no configuration outside
this admitted family:
\[
  \mathsf{Required}\subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}
  \subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}.
\]
The right inclusion specializes the reference monitor's safety boundary into
the installed topology, while the left inclusion prevents ``safe'' compilation
by deleting mandatory Agent behavior.

Making this compilation exact requires evidence that is easy to conflate.
Copied handles must be normalized by atomic redemption identity, whereas gate
uses must be normalized independently by authoritative controller identity.
Moreover, locally safe controllers can create a forbidden higher-order union,
so exact compilation may need co-liveness evidence beyond pairs.  Finally,
both receipts and controller versions evolve as the agent runs; a conclusion
must be bound to one durable prefix and one co-durable epoch rather than reused
after either changes.

We ask three questions.  \emph{RQ1:} when is a typed Agent edge exactly a
monitor-sound and required-transparent specialization?  \emph{RQ2:} which
edge, receipt, identity, and co-liveness evidence is sufficient and
worst-case necessary for that decision?  \emph{RQ3:} can a small compiler
decide the criterion and emit independently checkable positive or negative
evidence?

We present \emph{Agent history admission}, a finite decision procedure and
proof system for this monitor-compilation property.  Six typed
Fork/Restore/Merge operators derive the candidate and required futures, a
greatest fixed-prefix filter derives $\mathsf{Admitted}$, and controller-cover
semantics derives $\mathsf{RawPhysical}$.  The central result characterizes
when the typed edge has a required-preserving realization beneath the
serialized monitor and when its declared controller topology is such a
realization; runtime refinement then transports the structural judgment to
actual commitments.  The same result yields an exact contract-indexed
co-liveness projection and worst-case lower bounds showing when smaller
evidence, or either identity normalization, cannot decide compilation.

Our prototype compiles a proposed edge and manifest to a structural seal or a
replayable obstruction, and an independent verifier recomputes the finite
judgment.  Lean~4 checks its typed-family, durable-prefix, controller-cover,
and evidence-tightness components; the reference-monitor and installation
bridges are stated separately.  Across 38 focused tests (62 for the complete
artifact) and all 2,166 three-cell refinement instances, the two
implementations agree with the direct finite semantics.  Truthful edge
contracts and topology evidence, atomic installation, complete mediation, and
runtime refinement remain explicit obligations.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
This paper makes three contributions to one Agent history-admission property.
\nopagebreak[4]

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item A reference semantics for Agent history edges that gives choice and
  parallel Fork, replacing and live Restore, and select and join Merge
  correlated may/required futures.  It defines exactly when the strongest
  serialized commit monitor can preserve the required future at a fixed
  durable prefix
  (\cref{sec:model,sec:semantics}).

  \item A monitor-compilation characterization for controller topologies.  It
  connects required-future preservation and reference-monitor safety through
  the refinement chain above, gives constructive obstructions and
  fixed-manifest repair, and proves an exact evidence boundary for cell
  identity, controller identity, and contract-indexed co-liveness
  (\cref{sec:results}).

  \item A compiler, independent verifier, and mechanized finite components for
  the characterization.  Tests, exhaustive three-cell cross-checking, litmus
  manifests, and bounded projection measurements validate the finite decision
  while keeping adapter and installation assumptions outside the seal
  (\cref{sec:algorithm,sec:validation}).
\end{enumerate}
