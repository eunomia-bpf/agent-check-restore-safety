\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

An agent receives one approval to perform a consequential action.
Its runtime forks two candidate plans and promises to retain at most one.
Each branch reserves the approval for a different action.
This is safe: one unit of authority can back two promises whose futures are mutually exclusive.
Now suppose one branch calls a remote tool before selection, or an operator merges both branch results.
The former effect survives even if its branch loses; the latter makes two previously alternative promises contribute together.
The files may still look valid, and each tool transaction may be individually well formed, yet the execution can consume two uses of a one-use grant.

This is not merely a workspace rollback bug.
Traditional authorization decides an action in one current state.
State-continuity systems decide whether one recovered continuation extends an accepted history.
Some emerging tool-using runtimes instead make \emph{history transformation} routine: they create intentional alternatives, keep old continuations live, delegate in parallel, issue effects during search, and merge artifacts from several trajectories.
Authorization must now decide whether an evolving \emph{family of promised futures} remains jointly solvent.

The state relevant to that decision spans different rollback domains.
Files, model context, and staged payloads may be reconstructed; branch eligibility, selections, claim ownership, and revocation epochs are lifecycle facts; messages, disclosures, remote writes, and uncertain sends are external history.
Rolling back all of them is usually impossible and often wrong: restore-live intentionally preserves the old continuation, and merge intentionally preserves information from several branches.
The question is which outcomes may still become durable together and which effects every future must already carry.

Linear capabilities, consumable credentials, and state-continuity systems provide essential ownership and anti-rollback foundations~\cite{bowers2007consumable,terauchi2006capability,parno2011memoir,matetic2017rote,brandenburger2017lcm}.
Transactions and recent agent runtimes provide staging, branch isolation, first-commit-wins selection, semantic transactions, and commit-time checks~\cite{wang2026fork,mohammadi2026atomix,chen2026cordon,santos2026committime}.
These mechanisms do not by themselves characterize an advance authorization promise whose validity depends on which branches may later coexist.
Nonduplicating conservative splitting prevents cross-branch oversubscription under our mediation and demand assumptions but rejects useful choose-one exploration; parent escrow is sufficient for pure candidates but gives branches no advance guarantee.
In our action model, topology affects quantitative admission once a runtime offers a conditional reservation or changes that claim's support.

We introduce \emph{authority continuity} for this boundary.
A tentative claim is funded only in durability configurations containing its branch.
After the effect escapes, the same claim becomes durable consumption funded in every remaining configuration.
Selection and replacing restore shrink support; live restore and merge can change correlation; escape expands support.
The memorable rule is: \emph{authority follows durable support, not copied state}.

This view gives a sharper answer to what a checkpoint is missing.
For a fresh reservation on one branch, the exact authorization summary is that branch's minimum headroom over all permitted futures containing it.
For reservations across branches, per-branch headroom is insufficient: exclusive and parallel states can expose identical individual headroom but accept different batches.
The minimal Reserve behavior is a correlated, downward-closed \emph{residual authorization profile}.
It is update-closed under further reservations, while its one-branch slices are not.
Thus the missing state is not generic “history” or a scalar balance, but the residual profile for the action class.
Boundary~I derives when that profile is rectangular---and therefore implementable by a Cartesian product of noncommunicating branch-local budgets---and when a product-only runtime must instead coordinate, reduce local budgets, or restrict topology.

Escape exposes a second boundary.
The exact safe pruning after promotion may no longer be expressible by a choice/parallel tree: promotion can permit every pair among three branches while forbidding their triple.
We complete the representation with frozen quantitative support guards.
One compact row expresses the exact promotion repair; freezing prevents later withdrawal from silently reopening discarded futures.
The specified fork and restore rules transport singleton lineage by OR; a general merge is covered only with an explicit projection certificate or full target re-admission.

\paragraph{Contributions}
This paper makes four contributions.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item We give a closed labeled transition system for checkpoint, fork, selection, abort, replacing and live restore, merge, revocation, Prepare, crash-aware dispatch/retry, and settlement.
  Checked simulation, admission, and sealing certificates cover every authority-changing rule.
  We prove abstract preservation and a conditional concrete trace corollary under complete mediation, demand bounds, and sink retry assumptions.

  \item Boundary~I gives a necessary-and-sufficient test for compiling fixed-topology batch Reserve into a product of noncommunicating branch-local budget checkers without losing concurrent soundness or one-branch completeness.
  Headroom and an update-closed correlated residual provide the construction and expose the information omitted by snapshot-only recovery.

  \item Boundary~II proves that, with exact prefix repair and immediate cleanup, final-owner support exactly characterizes when every owner-group promotion order remains executable.
  Promotion is not closed in the base choice/parallel language; frozen vector-threshold guards give exact closure and expose when the runtime must validate an order, coordinate cleanup, or atomically seal a batch.

  \item A dependency-free executable model checks both boundaries, frozen guards, lineage transport, and finite ticket traces with crash, retry, and revocation.
  Documented Claude Code and Codex interfaces identify possible adapter points; they neither instantiate the abstract state nor establish complete mediation.
\end{enumerate}

The mathematics applies to adaptive non-LLM runtimes.
Agents make it urgent because fresh effect bindings, branch topology, external tools, human resume, and semantic merge are synthesized during execution rather than fixed before it.
