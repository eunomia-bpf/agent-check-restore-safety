\section{Mechanized and Executable Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

The artifact is a dependency-free Python executable model of finite vector grants, unique claim ownership, residuals, exact promotion, lineage transport, and selected crash-aware lifecycle rules.
It stores frontiers explicitly only for bounded exploration; this is not the proposed runtime representation.
It is not an implementation of lifecycle Reserve, Select/Abort, generic Merge certificates, or the complete LTS.
The artifact README gives one command sequence that runs 24 unit tests and reproduces the checked-in JSON byte for byte.

The explorer enumerates every nonempty downward-closed family on one to three branches, scalar claim weights in \(\{1,2\}\), durable demand in \(\{0,1\}\), and grants from zero through seven.
Its explicit owner-support well-formedness predicate requires every conditional claim owner to occur in at least one admitted frontier.
All headline source-state counts in \Cref{tab:explorer-results} apply this predicate: 1,312 states are well formed, of which 730 are safe sources.
A separate raw algebra scan checks the \(\ac\)/need transcription on 2,816 states including unsupported owners; the JSON labels that scan as a diagnostic and we exclude it from the table's evidence counts.
\Cref{tab:explorer-results} gives the v5 results; every check passed.

For derivative checks, successor components are in \([0,2]\) and the parent bound is \(2+\max_bx_b\le3\), so every queried \(x+y\) is represented rather than rejected by truncation.

Fixed checks exercise boundaries that aggregate counts can hide.
Choice and parallel over two branches both initially report \(H=(1,1)\), but the batch \((1,1)\) belongs only to the choice residual.
Across all 730 well-formed safe sources, the exact residual equals the rectangular product \(\Box(H)\) iff the all-headrooms corner satisfies every configuration-slack inequality; 296 states are rectangular and 434 are not.
For each source, enumeration runs through its maximum headroom component, which covers \(\Box(H)\); every residual batch is already componentwise bounded by \(H\), so this check has no fixed-box truncation gap.
The fixed choice instance satisfies this criterion, whereas the fixed parallel instance does not.
After reserving one unit on the first branch, successor headroom is respectively \((0,1)\) and \((0,0)\), witnessing that \(H\) is not update-closed.
For the two-state knowledge set with common structural premises, all nine batches in \([0,2]^2\) agree with membership in the intersection of the two residual profiles.
Promotion in \(b\Box(x\parallel y\parallel z)\) produces exactly the seven subsets of \(\{x,y,z\}\) of size at most two; exhaustive enumeration of all eight three-vertex conflict graphs finds no representation, while the threshold-two guard represents it exactly.
Withdrawing \(x\)'s claim makes a recomputed current-\(Q\) guard admit the triple, whereas the frozen guard continues to exclude it.
After live restore, OR-lineage transport charges the retained old/restored pair two units; naively copying the term charges four and rejects it.
In the positive final-owner-support instance, the exact guarded atomic batch and both serial orders produce the same state and denotational support; the check deliberately does not require syntactically equal guards.
In the absent-support instance, cleanup after the \(s\)-first step terminalizes the pending \(t\) claim and disables that order, whereas the \(t\)-first order remains enabled and reaches the atomic batch's cleaned state and denotational support.
Separately, the witnessed condition-on-owner shortcut tombstones the next owner, terminalizes its claim, and disables the second escape.
Finally, the snapshot-local litmus constructs a replace pre-state with empty \(Q\) and a live-restore pre-state with one unit on the old branch, applies the same fresh \textsc{Reserve}(restored,1) proposal to both, and obtains safe/unsafe successors with needs one/two respectively.
The plain-escape witness gives load two under a one-unit grant.
The lifecycle graph enumerates eight reachable ticket-phase/grant-epoch states and 22 valid crash, Dispatch, Retry, Settle, and Revoke edges.
It checks that Dispatch-before-Prepare, tentative claims on tombstoned branch epochs, and reuse of a tombstoned name as a fresh restore descendant are rejected; crash preserves prepared work and maps inflight to uncertain, retry reuses \((e,c)\) without changing authority accounting, cancellation retains the durable claim, and a sealed ticket may dispatch after revocation.
Certified replace/live restore and choice/parallel fork check 26 target frontiers while preserving one receipt and prepared/uncertain tickets.

These checks validate the executable definitions, the threshold representation, and the reported finite counterexamples.
They do not constitute a proof for unbounded states, a performance evaluation, complete-mediation evidence, or a concrete-runtime refinement.

\paragraph{Lean mechanization}
A separate Lean 4.30.0 development over arbitrary finite carrier types defines one authoritative lifecycle step relation containing Reserve, selection/restriction, Prepare, ticket recovery, revocation, four canonical topology operations, and two explicit Merge modes.
Choice/parallel Fork and replacing/live Restore compute exact target contracts and branch epochs.
Their checker inspects only freshness and source-local transfer facts; target lifecycle well-formedness, active-support exactness, and authority continuity are derived using a checked coordinatewise fiber-conservation theorem.
Simulation Merge checks atomized target structure, a monotone zero-preserving projection, and load simulation; direct Merge instead performs explicit target admission.
Trace induction additionally preserves terminal IDs, epochs, and stable effect bindings.
A clean build completed 755 jobs in 10.02 seconds; source and dependency audits found no placeholder or project axiom, and fresh kernel replay succeeded.
The mechanization limits fragments to identifiers preallocated in the finite type and unissued at the source; it does not cover Boundaries I--II, issuer approval, semantic effect binding, complete mediation, or sink truthfulness.

\paragraph{Concrete adapter instantiation}
Codex 0.146.0 executes native \texttt{thread/fork}, \texttt{turn/start}, and client-owned \texttt{item/tool/call}; a loopback fixture selects the tool.
While its callback is pending, we kill a durable-controller worker before dispatch, after isolated-sink success, or after settlement.
The adapter, not Codex, assigns topology and Restore/Merge meaning.

Across its 20 histories, P3 matches 89/89 frozen decisions and replays 20/20 delta/hash chains, with no unsafe accept or duplicate aggregate effect.
Across all four policies, all 44 reached effects match raw callbacks and finish once; all 33 crashes recover in distinct processes.
Raw records match all 187 native forks (80 setup roots; 107 lifecycle materializations), while logical ancestry remains adapter metadata.
The local null control unsafely accepts 11/89 observed requests; split-all and parent escrow have no unsafe accept but safely reject 4/87 and 9/71, truncating two and 18 successors.
O0 and O1 each retain three mixed-label fibers---topology, grant lineage, and effect phase---whereas O2 retains none.
C02/C04 execute the paired accept/reject attempt probe, and the controller and independent replay decoder both check C19's projection and injective claim map.

This fixed witness is not Codex refinement: all histories fork one seed; ancestry and Restore/Merge are adapter state; topology-activation crashes are untested.
Frontend death, built-in tools, direct sink access, dishonest sinks, semantic binding, power loss, and generic Merge certificates remain out of scope.
The run instantiates one conditional boundary, not complete mediation or the general concrete simulation theorem.
