\section{Executable Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

The main result is a pen-and-paper theorem; the artifact is a small bounded
model intended to expose definition and cut mistakes, not replace the proof.
It is dependency free and exposes two API phases: a witness producer that
derives a cut and computes \(W\), and installation/use functions that
recompute trusted state rather than accept semantic fields from the operation.
The phases share one implementation and are not independent verification.

\subsection{What the checker executes}

The executable representation contains occurrences, finite pomsets, contract
choice/parallel/sequence, a trusted history state, and exactly the six
operation datatypes in \cref{tab:history-rules}.  Reflection tests verify that
these operation objects have no contract, outcomes, plan, sources, anchors,
receipts, or receipt-frontier fields.  The derivation returns
\((\mathcal C,\Src,\mathsf{Tgt},H')\); the admission routine enumerates every
topological sort, performs length-preserving fresh/alias resolution, tests
the fresh projection against a prefix policy, and constructs \(W\) as exactly
the set of safe-completion prefixes for the represented fixtures.  Contract
construction also rejects a complete trace that strictly prefixes another.
The
artifact computes \(W\), but does not execute the marked residual controller
of \cref{eq:canonical-program}; that implementation theorem remains on paper.
It also quotients structurally identical pomsets, whereas the theory retains
indexed outcome identities after residualization.  Its policy alphabet is the
cell IDs themselves rather than a separate \(\ell(d)\), and protected use
appends the ordered occurrence ID without separately advancing \(\nu\);
general label projection and the full Use transition remain paper semantics.

The model deliberately fails closed above 128 outcomes, eight occurrences per
outcome, 50,000 linearizations per pomset, or 100,000 distinct safe resolved
traces.  These caps make the search auditable and prevent an accidental
scalability claim.  They are not bounds in \cref{thm:exact-history}.

\subsection{Litmus suite}

Seventeen focused tests cover:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
 \item both Fork modes, both Restore modes, and both Merge modes;
 \item same-cell copies before and after an existing receipt, retaining every
       logical occurrence while emitting at most one fresh cell;
 \item rejection of a silent early completion that is also a prefix of a
       longer complete trace;
 \item universal outcome coverage and exact prefix construction;
 \item choice-selection consistency and derivation of every member owner for
       both Merge forms;
 \item rejection when a disabled or forged occurrence tries to borrow an
       enabled transition through the same cell;
 \item alias progress resolving a choice without changing receipts; and
 \item both linearizations of fresh--install and alias--install races:
       progress first makes the seal stale, while install first closes the old
       generation.
\end{itemize}

All 17 focused tests pass; the repository's full artifact suite passes 79
tests.  The anonymous archive is rebuilt, extracted into a clean directory,
and reruns the focused suite.  These tests found the distinction between the
receipt frontier and ordered logical-occurrence-ID frontier; the richer
\((x,\mathsf{fresh/alias},d)\) records in the theorem are not mechanized.

\subsection{Interpretation and falsifiability}

The checker directly executes a bounded subset of the finite definitions, so
agreement is sanity evidence rather than independent verification or
mechanization.  The marked residual program, full cut certificate, indexed
residual outcomes, global weak bisimulation, least owner closure for nested
histories and policy-connected scopes, and unbounded theorem remain paper
proofs.  The older Lean
development in the repository covers only a permutation-invariant endpoint
model and is not evidence for \cref{thm:exact-history}.

The executable subset is falsified by a represented fixture in which
derivation omits an affected owner, \(\Resolve\) loses or duplicates an
occurrence, its computed \(W\) differs from the safe-completion prefixes, or
a stale cut succeeds after fresh or alias progress.  The theorem is
additionally falsified if the marked program differs from
\((W,B^\star)\), an installed cut leaves an affected old use enabled, or the
two LTSs have different observable traces.  These latter statements are
explicit targets for future mechanization and model checking, not claims
about the present executable.
