\label{sec:validation}

trace reformulation and checks only its permutation-invariant endpoint
corollary.  We make this boundary explicit because neither successful tests

\subsection{Mechanized commutative corollary}

If both $\mathsf{Plant}_R$ and $\mathcal A$ are closed under permutations of
distinct events, a trace is characterized by its endpoint set.  In that
profile, the old downward-closed family semantics is a quotient of
\cref{eq:reference-language}.  The Lean development checks typed
choice/tensor/replacement constructors, durable-prefix filtering, monotone
fresh receipts, semantic-cell aliases, controller-cover decomposition, and
fixed-prefix structural transport for finite carriers.  Its audit uses no
project axioms; the remaining dependencies are Lean's standard
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Quot.sound}, and
\texttt{Classical.choice}.

This evidence catches useful mistakes in the unordered submodel, but it does
not prove sequence, shuffle, residual automata, or the installation LTS.
Accordingly, the abstract and contributions do not call the causal theorem
mechanized.

\subsection{Executable legacy checks}

The dependency-free Python artifact has independent symbolic and bit-mask
implementations of the endpoint predicate.  Thirty-eight of its 62 tests
exercise the six history labels, receipt growth, alias/clone distinctions,
controller correlations, malformed manifests, tampering, and deterministic
certificates.  A direct oracle agrees on all 2,166 candidate/required/physical
family instances over three cells.  These results validate the commutative
corollary and preserve regression coverage for the earlier compiler.

They are not reported as evaluation of $\mathsf{Can}(R)$.  In particular, the
artifact does not generate the causal residual program, bind it through a
version/frontier CAS, recover a partially installed epoch, or prove complete
mediation in Codex or Claude.  Implementing those bridges is engineering work
needed before a systems claim; the security statement here is the abstract
kernel theorem under the TCB in \cref{sec:model}.

\subsection{Falsifiable scope}

The main result would be falsified by a well-formed request for which the
constructed epoch admits a word outside $F_R$, omits a word in $F_R$, accepts
a stale-source fresh \Prepare{} after a successful install, or installs after
the compared receipt trace has changed.  It would also be falsified by a
monitor-sound, outcome-preserving epoch for a request rejected by
\cref{eq:causal-admission}.  These are finite trace/LTS statements suitable
for future mechanization and bounded model checking; no empirical workload
prevalence assumption is needed for the theorem.
