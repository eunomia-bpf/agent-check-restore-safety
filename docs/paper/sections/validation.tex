\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:validation}

We ask whether the theorems are machine-checked (VQ1), two implementations
agree with the finite semantics (VQ2), litmus cases expose lower-order blind
spots (VQ3), and public traces carry the required adapter evidence (VQ4).
No observational trace is labeled safe or unsafe, and no trace statistic is
used to establish either inclusion involving $\mathsf{Actual}$.

\subsection{VQ1: Mechanized theorem spine}

Lean~4 links the three family constructors underlying the six runtime operation
labels, greatest fixed-prefix admission, overlapping controller-cover
readiness and obstructions, the independent-identity erasure proposition,
exact projection, and runtime-refinement transport.  It also checks the
fixed-$E,L$ pruning corollary and recovers the older functional partition as a
canonical special case.  The artifact additionally mechanizes an append-only
commitment substrate, but that supporting result is outside the paper's
theorem spine.

For symbolic theorems, the audit rejects placeholders, project axioms, and
dependencies beyond
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Quot.sound}, and \texttt{Classical.choice}.  A
two-controller $U(2,3)$ realization checks correlation-cut extraction; a
separate arbitrary-$k$ singleton-controller construction checks both the
contract-indexed upper bound and its $U(k,k{+}1)$ lower bound
(\cref{thm:observation-arity}).

This proves a finite manifest-relative theorem.
It does not prove that a Claude or Codex adapter reports complete contracts,
that its declared controller product contains every actual behavior, or that
an external effect executes exactly once.

\subsection{VQ2: Compiler--verifier cross-check}

The dependency-free Python artifact contains two semantic implementations.
The compiler uses symbolic cell sets; the independent verifier reconstructs
the request with bit masks.  Both implement request schema~v3 and
result/verification schema~v4, derive $r$, enforce projection shape, and
distinguish full exact, exact-projection, and diagnostic sound-upper evidence.
Of 62 tests, 38 exercise history operators, evidence modes, readiness grades,
repairs, malformed inputs, caps, determinism, and tampering.  Every positive
seal sets $\mathsf{effect\_authorizes}=\mathsf{false}$.

An exhaustive oracle enumerates all 19 downward-closed families over three
cells.  Across every full-support candidate, every required subfamily, and
every physical family, it checks 2,166 refinement instances.
The compiler's five controller-realization grades agree with direct set differences in
every instance, and the independent verifier accepts every untampered result.
This enumeration checks the finite algorithm and its independent
reimplementation; it is not a proof that the JSON parser refines Lean.

A bounded CLI characterization exercises threshold families through 12 cells.
With six cells and one through four controllers, full-family pair expansion
rises from 64 to 70,592 iterations.  On a six-cell contract with $r=1$, exact
projection executes only 128--640 iterations for two through ten controllers;
analytically expanding the corresponding full families requires
4,288--16,846,784 and crosses the 200,000 cap at five controllers.  Both
projection and ordinary outputs are independently verified.  These are
deterministic loop counts and fail-closed checks, not latency or deployability
evidence.

\subsection{VQ3: Distinguishing litmus manifests}

Four in-memory cases exercise the witness taxonomy: a prefix-spent restored
cell yields \textsc{OutsideSupport}; one unsafe controller yields
\textsc{LocalOverpermission}; locally safe controllers whose union is the
forbidden $U(2,3)$ triple yield \textsc{CorrelationCut}, subtyped by declared
origins as \textsc{GateCut} or \textsc{GateClone}.  Folding true aliases to one
cell removes that correlation violation but still assumes atomic redemption;
multiple controllers remain safe for a full powerset future.  The unary
outside-support case reports required arity one; the $U(2,3)$ case reports
three, matching \cref{eq:required-coliveness-arity}.  Both implementations
propose the three controller pairs while leaving the original result
diagnostic; resubmitting that restriction seals.  Local overpermission instead
makes co-liveness-only repair infeasible.

\subsection{VQ4: Public trace-schema evidence}

TraceLab collects real Claude Code and Codex workload telemetry and reports
long autonomous loops and heterogeneous tool behavior
~\cite{zhu2026tracelab}.
We verified its v0.0.2 release of 8,058 sessions and 743,819 normalized tool
calls against the published artifact digest~\cite{tracelab2026release}.
Its normalized schema provides ordinary call/result correlation but not the
joint typed history edge, durable receipt prefix, cell-alias evidence,
controller identity and co-liveness, or a correlated leaf future contract.
The missing fields make history-admission prevalence unidentifiable; they do
not imply that failures are rare.
