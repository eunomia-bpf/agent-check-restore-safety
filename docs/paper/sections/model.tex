\section{Typed History-Admission Model}
\label{sec:model}

The security state at a history-transformation boundary has a fixed durable
past and a correlated future.  We model both explicitly, before choosing a
controller implementation.  All carrier types and families below are finite;
this makes one admission decision executable, but does not bound the lifetime
of a runtime that installs successive versions.

\subsection{Semantic redemption cells and lineage}

Let $\mathcal H$ be target-handle occurrences, $\mathcal D$ semantic
redemption cells, and $\mathcal U$ qualified authority atoms.  A trusted
normalization map
\[
  q_{\mathrm{cell}}:\mathcal H\to\mathcal D
\]
identifies handles that meet the same atomic spent-state linearization point.
Thus two aliases mapped to one cell denote one possible fresh commitment, not
two independently redeemable copies.  Conversely, independent arm
occurrences must remain distinct cells even if their payloads or resource names
match.  Any epoch, scope, issuer, nonce, or unit index needed to distinguish
such occurrences is part of the adapter-supplied identity evidence; the model
does not infer aliases from copied text.

Each target cell has source-authority lineage
\[
  \ell:\mathcal D\to\mathcal U.
\]
Two distinct cells may share one lineage atom when they are mutually exclusive.
They may not both occur in one admitted configuration: that would represent two
independent redemption points for one indivisible source atom.  This distinction
between handle aliasing, cell identity, and authority lineage is intentional.
A retry already resolved by an existing durable receipt is not a prospective
cell in $\mathcal D$; cells represent only possible fresh commitments.

\subsection{Correlated futures and the durable past}

A future family $F\subseteq 2^{\mathcal D}$ records which sets of fresh
commitments may become durable together.  It is nonempty, contains
$\varnothing$, and is downward closed:
\[
  X\in F\land Y\subseteq X \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Y\in F.
\]
The family retains correlation that per-cell flags erase.  For example,
$\{\varnothing,\{d_1\},\{d_2\}\}$ admits either alternative without admitting
$\{d_1,d_2\}$.

Let $\mathcal A\subseteq 2^{\mathcal U}$ be the downward-closed source
authority family.  Let $D\subseteq\mathcal U$ be the atoms named by the trusted
durable receipt frontier.  The ledger supplying $D$ is append-only, its receipt
bindings are immutable and injective by atom, and $D\in\mathcal A$.  An
admission decision holds $D$ fixed: receipt growth creates a new frontier and
requires a new decision rather than structural reuse of the old result.

For $X\subseteq\mathcal D$, write $\ell[X]$ for its lineage image.  Compatibility
with the durable past requires three independent facts:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prefix-ok}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathsf{PrefixOK}(X) \iff {}&
    \mathsf{InjOn}(\ell,X) \\
  &{}\land D\cap\ell[X]=\varnothing \\
  &{}\land D\cup\ell[X]\in\mathcal A.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first clause forbids a within-future lineage collision, the second forbids
replaying an atom already committed in the durable past, and the third preserves
the source authority contract.  These clauses characterize transport for every
finite additive source policy, but the family formulation also retains
non-pairwise correlation directly.

\subsection{Typed history operations}

A leaf contract is a pair $(M,R)$ of downward-closed families over normalized
target cells, where $R\subseteq M$.  The may-family $M$ lists possible behavior;
the required family $R$ lists behavior that an implementation may not silently
remove.  The adapter supplies complete leaf contracts; the compiler checks their
shape but does not infer them from a transcript or filesystem diff.

A typed operation $op$ is one of
\[
\begin{gathered}
  \mathsf{forkChoice},\ \mathsf{forkParallel},\\
  \mathsf{restoreReplace},\ \mathsf{restoreLive},\\
  \mathsf{mergeSelect},\ \mathsf{mergeJoin}.
\end{gathered}
\]
It derives two families
\[
  \mathsf{Candidate}_{op},\qquad \mathsf{Required}_{op}
  \subseteq 2^{\mathcal D}.
\]
Choice Fork and select Merge take the union of their arm families.  Parallel
Fork, live Restore, and join Merge take their tensor: all unions
$X_1\cup X_2$ with one configuration selected from each arm.  Replacing Restore
uses the checkpoint contract alone.  The construction is applied componentwise
to may and required families.  For a valid operation, both outputs are
downward closed and
\[
  \mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}.
\]
Detailed transition rules appear in the next section; here the important point
is that the future family is generated by the typed operation rather than
chosen as a premise that already satisfies the desired conclusion.

\subsection{Greatest fixed-candidate admission}

For fixed $\mathcal A$, $D$, $\ell$, and the generated candidate, define
\begin{definition}[Admitted future]
\label{def:admitted-future}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:admitted-future}
  \mathsf{Admitted}_{op}
  = \{X\in\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}\mid\mathsf{PrefixOK}(X)\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
This filter is downward closed and is the greatest prefix-compatible subfamily
of this fixed candidate.  The scope is essential: it assumes complete
observation of the supplied candidate and controllable pruning.  It neither
changes lineage, reissues atoms, expands the source contract, nor proves that
the candidate or required family describes a real runtime.

The semantic admission axis compares the typed output with this filter.
Structural \emph{inheritance} additionally requires a version-monotone parent
map into an old admitted envelope, injective on each target configuration, with
a commuting lineage map and unchanged durable prefix.  If inheritance is
unavailable, full readmission succeeds when
$\mathsf{Candidate}_{op}=\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$.  If instead
\[
  \mathsf{Required}_{op}\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}
  \subsetneq\mathsf{Candidate}_{op},
\]
the operation needs a pruning or coordination mechanism.  If
$\mathsf{Required}_{op}\nsubseteq\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$, no pruning-only repair
can preserve the declared requirement.  These are respectively the semantic
meanings of \textsc{Inherit}, \textsc{ReadmitOK}, \textsc{NeedsMechanism}, and
\textsc{Reject}; none is yet a claim about controller deployment.

\subsection{Independent controller realization}

Redemption-cell identity does not determine which executions share the state
that enforces a correlated family.  Let $\mathcal J$ be gate-use occurrences,
$\mathcal G$ authoritative controller instances, and
\[
  q_{\mathrm{ctrl}}:\mathcal J\to\mathcal G
\]
be a second, independent normalization.  Two handles may correctly share one
redemption cell while their gate uses map to different controllers.  Atomic
redemption then protects that cell, but the controllers may still recombine
different locally allowed cells into a globally forbidden configuration.

The manifest exposes this realization through:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item an access relation $E\subseteq\mathcal D\times\mathcal G$, because one
  cell may be reachable through zero, one, or several controllers;
  \item a local family $L_g\subseteq 2^{\mathcal D}$ for each controller $g$;
  and
  \item a co-liveness family $\Gamma\subseteq 2^{\mathcal G}$ describing which
  controller sets may run together.
\end{itemize}
We do not assume that every entry of $L_g$ is admitted: a forbidden local
entry is precisely a local-overpermission witness.  Downward closure of the
local families is an additional premise for shrinking a physical failure to a
minimal obstruction.
A cover plan chooses $G_0\in\Gamma$ and, for every $g\in G_0$, a piece
$X_g\in L_g$ whose cells are accessible through $g$.  Its realized
configuration is $\bigcup_{g\in G_0}X_g$.  The manifest's raw physical family
is therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:raw-physical}
\begin{split}
  \mathsf{RawPhysical}(E,L,\Gamma) = {}
  &\{C\subseteq\mathcal D\mid\\[-2pt]
  &\quad \exists G_0\in\Gamma,\ \exists (X_g)_{g\in G_0},\\[-2pt]
  &\quad C=\textstyle\bigcup_{g\in G_0}X_g,\\[-2pt]
  &\quad \forall g\in G_0:\ X_g\in L_g,\\[-2pt]
  &\quad \forall g\in G_0,\ \forall d\in X_g:\ E(d,g)\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The product is deliberately \emph{raw}: it ranges over every cell configuration
and does not discard behavior outside
$\operatorname{supp}(\mathsf{Admitted}_{op})$ before checking safety.

Let $\mathsf{Actual}\subseteq2^{\mathcal D}$ denote the configurations that the
installed runtime can in fact make durable together.  The compiler does not
observe or compute this family.  It checks the manifest-relative structural
predicate
\begin{definition}[Structural deployment readiness]
\label{def:deployment-ready}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deployment-ready}
  \mathsf{Required}_{op}
  \subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}
  \subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
The left inclusion is required-behavior coverage; the right inclusion excludes
physical over-permission.  Equality
$\mathsf{RawPhysical}=\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}$ is exact manifest fidelity, while
a strict right inclusion is a safe restriction that may omit only optional
behavior.

Actual deployment additionally owes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:actual-refinement}
  \mathsf{Required}_{op}
  \subseteq\mathsf{Actual}
  \subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}
  \subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}_{op}.
\end{equation}
The first two inclusions in \cref{eq:actual-refinement} are adapter/runtime
refinement obligations: required behavior must be reachable, and the declared
raw product must conservatively cover all actual behavior.  Together with
\cref{eq:deployment-ready}, they place every actual configuration inside the
prefix-safe family.  Structural readiness alone does not establish either
runtime inclusion.

\subsection{Assumptions and two decision axes}

Semantic admission and controller deployment are independent axes.  The first
derives the greatest continuation allowed by the source authority family,
durable prefix, lineage, and typed operation.  The second asks whether declared
controllers realize at least the required continuation without adding a
forbidden union.  A semantically admissible candidate can still have an unsafe
controller cover, and a locally linear redeemer does not by itself preserve
higher-order correlation.

The formal result is conditional on truthful, complete leaf contracts;
normalized cell identities or explicit aliases; qualified lineage atoms; an
authentic and complete append-only receipt frontier; and truthful controller
access, local-family, and co-liveness evidence.  Runtime use further assumes
complete mediation, atomic redemption at each semantic cell, controller
installation and freshness, and the two $\mathsf{Actual}$ refinements above.
The compiler and verifier check the finite structural model, not the truth of
these external facts.

Accordingly, an accepted structural seal is not an effect-authorization
credential.  The model does not infer natural-language intent, prove full lease
validity, establish a parser-to-model refinement, guarantee external
exactly-once execution, or claim that a concrete agent runtime realizes
$\mathsf{RawPhysical}$.  Those obligations remain outside typed history
admission.
