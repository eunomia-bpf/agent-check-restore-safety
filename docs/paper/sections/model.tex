\section{Agent History-Admission Model}
\label{sec:model}

An Agent history edge changes which continuations may commit together.  The
admission question is whether the controller epoch installed after that edge
can run without consulting one serialized monitor while producing only traces
that the monitor would have accepted and retaining every outcome that the edge
declares required.  We model one edge at a time.  All carriers and families are
finite, so the decision is executable; a runtime may install unboundedly many
successive finite epochs.

\subsection{Redemption cells, lineage, and durable receipts}

Let $\mathcal H$ be target-handle occurrences, $\mathcal D$ semantic
redemption cells, and $\mathcal U$ qualified authority atoms.  A trusted
normalization
\[
  q_{\mathrm{cell}}:\mathcal H\to\mathcal D
\]
maps aliases to their shared atomic spent-state linearization point.  Two
handles mapped to one cell denote one possible fresh commitment.  Independent
arm occurrences remain distinct cells even when their payload text matches.
The adapter must therefore supply any epoch, scope, issuer, nonce, or unit
index needed to distinguish identities; the model does not infer aliasing from
a copied workspace.

Each prospective cell has source-authority lineage
\[
  \ell:\mathcal D\to\mathcal U.
\]
Distinct cells may share an atom only when no admitted outcome contains both:
one indivisible source atom cannot fund two fresh commitments.  A retry already
resolved by a durable receipt is not a prospective cell; successful replays
stutter and do not appear as fresh commits.

Source authority is a nonempty downward-closed family
$\mathcal A\subseteq2^{\mathcal U}$.  The authenticated receipt frontier names
a set $D\subseteq\mathcal U$ with $D\in\mathcal A$.  Receipt bindings are
immutable and injective by authority atom.  One admission decision fixes $D$;
an older local snapshot cannot remove an atom from it.

For a cell set $X$, write $\ell[X]$ for its lineage image.  Exactly three
conditions make $X$ compatible with the fixed durable past:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prefix-ok}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathsf{PrefixOK}_{D}(X) \iff {}&
    \mathsf{InjOn}(\ell,X)\\
  &{}\land D\cap\ell[X]=\varnothing\\
  &{}\land D\cup\ell[X]\in\mathcal A.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
They respectively rule out two fresh cells spending one atom, reuse of an atom
already named by a receipt, and a future outside the source contract.
Downward closure of $\mathcal A$ makes $\mathsf{PrefixOK}_{D}$ downward closed.

\subsection{Typed before/after Agent edges}

A continuation contract $K=(M_K,R_K)$ contains a downward-closed
\emph{may} family and a downward-closed \emph{required} family over cells, with
$\varnothing\in R_K\subseteq M_K$.  May describes the outcomes the
continuation can expose; required describes outcomes that installing the edge
may not silently remove.  The adapter supplies complete leaf contracts.
Admission checks their finite shape but does not infer them from model text,
tool arguments, or a filesystem diff.

A typed edge $e$ records a before node and one of six before/after relations:
\[
\begin{gathered}
  \mathsf{ForkChoice},\quad \mathsf{ForkParallel},\\
  \mathsf{RestoreReplace},\quad \mathsf{RestoreLive},\\
  \mathsf{MergeSelect},\quad \mathsf{MergeJoin}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Choice/select leave alternatives; parallel/live/join leave both continuations
capable of durable commitment; replace retires the current continuation in
favor of the checkpoint continuation.  The type and its target contracts
derive two endpoint families
\[
  \mathsf{Cand}_{e},\qquad \mathsf{Req}_{e}\subseteq2^{\mathcal D}.
\]
The caller does not submit either family directly.
\Cref{sec:semantics} gives the six derivations and proves
$\mathsf{Req}_{e}\subseteq\mathsf{Cand}_{e}$.

At the fixed receipt frontier, the greatest candidate subfamily safe for the
serialized monitor is:
\begin{definition}[Admitted endpoint family]
\label{def:admitted-future}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:admitted-future}
  \mathsf{Adm}_{e}(D)
  =\{X\in\mathsf{Cand}_{e}\mid\mathsf{PrefixOK}_{D}(X)\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
The word ``greatest'' is scoped to the fixed edge, candidate, authority family,
frontier, and lineage map.  Admission may prune candidate behavior; it does
not reissue atoms, change lineage, or expand the source contract.

\subsection{Fresh-commit traces and the exact monitor}

A trace $\sigma=d_1\cdots d_n$ lists successful \emph{fresh} commitments and
contains no repeated cell.  Let
$\operatorname{cells}(\sigma)=\{d_1,\ldots,d_n\}$ and, for a trace language
$\mathcal T$, let
\[
  \mathsf{End}(\mathcal T)
    =\{\operatorname{cells}(\sigma)\mid\sigma\in\mathcal T\}.
\]
Replays, retries, and crashes are stutters and are erased from this trace.
Authorization and the edge contract are order-insensitive here; order-sensitive
application policies must first be represented by qualified cells or a richer
leaf contract.

The strongest baseline is a serialized atomic monitor that knows the entire
typed candidate, authority family, frontier, and lineage.  From the
within-epoch committed set $X$, it has the single successful transition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:global-monitor-step}
\frac{
  d\notin X\quad
  X\cup\{d\}\in\mathsf{Cand}_{e}\quad
  \mathsf{PrefixOK}_{D}(X\cup\{d\})
}{
  X\xrightarrow{\mathsf{commit}(d)}X\cup\{d\}
}.
\end{equation}
Call this transition system $\mathsf{GM}_{e}(D)$ and its fresh-commit language
$\mathsf{Traces}(\mathsf{GM}_{e}(D))$.  The transition checks the accumulated
durable prefix, not each effect in isolation.  Because both predicates are
downward closed, every prefix of an accepted trace is accepted and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:global-monitor-endpoints}
  \mathsf{End}\bigl(\mathsf{Traces}(\mathsf{GM}_{e}(D))\bigr)
  =\mathsf{Adm}_{e}(D).
\end{equation}
Any serialized monitor that stays within the same candidate and never violates
\cref{eq:prefix-ok} accepts a sublanguage of
$\mathsf{Traces}(\mathsf{GM}_{e}(D))$.  The baseline is therefore maximally
permissive for these fixed inputs.

\subsection{Controller-epoch physical language}

The monitor language is a specification, not the behavior of detached
controllers.  Let $\mathcal J$ be gate-use occurrences, $\mathcal G$
authoritative controller instances, and
\[
  q_{\mathrm{ctrl}}:\mathcal J\to\mathcal G
\]
an independent normalization.  Two uses may share one redemption cell yet
reach different controllers, or distinct cells may be coordinated by one
controller.  Atomic redemption resolves the former identity question; it
does not make independently live controllers preserve a correlated policy.

A normalized controller manifest contains:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item access $E\subseteq\mathcal D\times\mathcal G$;
  \item a downward-closed local family $L_g\subseteq2^{\mathcal D}$ for each
        $g\in\mathcal G$; and
  \item a downward-closed co-liveness family
        $\Gamma\subseteq2^{\mathcal G}$.
\end{itemize}
A set $G_0\in\Gamma$ means that its controllers may remain authoritative in
the same co-durable epoch; it is not a scheduler snapshot.  A cover chooses
$G_0\in\Gamma$ and an accessible local piece $X_g\in L_g$ for each live
controller.  The manifest-induced endpoint family is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:raw-physical}
\begin{split}
  &\mathsf{RawPhysical}(E,L,\Gamma)\\[-2pt]
  &\quad=\{C\subseteq\mathcal D\mid
    \exists G_0\in\Gamma,\ \exists(X_g)_{g\in G_0},\\[-2pt]
  &\hspace{12mm}C=\textstyle\bigcup_{g\in G_0}X_g,\\[-2pt]
  &\hspace{12mm}\forall g\in G_0:\ X_g\in L_g,\\[-2pt]
  &\hspace{12mm}\forall g\in G_0,\ \forall d\in X_g:\ E(d,g)\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The product is deliberately raw: it is not intersected with the candidate or
admitted family before checking safety.  Access may overlap, so one cell can
be reachable through several controllers.

The corresponding physical trace language contains every serialization
consistent with this order-insensitive manifest:
\begin{definition}[Controller-epoch physical language]
\label{def:physical-language}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:physical-language}
\begin{split}
  &\mathsf{Phys}(E,L,\Gamma)\\[-2pt]
  &\quad=\{\sigma\mid \sigma\text{ is distinct},\
    \operatorname{cells}(\sigma)\in
    \mathsf{RawPhysical}(E,L,\Gamma)\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
Hence
$\mathsf{End}(\mathsf{Phys}(E,L,\Gamma))
=\mathsf{RawPhysical}(E,L,\Gamma)$.
A concrete controller may impose additional order constraints; its actual
language must refine, not extend, this declared language.

\subsection{History admissibility}

An epoch is safe only if its physical traces refine the exact monitor.  It is
faithful only if every required endpoint remains physically possible.  These
two clauses are the definition of Agent history admissibility:
\begin{definition}[Agent history admissibility]
\label{def:history-admissible}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:history-admissible}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathsf{HistoryAdmissible}_{D}(e;E,L,\Gamma)\\[-2pt]
&\quad\iff
  \mathsf{Phys}(E,L,\Gamma)
    \subseteq\mathsf{Traces}(\mathsf{GM}_{e}(D))\\[-2pt]
&\qquad{}\land
  \mathsf{Req}_{e}
    \subseteq\mathsf{End}(\mathsf{Phys}(E,L,\Gamma)).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
The first conjunct is \emph{monitor soundness}; the second is
\emph{required transparency}.  Transparency is a possibility guarantee, not
scheduler fairness or eventual task completion.  We separately name the
finite endpoint judgment checked by the compiler:
\begin{definition}[Structural deployment readiness]
\label{def:deployment-ready}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deployment-ready}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathsf{DeploymentReady}_{D}(e;E,L,\Gamma)\\[-2pt]
&\quad\iff
  \mathsf{Req}_{e}\subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}(E,L,\Gamma)\\[-2pt]
&\hspace{22mm}\subseteq\mathsf{Adm}_{e}(D).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
We call any family $\mathcal K$ with
$\mathsf{Req}_{e}\subseteq\mathcal K\subseteq\mathsf{Adm}_{e}(D)$ an
\emph{admissible edge cut}.  A successful manifest supplies the concrete cut
$\mathcal K=\mathsf{RawPhysical}(E,L,\Gamma)$.

Let $\mathsf{ActualTrace}$ be the fresh-commit language of an installed
runtime and
$\mathsf{Actual}=\mathsf{End}(\mathsf{ActualTrace})$.  The runtime owes
required reachability and conservative manifestation:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:actual-refinement}
  \mathsf{Req}_{e}\subseteq\mathsf{Actual},
  \qquad
  \mathsf{ActualTrace}\subseteq\mathsf{Phys}(E,L,\Gamma).
\end{equation}
Together with history admissibility these obligations yield both
$\mathsf{ActualTrace}\subseteq
\mathsf{Traces}(\mathsf{GM}_{e}(D))$ and
\[
  \mathsf{Req}_{e}\subseteq\mathsf{Actual}
  \subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}(E,L,\Gamma)
  \subseteq\mathsf{Adm}_{e}(D).
\]
The finite checker establishes history admissibility for supplied inputs.  It
does not establish truthful leaf contracts or identities, an authentic and
complete frontier, conservative controller evidence, complete mediation,
atomic cell redemption, or \cref{eq:actual-refinement}.  A seal is consequently
not permission for an external effect and proves neither exactly-once delivery
nor the safety of unmodeled Agent behavior.
