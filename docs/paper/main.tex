\title{Authority Continuity: Residual Contracts for Fork, Restore, and Merge}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}

\begin{abstract}
Some emerging tool-using runtimes intentionally fork, restore, and merge executions while tools create effects outside copied state.
Authorization must therefore govern a family of possible durable futures, not one current state: mutually exclusive branches may share a bounded grant, but an early tool call or later merge can make previously alternative claims contribute together.
We introduce \emph{authority continuity}, which distinguishes branch-conditional commitments from durable consumption and requires every permitted durability configuration to remain funded.
We give a certificate-checked lifecycle semantics in which topology changes simulate a solvent source and every protected effect has exactly one durable matching claim before dispatch.
Our two main results are exact runtime boundaries.
First, in the fixed-topology batch-Reserve fragment, the residual admission region factorizes into noncommunicating branch-local budget capabilities iff its all-headrooms corner is safe; otherwise a product checker cannot be both sound for concurrent batches and complete for every one-branch batch.
Second, for a valid promotion batch with exact prefix guards, immediate cleanup, and no interleaved lifecycle change, every owner-group order succeeds iff every promoted owner remains supported after final repair.
The first boundary decides when reservation may be decentralized; the second decides when promotion is order-independent or instead needs a verified order, coordinated cleanup, or atomic sealing.
Residual derivatives, frozen threshold guards, and explicit lineage projections provide the supporting constructions.
A finite executable model exhaustively checks these constructions, including crash/retry/revocation ticket traces, on small states.
\end{abstract}
