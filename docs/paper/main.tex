\title{Agent History Admission: Exact Monitor Compilation for Fork, Restore,
and Merge}

\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Tool-using Agents increasingly fork alternatives, restore checkpoints, and
merge descendants while their effects and authorization receipts outlive any
restorable workspace.  A serialized atomic monitor can keep every commit safe,
but it gives no advance guarantee that the controller topology created by a
history edge can still realize the outcomes that edge promises.  It may accept
two individually legal effects and discover only at a required join that the
third is forbidden.  Calling the monitor at every effect also defeats detached
or low-latency controllers.

We introduce \emph{Agent history admission}: the property that a post-edge
controller epoch both refines this strongest atomic monitor and preserves every
declared required outcome that the monitor permits.  Six typed
Fork/Restore/Merge relations derive candidate and required futures from the
before/after Agent history; immutable receipts derive the monitor language;
and a controller manifest derives the physical language.  Our main theorem is
an exact characterization:
\[
  \mathsf{Required}\subseteq\mathsf{Physical}
  \subseteq\mathsf{Admitted},
\]
where $\mathsf{Admitted}$ is exactly the endpoint family of the atomic
monitor's trace language.
Thus history admission is neither an extra safety check nor a composition
slogan: it is the precise criterion for compiling serialized authorization
into the controllers created by an Agent history edge without silently
dropping required behavior.

The characterization is constructive and worst-case tight.  A
proof-producing compiler emits an independently checkable certificate, a
coordination/pruning proposal, or a concrete obstruction.  Exact verdicts
require, up to equivalent encodings, the edge mode, durable frontier,
redemption-cell identity, controller identity, and contract-indexed
co-liveness information; fixed-field collisions and an arbitrary-arity family
establish the lower bounds.  Lean~4 mechanizes the typed-family,
durable-prefix, controller-cover, and evidence-tightness components.  The
compiler/verifier passes 38 focused tests (62 artifact tests total), and an
independent oracle agrees on all 2,166 bounded refinement instances.
\end{abstract}
