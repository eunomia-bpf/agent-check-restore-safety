\title{Agent History Admission: Canonical Causal Epochs for Fork, Restore,
and Merge}

\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Tool-using Agents build execution graphs online by forking alternatives,
restoring checkpoints, and merging descendants while protected effects reach
services that no workspace restore can undo.
A history edge can therefore preserve every endpoint set yet violate the
promised causal outcome: payment may precede approval, a canceled branch may
commit after selection, or a join continuation may run before both inputs.
The structural cause is that a restorable Agent view, an append-only receipt
trace, and the controller epochs that remain able to prepare effects advance
at different history cuts.
Atomic monitors and classical supervisor synthesis enforce a fixed
prefix-closed language, but do not establish that an online history rewrite
closes old controllers at the same durable frontier.
We treat each bounded protected slice as a causal-language compilation
request whose output is a new, version-bound epoch.
We formalize raw Fork/Restore/Merge plans, normalize ledger-backed handle
aliases, derive the exact future language of the strongest atomic monitor,
and construct its canonical residual epoch.
Our Canonical Causal Epoch Theorem proves that admission is equivalent to
every promised partial-order outcome having a reference-language
linearization; the synthesized epoch then realizes the entire reference
language, and frontier-CAS installation preserves that equality across
Prepare/Install races and successive edges.
Residual automata are classical machinery; the theorem's new boundary is the
raw Agent-edge-to-installed-epoch refinement, including source-controller
fencing and stale-receipt failure.
We give self-contained paper proofs for the causal language, exact epoch, and
installer invariant; the existing Lean and Python artifacts are reported only
as evidence for the permutation-invariant endpoint corollary, not as
mechanization of the causal theorem or a production Agent integration.
\end{abstract}
