\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}

\begin{abstract}
Agent runtimes increasingly fork, checkpoint, restore, and merge executions while their tools mutate resources outside the copied state.
The resulting security problem is not ordinary workspace rollback: two byte-identical continuations can require different authorization depending on whether both may still commit, and a tool call from a losing branch remains observable after that branch is discarded.
We introduce \emph{authority continuity}, a lifecycle contract for bounded authority in history-transforming executions.
The central distinction is between a conditional commitment, which may be shared among mutually exclusive futures, and durable consumption, which every future must account for once an effect escapes.
We give concrete lifecycle semantics for exclusive and parallel fork, checkpoint, two restore modes, abort, escape, revocation, and merge, together with an abstraction into typed claims over co-durable frontiers.
We prove general abstraction soundness and state the additional exactness assumptions explicitly.
We then show that no checker using only restored snapshot state can be both sound and maximally permissive: replacing and live restore can reconstruct identical bytes while requiring opposite authorization decisions.
For effect escape, we derive the exact admission condition and the unique largest family of futures that can safely remain possible without new authority or cancellation of other claims; batched promotions yield the same weakest lifecycle restriction independent of order.
Classical cograph and independent-set results then yield efficient admission for structured lifecycle contracts and explain the cost of less structured representations.
We define an executable-validation and mechanization plan that targets the rule premises and minimal separating executions rather than model-dependent anecdotes.
\end{abstract}
