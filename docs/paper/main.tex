\title{History Admission: Safe Fork, Restore, and Merge under Durable Authority}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Agent runtimes may fork, restore, and merge histories without retracting
durable receipts or external effects.  These operations change which future
effects can become durable together: three fresh, individually authorized
effects can violate a contract that permits every pair but forbids their
union.  Per-effect authorization, linear capabilities, and rollback protection
do not decide this joint-outcome question.

We define \emph{history admission}: before installing a typed history edge, a
runtime must show
\[
  \mathsf{Required}\subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}
  \subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}.
\]
The left inclusion preserves the behavior promised by the edge; the right
prevents its independently live controllers from realizing a future excluded
by the authority contract and immutable receipt prefix.

Our central result gives a sufficient structural view and matching
component-wise worst-case lower bounds.  Two identity relations are
independently nonerasable:
copied handles must be normalized to redemption cells, and gate uses to
authoritative controllers.  After this normalization, the complete
co-liveness projection through a contract-derived arity $r$ determines
readiness; for every lower uniform bound, a well-formed cover gives identical
lower-order evidence but the opposite decision.  Thus the contract, rather
than a fixed pairwise rule, determines the necessary observation depth.

A certifying compiler derives the futures of Fork/Restore/Merge, emits an
admission seal or counterexample, and is checked by an independent verifier.
Lean~4 mechanizes the theorem spine.  The executables pass 62 tests, and an
independent oracle agrees on all 2,166 bounded refinement instances.  A public
trace-schema study confirms that ordinary Agent telemetry omits the required
identities and co-liveness; it is not used as a safety oracle.
\end{abstract}
