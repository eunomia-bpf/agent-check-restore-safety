\title{History Admission: Safe Fork, Restore, and Merge under Durable Authority}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}

\begin{abstract}
Agent runtimes fork, restore, and merge execution histories while protected
tool effects and receipts outlive any restorable workspace.  Such an edge can
leave each prospective effect fresh yet change which sets may become durable
together, so locally correct gates can compose a forbidden outcome.  Rollback,
per-effect checks, linear capabilities, and history monitors address parts of
this boundary but do not decide a topology-changing edge and its controller
realization.
We define \emph{history admission}.  Three family constructors instantiate six
Fork/Restore/Merge labels, derive candidate and required futures, and
filter them against an immutable receipt prefix.  Controller covers then
check $\mathsf{Required}\subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}
\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}$.  Exact admission distinguishes copied handles
from authoritative controller instances: a mechanized two-by-two construction
fixes all other raw fields and shows that erasing either identity normalization
conflates ready and nonready worlds.
We prove a contract-indexed exact evidence boundary: after normalization, the
complete co-liveness projection through arity $r$ determines readiness.
Fixed-manifest collisions make each raw identity relation nonerasable, while an
arbitrary-$k$ cover rules out uniformly lower projection arity in the worst case.
Greatest prefix admission, obstruction extraction, and pruning make the result
constructive.  A small compiler
accepts full exact evidence, its attested exact $r$-projection, or a diagnostic
upper bound, and an independent verifier recomputes the result.  Lean~4 checks
the theorem spine, 38 focused tests (62 total) cover the executables, and an
oracle agrees on all 2,166 three-cell refinement instances.  Claude/Codex
trajectories expose adapter telemetry gaps but provide neither safety labels
nor effect authorization.
\end{abstract}
