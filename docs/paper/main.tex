\title{History Admission: Structural Readiness for Fork, Restore, and Merge}

\author{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Agent runtimes may fork, restore, and merge histories without retracting
durable receipts or external effects.  These operations change which future
effects can become durable together: three fresh, individually authorized
effects can violate a contract that permits every pair but forbids their
union.  Per-effect authorization, linear capabilities, and rollback protection
do not decide this joint-outcome question.

Within a finite model of unordered, downward-closed future effects, we define
\emph{history admission}.  Before installing a typed history edge, its
submitted manifest must show
\[
  \mathsf{Required}\subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}
  \subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}.
\]
The left inclusion preserves the behavior promised by the edge; the right
prevents its independently live controllers from realizing a future excluded
by the authority contract and immutable receipt prefix.

Our central result characterizes an exact manifest-relative decision.  For
fixed normalized contracts, access, and local controller families, any two
downward-closed co-liveness families with the same projection through a
contract-derived arity $r$ have the same readiness Boolean.  For every
uniformly smaller arity, a well-formed pair has the same visible projection
and opposite decisions.  Before normalization, a separate two-by-two
construction shows that copied-handle identity and authoritative-controller
identity are independently nonerasable.  Thus the contract, rather than a
fixed pairwise rule, determines the worst-case observation depth.

An untrusted compiler derives Fork/Restore/Merge futures and emits a decision
or counterexample; an independent verifier recomputes it, and Lean~4
mechanizes the theorem spine.  The result is exact for the submitted finite
manifest.  Transferring it to an installed runtime additionally requires
authenticated evidence, complete mediation, and explicit runtime-refinement
obligations; the compiler does not prove those facts.
\end{abstract}
