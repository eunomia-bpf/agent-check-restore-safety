\title{When May an Agent Rewrite Its History?\\
An Exact Realization Theory for Fork, Restore, and Merge}

\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Agents can rewrite which past continuations remain capable of acting:
Fork creates several presents, Restore revives an earlier one, and Merge
selects or joins their futures.
The world does not follow these edits.
Approvals, payments, and other authority commitments remain in an append-only
ledger, while controllers from discarded histories may still be live.
We ask when an Agent history rewrite has any implementation that is
simultaneously policy safe, faithful to the rewritten history, able to
complete, and able to realize every outcome structurally promised by the
operation.

We introduce \emph{Agent history admission}.
A trusted history machine records an append-only Fork/Restore/Merge DAG,
checkpoints, live continuations, durably resolved logical occurrences, and their
controller owners.
Each typed edit derives---rather than accepts from the Agent---a finite family
of causal completions and the complete set of owners it affects.
A length-preserving resolution semantics distinguishes a fresh commitment
from a copied occurrence that aliases an existing receipt; the latter advances
logical history without advancing the ledger.

Our Exact Agent History Realization Theorem gives a necessary-and-sufficient
admission test.
When it succeeds, the prefixes of all policy-safe completions form the unique
greatest history-faithful, completion-nonblocking realization; when it fails,
one derived completion is a proof that no such realization exists.
We compile the admitted language into an occurrence-sensitive controller and
prove a global weak bisimulation between an ideal atomic history machine and a
durable runtime for arbitrary sequences of all six edits.
The implementation cut orders fresh commitments, receipt-silent aliases, and
history installation while deriving and fencing every stale source generation.
A small bounded checker exercises the six derivations, alias resolution, and
cut races.  The result makes history editing an exact authorization boundary
rather than a rollback heuristic.
\end{abstract}
