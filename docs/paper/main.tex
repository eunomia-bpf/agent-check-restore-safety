\title{History Admission: Safe Fork, Restore, and Merge under Durable Authority}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Agent runtimes increasingly fork, restore, and merge execution histories while
protected tool effects and receipts outlive any restorable workspace.
A history edge can leave every prospective effect individually fresh while
changing which sets of effects may become durable together, so locally correct
gates can compose a globally forbidden outcome.
The root cause is that security state includes a fixed durable past, a
correlated future, and a controller layout that can outlive or recombine the
restored workspace.
Rollback protocols, per-effect commit checks, linear capabilities, and
history-sensitive monitors each address part of this boundary but do not by
themselves decide a topology-changing edge and its controller realization.
We observe that a sound boundary must normalize copied handles into semantic
redemption cells independently from gate uses into authoritative controller
instances, then admit each topology-changing edge against both the durable
prefix and the product of controllers that may remain live.
We formalize six typed Fork/Restore/Merge operators that derive candidate and
required future families, compute the greatest prefix-compatible admitted
family, and check the manifest relation
$\mathsf{Required}\subseteq\mathsf{RawPhysical}
\subseteq\mathsf{Admitted}$.
We prove a four-way semantic classifier, compositional certificate inheritance,
an exact minimal-obstruction criterion, a contract-indexed sufficient
co-liveness observation arity, and an arbitrary-$k$ family proving that bound
worst-case tight.
A small compiler and independent verifier expand full declared $\Gamma$, retain
its coverage attestation, and report the required arity; Lean~4 checks the
theorem spine, 31 focused compiler/verifier tests (55 across the complete
artifact) cover the executable distinctions, and an exhaustive oracle agrees
on all 2,166 three-cell refinement instances.
Public and private Claude/Codex trajectories characterize the need for online
adapters and their telemetry gaps, but neither traces nor a verified
history-admission seal is treated as effect authorization.
\end{abstract}
